Construction & Contracting

/projects/:id

A project can now run a full construction / main-contracting workflow — drawings and RFIs, a programme with a real critical path, tenders that become subcontracts, valuations and retention, cost-value reconciliation, site diaries and snags. These aren't a separate product or a “construction mode” you switch on: they're sections of every project, surfaced by your role and finance access rather than by the project's category. This page is the map — what lives where, and where each deep guide is.

Where: everything is inside a project at /projects/:id. The project detail page organises its sections into seven stages on one row; the construction tools sit across the Commercial, Delivery, Client and Review stages.

The seven stages

A project's work is grouped into seven stages, left to right — you don't have to use them in order, they just gather the sections:

StageSections
OverviewThe project summary.
DesignRooms, Moodboards, Sheets.
SpecificationProducts, Plan (blueprint estimate), Purchases (made-to-order joinery + supplier sheet).
CommercialQuotes, Contracts, Billing, and Finance — the construction money chain (below).
DeliveryTasks & Programme, Site, Drawings.
ClientClient View, Requests (RFIs & submittals), Handover (warranties).
ReviewAI Assessment, Timeline.

A project KIND is a label, not a switch. You can tag a project with a category (Renovation, Trip, Warehouse, Real Estate, or your own) for filtering and reporting — but the category does not change which stages or tools appear. Every project has the same seven stages; what you see depends on whether you're the owner and whether you have finance access.

The construction toolset — where each lives

Commercial → Finance (the money chain)

The contracting money tools are stacked as cards inside the Finance section of the Commercial stage. They need the project owner plus finance access (the Finance module + finance.manage):

  • Bill of Quantities (the priced schedule — the accepted one is the contract sum) and Takeoff from drawings that feeds it.
  • Cost codes & job cost — a trade classification and what the job has cost by code, with margin.
  • Variations & CVR — scope changes both directions and a cost-value reconciliation.
  • Valuations / applications for payment and retention (held and released).
  • Tenders → subcontracts — send packages out, compare bids, award (an award is the subcontract/PO).
  • Labour (typed rate vs what payroll actually cost) and stock held for the job.

Delivery

  • Programme — a Gantt with a real critical path, float and a baseline (the Schedule view of Tasks).
  • Site — the site diary, inspection checklists and the snag/defect list (with a dictate-a-walk shortcut).
  • Drawings — a controlled register with revisions, plus markup → RFI and printed-schedule takeoff.

Client & Review

  • Requests — the RFI & submittal register (numbered, due-dated, de-duplicated, tied to a drawing).
  • AI Assessment — an AI health verdict of the project and a ranked plan.

What the assistant can do

The assistant can read the commercial picture from chat — the CVR, applications (claimed / certified / retention), variations, tender status and a bid analysis that ranks bids fairly (on a comparable total, flagging unpriced lines). These are read-only: raising a variation, certifying a valuation or awarding a tender stays a human decision. The on-site tools (drawings, RFIs, snags, programme) are UI-only.

Gating in one line: the Delivery and Client site tools are owner-gated in the free Projects module; the Commercial money chain additionally needs the Finance module and finance.manage; AI Assessment is its own Pro add-on. There is no separate “construction” add-on to buy.
