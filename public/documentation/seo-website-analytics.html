Website Analytics (Google Analytics 4)

/websites/:id?section=analytics ?section=analytics-pages ?section=analytics-geo ?section=analytics-tech ?section=analytics-commerce ?section=analytics-funnel ?section=analytics-retention

Search Console tells you how people find your site; Google Analytics tells you what they do once they arrive. Connect a GA4 property to a connected website and its dashboard gains an Audience group of seven sections: Overview, Pages, Geography, Devices & events, Products & ads, Funnel and Retention. Every table can be broken down further, live, one level at a time.

Where: open a website from Profile → Websites, then pick a section under Audience in the dashboard's rail. Each section has its own address (?section=…), so you can bookmark or share one.

Connecting a GA4 property

  1. Analytics uses the same Google connection as Search Console. If your Google connection predates Analytics, the Overview section offers Reconnect Google to include Analytics. Run it once.
  2. Click Choose an Analytics property and pick from the list (Pick a property…). Each entry shows the property name, its measurement ID (G-…) and the account. If the list is empty, the Google account has no access to a GA4 property. Give it at least Viewer access in Google Analytics.
  3. Picking a property pulls the last 28 days straight away. After that, it syncs every hour on its own; Sync pulls immediately.
  4. Picked the wrong one? The button now reads Change Analytics property.

The Overview card's subtitle names the property, its measurement ID and when it last synced. Compare that ID with the tag on your site to be sure you're reading the right property.

The seven sections

SectionWhat it shows
OverviewSix tiles: Sessions, Active users, New users, Engaged sessions, Conversions and Revenue, each with its change vs the previous period. Below them: Where the sessions came from (top channel groups), the Right now card, and a Sources table (source / medium, sessions, trend, new users, average time, conversions).
PagesPages: every page with a recorded view, busiest first. Landing pages: where sessions started. A page high on the landing list and low on the pages list is a doorway people leave from. Page names open the live page.
GeographyA world map, Where your visitors are (hover a country for its sessions), then Countries, Cities, Age and Gender tables. Age and gender are a Google sample and won't add up to your sessions.
Devices & eventsDevices, New vs returning, Events, Browsers, Operating systems, Languages and Hostnames. A hostname you don't recognise is a staging copy, or someone else reporting into your property.
Products & adsProducts (viewed, added, bought, revenue). This stays empty unless your store sends GA4 ecommerce events. Ad campaigns (sessions, cost, clicks, conversions, revenue, ROAS) needs a linked Google Ads account. Cost is never guessed from sessions.
FunnelWhere people leave (see below).
RetentionWeekly return rates (see below).

Working with the tables

  • Search, sort and window — every table has a search box, sortable columns and a 7d / 28d / 90d switch. 28 days is read from the stored sync. 7 and 90 days are asked of Google live, and the footer says so.
  • Break a row down — click any row (or its chevron) to open a side panel on that slice, asked of Google Analytics live. Choose Break down by… (by city, by source / medium, …) to go one level deeper. Use the breadcrumbs (e.g. Country: Greece › City: Athens) to jump back. The panel has its own search and its own date switch.

Right now

The Right now card on Overview shows active users in the last 30 minutes, with the top pages, countries and devices. It refreshes every minute and is read live, never stored.

Funnel — where people leave

What it is — the steps visitors take, the share who reach each one, and how many left at each step. How the steps are chosen — you don't define them. On each sync the platform checks which events your property actually reports and picks the journey that fits: a purchase journey (session start → item list → item → add to cart → checkout → purchase) or an enquiry journey (session start → page view → search results → lead). Only the steps your site really fires are drawn. If fewer than three fire, the Funnel reads Not available and names the events it looked for. A brochure site isn't shown a shop's funnel.

Retention — who comes back

Of the people who first arrived in a given week, how many came back in the weeks after. There are six weekly cohorts (Week of …), each with Week 0 to Week 5. Week 0 is always 100%. Hover a cell for X of Y. A week that hasn't happened yet shows —, never 0%.

When a figure is missing

Analytics follows the same rule as the rest of the website dashboard: a missing figure is never shown as 0. It reads None (Google answered and there's genuinely nothing), Unknown (the read failed, so the figure is unknown, not zero), Not measured (never collected yet) or Not available (your property doesn't report it, e.g. no ecommerce events or no linked Ads account). Each dimension is collected on its own, so one failure doesn't empty the other tables. A failed sync clears the old rows rather than leaving last week's numbers on screen as if they were current.

Worked example: on Pages, find a landing page with lots of sessions but little engagement → click it and Break down by source / medium to see which channel sends the visitors who leave → check the Funnel to see which step they drop at → improve that page, or write an article that answers what they came for (see the article writer).
