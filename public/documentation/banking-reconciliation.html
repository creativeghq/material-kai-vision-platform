Bank feed & reconciliation

/finance?tab=bank_feed finance.manage

Once Revolut is connected, every transaction on your account flows into a bank feed, and the platform tries to reconcile each one automatically: an incoming transfer is matched to the invoice it settles, an outgoing one to the supplier bill it pays. The matches it's confident about are applied for you; the uncertain ones wait in a review queue for one click. Crucially, settling always goes through the platform's single money path — so a reconciled payment books exactly like a manually recorded one, and never over-pays.

Where: Finance → Bank feed (in the section rail). The feed refreshes on a schedule and whenever you press Sync; Match now re-runs reconciliation, and Import statement… loads a statement file for a bank account that is not on Revolut.

Reading the feed

The feed is a table, 20 lines per page with numbered pages. Each line shows:

ColumnWhat it shows
DateWhen the transaction happened.
CounterpartyWho the money came from or went to, with the tail of their IBAN, and a CRM: link to the company it belongs to (matched by the IBAN on file, their Revolut account, or a unique legal name) — or Not in the CRM.
What It IsThe nature of the line — for example Customer Payment, Supplier Payment, Card Spend, Bank Fee, Between Own Accounts, Payout to Bank.
ReferenceThe payment reference.
AccountWhich of your accounts the line is on.
Transaction IDThe provider's transaction ID (copyable).
StatusThe match state (below).
AmountThe signed amount.
StatusMeaning
MatchedReconciled to an invoice, bill, expense document or planned payment.
Part MatchedOnly part of the amount has been matched so far.
Needs ReviewThe platform has one or more likely candidates but isn't certain — waiting for you to confirm. Outgoing card spend is listed here too.
UnmatchedNothing plausible was found (see the alert below).
IgnoredYou've marked it as not-to-reconcile (an internal transfer). Money you take out of the business is not ignored: add it as an expense in Profit split or Partner withdrawals, then match the line to it.

Filters across the top narrow the feed by provider, account, direction (Money In / Money Out), match status and date range; the search box takes a counterparty, reference or transaction ID. Lines from Viva and Stripe name the payer too, not only Revolut ones.

The details panel

Click a line (or View Details) to open its details: the counterparty's name, IBAN, BIC, country and account type, the fee and balance after, the legs of the transaction, and — per provider — the card, issuing bank, payer email, order code or customer note; for an imported statement, the raw statement line. The panel ends with Suggested Matches: the documents this line may settle, ranked — open invoices (by the invoice, order or quote number quoted, the amount, the customer), open supplier bills (by bill or PO number, amount, supplier), received expense documents not yet paid, and Book on Account to the matched CRM party when no document fits. Suggestions never write anything until you confirm one.

From a notification. Bank notifications — a large card spend, an unmatched incoming or outgoing payment — open the feed narrowed to that one line (Showing the transaction from your notification), with Show all transactions to go back.

How incoming transfers match to invoices

After every sync the platform runs a matching ladder, most-certain first, and only auto-applies the top two rungs:

  1. Reference match — the transfer text contains exactly one open invoice's number → matched automatically.
  2. Amount + name — exactly one open invoice whose balance equals the transfer to the cent and whose customer name matches the sender → matched automatically. (Names are transliterated, so a Greek bank statement still matches a Latin-script CRM name.)
  3. Weaker signals — amount-only or name-only gives up to five candidate invoices and the line goes to Needs review rather than guessing.
  4. Nothing — the line stays Unmatched.

Clearing the review queue

For a line that needs review, the feed gives you, inline:

  • Settle — a one-click button for the top suggestion.
  • Match to Invoice… / Attach to Order… (row menu) — search for the right open invoice, or the order whose open invoice it settles, if the suggestion is wrong.
  • Ignore — mark the line as not-a-customer-payment (and Unignore to undo).

Confirming a match records the payment against the invoice through the same routine as a manual payment — it books to receivables, allocates correctly, and never over-collects.

Outgoing transfers → supplier bills

Completed outgoing transfers reconcile the same way against your supplier bills — by bill number in the reference, or a cent-equal amount plus a supplier-name match — writing the payment and allocation so the bill's paid/outstanding figures derive correctly. This is how a batched supplier bill run marks its bills paid once it actually executes at the bank. It's automatic and idempotent — a re-delivered transaction repairs rather than double-books.

To match one by hand, use Match to Supplier Bill… in the line's menu. A payment matched to a bill — automatically or by hand — takes the bill's category, so an owner withdrawal booked under Partner withdrawals still counts as paid to owners once it is reconciled.

Outgoing lines → expense documents

An outgoing line can also pay a received expense document (a supplier invoice that came in through myDATA) directly: Match to Expense Document… in the row menu lists candidates ranked by exact amount, merchant name (Greek and Latin spellings match), closest amount and date. Attaching books the payment the same way as attaching it from the Expenses side; a card line is booked as a card payment.

Outgoing transfers → planned payments

What it is — rent, a tax or a payroll run has no supplier bill to match against, but it usually has a planned payment. When an outgoing line's amount equals an open planned payment's to the cent (within 45 days of its date), the line offers Close “<plan>” — or N plans match when several do, which opens a picker that ranks a same-counterparty plan first. The row menu also has Match to a Planned Payment…. What it affects — the bank line becomes the plan's payment (allocated to the bill the plan names, if any) and the plan closes, so the same payment is never recorded twice. A plan with a different amount is never offered.

When nothing matches — the alert

An incoming transfer that matches no open invoice emits the Unmatched Bank Payment event once, which you can route through Flows to notify whoever should chase it (e.g. “money arrived we can't tie to an invoice — is it a deposit, a wrong reference, or a new customer?”). It fires a single time per line so it never nags.

Self-healing sync. After each pull the platform asks Revolut which webhook deliveries failed and re-pulls anything missed, so a dropped webhook can't leave a gap in your feed — the sync is the repair.

Day-to-day: open Finance → Bank feed, hit Sync, set the status filter to Needs Review, and clear the queue with the Settle and Close plan buttons (or Match to Invoice… where a reference was missing). Everything confident is already matched; you only touch the ambiguous few.
