Bank feed & reconciliation

/finance → Settings → Accounts finance.manage

Once Revolut is connected, every transaction on your account flows into a bank feed, and the platform tries to reconcile each one automatically: an incoming transfer is matched to the invoice it settles, an outgoing one to the supplier bill it pays. The matches it's confident about are applied for you; the uncertain ones wait in a review queue for one click. Crucially, settling always goes through the platform's single money path — so a reconciled payment books exactly like a manually recorded one, and never over-pays.

Where: Finance → Settings → Accounts → the Bank feed card (under your bank accounts). The feed refreshes on a schedule and whenever you press Sync.

Reading the feed

Each line shows an arrow for direction (incoming vs outgoing), the counterparty name, the date and reference, the signed amount, and a plain-word match state:

StateMeaning
MatchedReconciled to an invoice or bill (a matched incoming line shows → <invoice number>).
Needs reviewThe platform has one or more likely candidates but isn't certain — waiting for you to confirm.
UnmatchedNothing plausible was found (see the flow alert below).
IgnoredYou've marked it as not-to-reconcile (an owner drawing, an internal transfer).

The header's Needs review toggle filters the feed to just the incoming lines that want attention, so you're not scrolling past everything already handled.

How incoming transfers match to invoices

After every sync the platform runs a matching ladder, most-certain first, and only auto-applies the top two rungs:

  1. Reference match — the transfer text contains exactly one open invoice's number → matched automatically.
  2. Amount + name — exactly one open invoice whose balance equals the transfer to the cent and whose customer name matches the sender → matched automatically. (Names are transliterated, so a Greek bank statement still matches a Latin-script CRM name.)
  3. Weaker signals — amount-only or name-only gives up to five candidate invoices and the line goes to Needs review rather than guessing.
  4. Nothing — the line stays Unmatched.

Clearing the review queue

For a line that needs review, the feed gives you, inline:

  • Settle <invoice#> — one-click buttons for the top suggestion(s).
  • Pick invoice — a search dialog to find the right open invoice by number if the suggestion is wrong.
  • Ignore — mark the line as not-a-customer-payment (and Unignore to undo).

Confirming a match records the payment against the invoice through the same routine as a manual payment — it books to receivables, allocates correctly, and never over-collects.

Outgoing transfers → supplier bills

Completed outgoing transfers reconcile the same way against your supplier bills — by bill number in the reference, or a cent-equal amount plus a supplier-name match — writing the payment and allocation so the bill's paid/outstanding figures derive correctly. This is how a batched supplier bill run marks its bills paid once it actually executes at the bank. It's automatic and idempotent — a re-delivered transaction repairs rather than double-books.

When nothing matches — the alert

An incoming transfer that matches no open invoice emits the Unmatched Bank Payment event once, which you can route through Flows to notify whoever should chase it (e.g. “money arrived we can't tie to an invoice — is it a deposit, a wrong reference, or a new customer?”). It fires a single time per line so it never nags.

Self-healing sync. After each pull the platform asks Revolut which webhook deliveries failed and re-pulls anything missed, so a dropped webhook can't leave a gap in your feed — the sync is the repair.

Day-to-day: open Finance → Settings → Accounts, hit Sync, flip on Needs review, and clear the queue with the Settle buttons (or Pick invoice where a reference was missing). Everything confident is already matched; you only touch the ambiguous few.
