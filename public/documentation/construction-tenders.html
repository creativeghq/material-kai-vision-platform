Construction · Tenders & Subcontracts

/projects/:id?tab=finance /bid/:token bid page owner · finance.manage

Tendering bundles a trade into a package, sends it to subcontractors to price, lets you compare bids fairly, and awards it — where the award IS the subcontract: it raises a purchase order and the commitment lands in your cost report. The comparison is built so a subcontractor who omits a line can't come out looking cheapest.

Where: a project → Commercial → Finance → the Tender packages card (?tab=finance; owner + finance access). Subcontractors price it on a public page at /bid/:token — no account needed.

The flow

  1. Create a package — name the trade/scope.
  2. Add items to price — pull them from the schedule (it brings the description, unit, quantity and cost code but never your rate — sending your price would answer the question you're asking), or type them.
  3. Invite subcontractors — pick CRM companies; each gets a bid with the quantities frozen, so everyone prices the same scope.
  4. Send the enquiry — this mints a private link per bidder and emails it (the link is returned even without an email, so you can paste it into WhatsApp).
  5. They price it — on the public /bid/:token page they see the scope and due date, fill in their rates, and Send my prices; a blank line is submitted as null, never 0, and they can revise until you decide. (You can also key rates in yourself and Mark received.)
  6. Compare — the grid highlights the lowest priced rate per line and shows a bid analysis (below).
  7. Award — one click raises the subcontract as a purchase order, writes its lines, withdraws the losing bids and puts the commitment into the cost report — all in one transaction.

Why the cheapest bid isn't always the cheapest offer

Bids are ranked on a comparable total, not on whatever number a bidder typed at the bottom. The comparison separates:

  • As submitted — the total of what they actually priced.
  • Not priced — the lines they left blank, valued at everyone else's rates so an omission is costed, not ignored.
  • Comparable — submitted + the valued omissions. This is the column the “lowest” badge sits on.

An unpriced line shows “not priced” (never 0), so a bid that quietly drops a line can't rank as the cheapest — and Award is disabled while any line is unpriced (the platform refuses it too). Only received bids enter the comparison.

The award is the subcontract

There's no separate “create a subcontract” step: awarding a package IS the subcontract, expressed as a purchase order. From that moment it's a committed cost in the CVR, an awarded package is locked read-only, and paying the subcontractor runs through the normal payables path.

From chat: the assistant can read tender status (who was invited / replied / awarded) and run a bid analysis that ranks on the comparable total and flags unpriced lines and outliers — but awarding stays a human decision.
