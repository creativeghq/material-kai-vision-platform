Knowledge Base

/knowledge-base Public

The platform's searchable, image-rich library of materials and knowledge documents — built automatically from supplier PDFs and catalogues. Because it's public, it doubles as a shareable catalogue you can send to a customer or open without signing in.

What this page is for

The Knowledge Base is the front door to everything the platform knows about materials. When a supplier PDF or catalogue is processed, the platform's extraction pipeline reads it page by page, finds each product, lifts out its imagery, recognises its specifications, and writes a clean, structured entry. The result is a single library where every material is consistent and searchable — no matter which supplier or catalogue layout it came from.

Each entry typically carries product imagery, a description, technical specifications, and a link back to the catalogue it came from. Because the page is public, it works equally well as your internal reference library and as a customer-facing catalogue you can share.

How search works

Search here is not a plain keyword match. The platform compares your query against several different signals at once and blends them into a single relevance score, so you can find materials by what they are and by what they look like — described in your own words.

SignalWhat it captures
TextThe words on the entry — name, description and written specifications.
Visual appearanceHow the material actually looks in its images, so a photo or a "looks like…" phrase can match.
UnderstandingAn AI reading of each material — what it is, where it's used and how it would be described.
ColourThe dominant colours and tones of the material.
TextureSurface character — smooth, grainy, ribbed, matte, glossy, and so on.
StyleThe overall aesthetic — modern, rustic, industrial, classic.
MaterialThe underlying material family — ceramic, wood, stone, metal, textile.

Because these signals are combined, natural-language queries work well. Searching for "warm matte stone for a bathroom floor" weighs the words, the look, the colour family and the surface together — rather than only matching the exact characters you typed.

Tip: describe the feel you want, not just the name. "Soft grey concrete-look with a fine texture" usually returns more useful results than a single keyword, because the visual and texture signals have something to work with.

Filtering with facets

Alongside the search box, facet filters let you narrow the library by its structured attributes. Pick one or more values and the results update to match:

  • Category — the type of product (for example tiles, flooring, fixtures).
  • Colour — filter to a colour family.
  • Material — the material family the item is made from.
  • Finish — surface finish such as matte, polished or textured.
  • …and more — additional facets appear automatically based on what the catalogues contain.

Facets are populated from the structured data the pipeline extracts, and equivalent values across languages and spellings are grouped together — so "white", for instance, gathers its variants under one filter instead of scattering across dozens of near-duplicates. You can combine facets with a text or natural-language query to zero in quickly.

Opening a material

Select any result to open its detail view, where you'll find:

  • Imagery — the product photos and crops the pipeline extracted from the catalogue.
  • Specifications — the recognised technical attributes (dimensions, finish, material and other catalogue details).
  • Source — a reference back to the catalogue or document the entry was built from, so you can trace any figure to its origin.

Conversational and visual search

For anything that's easier to describe than to filter — "something like travertine but darker", or finding matches for a photo you upload — the Agent Hub runs the same blended search engine. The difference is that it works as a conversation: you can refine across several turns, attach images, and it will explain why each result matched (the colour, the style, the material it picked up on). If you want to confirm whether an item in a photo exists in the library, Recognition identifies materials directly from an image.

Good to know: the Knowledge Base reflects the catalogues that have been processed for your workspace. If a material seems to be missing, it usually means its source catalogue hasn't been added or finished processing yet — not that the platform can't represent it.
