Discover · Products

/discover

The Products tab is one image-led catalogue of the materials and products published across the platform, plus your own workspace's drafts. It's the fast way to see what's available before you commit to a specific manufacturer or listing — a grid you can scan, filter, sort and page through.

Where it is

Top navigation → Discover → the Products tab (/discover#products opens it directly). Discover's tabs are Profiles, Products, Marketplace and, with the Real Estate module, Properties. A workspace without the marketplace sees only the catalogue, under the title Products.

What's on the screen

  • Browse / Smart search — the two ways to look (see Smart search below).
  • Search and filters — the box (“Search materials by name or brand…”) matches the product name or its brand; Filter opens “Filter materials” with category, brand and property groups.
  • Sort — Name A–Z or By Brand.
  • The grid — square image tiles with the category, name, brand and your price under the picture, 24 to a page. A tile can carry a Draft tag (not published — only your workspace sees it) or a Surplus €… tag (on offer on the Marketplace). Hover a tile (or, on a phone, look at its corner) for the quick actions: add to a moodboard, add to a quote, and See it on a surface.

How to use it — step by step

  1. Open Discover and select the Products tab.
  2. Type a product or brand name into the search box, or open Filter to pick a category, a brand or a property value.
  3. Sort by Name A–Z or By Brand, and page through the grid.
  4. Click a tile to open the product window, or use its quick actions to add it to a moodboard or a quote straight from the grid.

Worked example — scanning what's available. Open Discover → Products → Filter → pick the category you're after → sort By Brand → hover a tile you like and add it to a moodboard or a quote, or click it to read its full specification.

Everyone signed in sees the whole catalogue

The Products list is the cross-workspace catalogue — every supplier's published products, not just your own workspace's. Your own workspace's drafts show too, tagged Draft; other workspaces never see them. This matters most for a signed-up architect or specifier: you browse the full market here, then act on what you find. (Prices are still shown per viewer — your price versus retail — and cost and margin are never listed.)

The product categories

What it is — every product is filed under one of 23 categories, and each category carries its own set of technical fields (a door has a U-value, fire class and handing; a cable a cross-section and coil length). Why it matters — the category decides which properties a product can be filtered on and which fields are read off its catalogue on import. The same categories are what a supplier “deals in” in the CRM, so tagging a supplier and importing their catalogue use one vocabulary.

Filtering by material properties

Beyond category and brand, the Filter modal (“Filter materials”) carries a Properties group — the actual technical attributes the products in the list carry (finish, format, slip rating, and so on). The list is built from the catalogue itself: it offers only registry-known, public fields whose value appears on more than one product, so you're always filtering on something real rather than a fixed menu that may not apply. Pick one or more values to narrow the list.

Smart search & saved searches

The Products tab has two search modes. Plain Browse matches on name or brand (above); Smart search is the platform's multi-vector search — it reads intent, not just keywords, and ranks on text, image, colour, texture, style and material together. Results come back as image tiles, each with the product's cover picture, its match score and its category, so a search by pattern shows you the patterns.

  • Save a search you'll run again. With a smart query active, Save this search keeps it as a chip above the box. Click a saved chip any time to re-run it; the trash icon on a chip removes it. Your saved searches are yours, scoped to the workspace you're in, most-recently-used first.
  • If they can't be loaded the page says so — “Saved searches could not be loaded — this is not a statement that you have none” — rather than silently showing an empty rail.

The product window

Clicking a product opens its window. It leads with the picture: the gallery and the description with Key Specifications share the top row, and the gallery stays in view while you scroll the specs. Small images are never scaled up.

  • Header — the name, brand (linking to the brand's knowledge page), a colour chip that counts the colourways, and for tiles a Formats row drawing each size as an outlined box to scale.
  • Actions — Add to Quote, Add to Moodboard, and More with Test on a room, See it on a surface (the product tiled on a room surface at real size, no credits), Catalog shot and Stage in a room.
  • Tabs — Details, Knowledge, Related and Reviews for everyone. Pricing managers also get Monitoring, SEO and Pricing; Fiscal & Customs, Stock and Service appear for the people who manage those.

Details — each fact once

  • Specifications are grouped into cards by section (Application under Key Specifications, thermal figures inside Specifications). Every value is formatted — true/false reads Yes/No, field names never appear in snake_case, and a fact shown in one place is not repeated in another.
  • Appearance shows the product's colours as real swatches. Shades the AI only observed in photos are labelled as such.
  • Models — one table of the product's models or sizes with their per-model figures.
  • Item tables — the catalogue's own per-colour code tables (codes, sizes, pack data, discontinued or made-to-order items), one card per table with its source. Long tables have a search (“Search code, colour, size”) and colour, size, status and availability filters. A table flagged by the completeness checks carries a Needs review badge. Price columns are hidden from viewers without cost access.
  • Packaging — listed per size where the catalogue gives it; a value the supplier did not publish is marked est. (derived from the tile size or the same size in this or other brands); hover it for its basis.
  • Certifications — chips link to the recorded certificate or the Knowledge document that names the standard.

Certificates, datasheets & the original file

The Knowledge tab is where a product's compliance record and documents live, including documents still held as drafts (marked Draft, with a Publish action for editors). For a product in your own workspace you can also manage them here.

Certificates — a record, not a chip

A certificate is a proper record with a Standard (e.g. EN 13501-1), a Result (e.g. A1), a certificate number, an issuer, a scope, and Valid from / Valid until dates. The Certificates table shows each with a Status badge the platform derives from the expiry — Valid, Expiring soon, Expired, or No expiry stated — so a lapsing certificate warns you before it bites, and products can be filtered on what they're certified for.

  • Add certificate opens a form for those fields; each row has Edit and Remove. Editing needs it to be your product plus a finance/warehouse/pricing capability.
  • Read off the documents for you. Two suggestion rails offer standards the extractor already found — “From documents” (read out of the product's uploaded files) and “From product text” — as one-click drafts. A date it couldn't parse is left blank rather than guessed; you check each against the source before recording it.

The branded datasheet, and the original file

  • Datasheet PDF (on your own product) builds a branded one-product datasheet — your letterhead, the product's image, its specs and its certificates — and opens it to download.
  • Original PDF on a knowledge-base article hands over the source file the specs/certificates were read from, not just the text we extracted — so you can pass on the real document.

Getting your catalogue in and out

What it is — a bulk import/export for your own products, in the Data Import Hub (/admin/data-import, workspace admins). Why it matters — you can seed your catalogue from a spreadsheet and push it to a sales channel without re-keying.

  • Import from a spreadsheet (CSV) — it reads name, sku, description, category, barcode, country of origin, warranty and a product URL; anything else is ignored and price and cost are never taken from a file. Rows upsert by SKU, so re-importing updates products rather than duplicating them, and it reports how many were created, updated and skipped.
  • Upload a PDF catalogue — pick the Material Category and the file, and the catalogue is read into products. Multi-brand catalog (distributor) keeps each product's own brand and records the catalogue's company as a supplier of them (leave it off for a single-brand catalogue). Publish products when processing finishes (on by default) makes the products Active once every product in the PDF has processed; if any fails they all stay drafts. For a new edition of a catalogue you already loaded, set an Edition label, choose the catalogue it Replaces, and decide what happens to Products missing from this edition — Keep, only report them, Retire (mark discontinued), or Retire and list as needing a new source. Products are matched to the earlier edition and nothing is published until you review the changes.
  • Export to a channel — as CSV, JSON or XML, using your own column names or a saved channel mapping that renames them to a partner's vocabulary. Only active products are exported, and cost, margin and supplier links are never included.

What it affects elsewhere

Products you find here are the same items you specify on quotes and documents in Finance and search conversationally in the Agent Hub — Discover is the browse surface, the others are where you act on what you found. Suppliers can see what people searched their catalogue for, and the same product catalogue can be embedded on your own site as a searchable widget.
