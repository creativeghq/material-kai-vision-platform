Templates

/templates

Templates is the one place that answers “what can I start from?” Instead of building the same invoice, quote or project from scratch each time, you save any record as a template and reuse it — or start from a platform starter we ship. It's a cross-cutting hub: one home for reusable starting points across invoices, quotes, orders, projects, moodboards, contracts and more, plus signposts to the per-feature template libraries that live elsewhere.

Where: the Templates tile in the App Launcher, at /templates. The record types you see depend on your role — you only get tabs for the things you can actually create.

What's on the screen

A left rail chooses what the grid shows; the grid holds the template tiles.

  • Add New — create a template from nothing: pick what it makes (a record type) and give it a name. (Until now every template had to be born from an existing record via Save as template; this is the blank-start path.)
  • My Templates — the ones you've saved yourself, with a count. Each tile offers Use, Edit and delete.
  • Starters — the ready-made examples the platform ships. A starter tile offers Use and Copy to edit (which drops a copy into My Templates so you can tweak it without touching the original).
  • A tab per record type — Invoices, Quotes, Projects, Moodboards, Orders, Contracts, Expenses, Onboarding checklists, Listings and Customer terms (a ready set of payment/credit terms for a company, e.g. “Retail customer — pay on order” or “Supplier — 30 days”) — each with a count, filtering the grid to that type.
  • Other libraries — link-outs to the per-feature template systems that live in their own areas (below).

Using a template — draft vs. prefill

What Use does depends on the record type, and the difference is deliberate:

TypeWhat “Use” does
Quote, Project, Moodboard, Onboarding checklist, ListingCreates a draft record outright and drops you straight into it.
Invoice, Order, Expense, ContractOpens a pre-filled create form — it does not conjure the document behind you.

Why money and legal documents only pre-fill. An invoice, order, expense or contract created silently from a template would skip the things that make it real — the sequential numbering, the buyer-risk and myDATA classification, the fiscal fields. So a template for one of those fills the form and hands it to you; you review and issue it the normal way. It's the same reasoning as “re-order” producing a prefill rather than a finished order.

What a template captures (and what it never does)

A template stores an allowlist of the reusable content — for an invoice, that's the line items, notes, payment terms, default VAT rate and myDATA document type; for a project, its structure; and so on. It deliberately never copies the things that must be unique to a real record: ids, status, share tokens, fiscal numbers or any derived total. That's not a nicety — a cloned legal number would be a fake document, and a cloned share token would hand a stranger the new record. So a template is a safe starting point, never a duplicate of the original's identity.

Other libraries — templates that live elsewhere

Some things are “templates” but belong to their own feature, with their own editor. The hub doesn't absorb them; it points to them under Other libraries:

LibraryWhat it is
BlueprintsParametric scope-of-works that rescale from a few measurements and price from your services catalogue.
Transactional emailsThe email bodies your workspace sends automatically (in the email settings).
Marketing emailsDesigned campaign templates for bulk sends.
WhatsApp messagesApproved WhatsApp / SMS message templates (in messaging settings).
Catalog designsCover, background and accent styling for product catalogues.
Invoice & PDF designsHow your invoices, quotes and sheets are laid out and branded — in Finance → Settings.
XML import mappingsSaved supplier-feed field mappings.

Each opens in its own place — so the kitchen zone/pricing model, for instance, is still edited in the Blueprint Library, not here.

Worked example — a standard quote. Build a quote you send often, then Save as template from it. Later, open Templates → Quote → Use on it: a new draft quote opens, pre-populated with your usual lines and terms, ready to tailor and send. For a recurring invoice, Use instead opens the invoice form pre-filled — you give it a fresh number and issue it.
