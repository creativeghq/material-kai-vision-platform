Construction · Drawings, Markup & Takeoff

/projects/:id?tab=documents owner (write)

The Drawings & documents register is a controlled drawing register — not a folder of files. Each drawing is a numbered slot; revisions stack under it and exactly one is marked current, so a client (or a trade on site) is only ever shown the current revision, never an older one. Drawings also feed the takeoff, which reads quantities off the PDFs into a quote, and you can mark one up — cloud the bit that's wrong, measure it, and raise it as an RFI in one step.

Where: open a project → the Delivery stage → the Documents section (?tab=documents). Adding, revising and marking up are owner-only; collaborators and clients see the current revision of the sheets you've made visible.

The register

Each row shows the drawing number (unique in the project, e.g. A-101), title, discipline, and the current revision (or an amber “No revision uploaded”). A purpose badge is green when the sheet is buildable (issued for construction or as-built) and amber otherwise — so an information-only or superseded sheet can't be mistaken for one you can build from. Expand a row to see its earlier revisions; the owner can Make current on any of them.

Adding a drawing

New document opens the dialog with two ways to fill it:

  • Scan the title block — drop in a sheet and it reads the title block and pre-fills the number, title, scale, discipline and sheet size (snapping to the platform's controlled lists; anything it can't map is reported, not silently dropped). It writes nothing until you confirm.
  • Type it — number, title, kind, discipline, scale, sheet size, and whether the current revision is shown to the client.

A scanned sheet is filed as revision A automatically. Creating is resumable: if the register entry saves but the file upload fails, the entry survives and pressing Create again just re-attaches the sheet.

Revisions

Upload revision suggests the next letter/number and asks for the file, the date printed on the sheet (“Issued on” — deliberately not defaulted to today, because it's the sheet's own date), what it was issued for, and what changed. The new one becomes current; the previous revision is kept and stays viewable. That's the whole point of a register: history is never lost, and everyone downstream sees the same current sheet.

Takeoff from drawings

What it is — quantities read off the project's drawing PDFs and the engineer's study, reviewed by a person and added to a quote. Why it matters — a construction job can start from the drawings the client hands you, without retyping a προμέτρηση or counting doors by hand.

Where: the Specification stage → Estimate → From Drawings (on a Construction Contract project, or with Drawing Takeoff switched on under Sections). Start from drawings on the Projects list creates a construction project and opens this view. Uploads are PDF only.

  1. Add the drawings. The list shows each drawing with its Status (Not analysed, Analysing…, Failed), Pages, To Review and Confirmed counts.
  2. Analyse a drawing (one credit). Each page is sorted into CAD-exported or Scanned. On CAD-exported pages the printed scale, room area labels, door swings and wall lines are read; printed tables — the προμέτρηση and door, window and radiator schedules — are transcribed; on scanned pages symbols are counted. Scans give counts only, never lengths.
  3. Review on the sheet. Everything arrives as a proposal in the Quantities list, tagged with where it came from — Printed, Detected, Counted, Seen on scan or By hand. Pick rows to highlight them on the sheet, then Confirm or Reject each one.
  4. Measure what is missing with the sheet tools: Find all like this (drag a box tightly around one symbol to count every match — CAD-exported pages only), Count (click each item), Length (click along a run, double-click to finish), Area (click round the outline, double-click to close) and Set scale (click both ends of a dimension you know and type its Real Length).
  5. Add to quote. Tick confirmed rows and press Add N to quote: choose a quote (or New draft quote) and a line (or New line). The quantity comes from the confirmed measurements; you set the rate on the quote. One quote line has one unit, so rows in different units are added separately.

No scale, no length. A sheet with no scale says “No scale — lengths and areas need one”, and a measured length or area cannot be confirmed until the scale is set. Setting the scale later re-derives every measurement on that sheet.

When a drawing is revised

Analysing a new revision compares it with the previous one. Areas that changed are shaded on the sheet (“Shaded areas changed since the previous revision”), confirmed measurements inside them are flagged for remeasure, and the Quotes section warns on the lines they feed. After checking a row, mark it Checked against the new revision.

From chat: the assistant's drawing takeoff tool can report status and start an analysis, and can confirm transcribed printed rows only after you press Approve. Counted and measured figures are confirmed only by a person on the sheet.

Marking up a drawing → an RFI

What it is — the Mark up action on a drawing opens a viewer where you can cloud, box, arrow, add a note, and measure — then raise the marked region as an RFI. Why it matters — “this dimension doesn't work” is far clearer as a cloud on the exact spot than as a sentence.

  1. Pick the Cloud tool and outline the problem area (double-click to close the cloud).
  2. To measure, first Set scale: click the two ends of something whose real length you know and type that length — the printed title-block scale is deliberately not trusted. Until you've calibrated, the sheet shows “Not to scale — measurements unavailable” and any measurement reads “not measured” rather than a made-up number.
  3. Select your markup, add a “What is wrong with this?” note, and Raise as RFI. It's idempotent — pressing it again returns the same RFI, never a second numbered question — and the drawing then shows an “RFI raised” badge. You can also Export the marked-up sheet as a PDF.

Worked example. A subcontractor flags a clash. Open the current architectural sheet → Mark up → cloud the clash, set the scale off a known 3000 mm grid line, measure the overlap → add the note and Raise as RFI. It lands in the project's Requests register as a numbered RFI, tied to that sheet, with the marked-up PDF attached.

From chat: the drawing register and markup are UI tools — the assistant does not operate them.
