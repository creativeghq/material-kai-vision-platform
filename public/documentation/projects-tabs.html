Projects · Kinds, stages & who sees what

/projects/:id /projects/:id?tab=…

A project's page shows only the sections its kind of job needs. A design-and-supply job gets rooms, moodboards and selections; a construction contract adds drawing takeoff, tenders, valuations and cost-value reconciliation. This guide covers the four kinds, the six stages the sections sit in, how to switch sections on or off, who on the job sees what, and what an invited client, architect or subcontractor gets.

The kind of project

What it is — the first choice in the New Project dialog. It decides which sections the project offers. Why it matters — a trip or an internal stock move does not need a moodboard, and a bathroom refit does not need tender packages. Each job shows the tools it uses.

KindForWhat it adds
GeneralTasks, documents and costs: a trip, a stock move, an internal job.Tasks, Documents, Purchases, Costs, Requests.
Design & SupplyRooms, moodboards and selections, quoted and supplied to a client.Assessment, Rooms, Moodboards, Presentations, Selections, Quotes, Contracts, Invoicing, Purchases, Costs, Tasks, Documents, Requests.
Renovation & Fit-OutDesign through site work: estimate, purchases, snags and handover.Everything in Design & Supply, plus Estimate, Changes, Site and Equipment & Warranties.
Construction ContractA main or trade contract.Everything in Fit-Out, plus the construction features: Drawing Takeoff, Tender Packages, Valuations & Retention and Cost-Value Reconciliation.

Overview and Timeline are on every project. Start from drawings on the Projects list opens the same dialog with Construction Contract chosen and, once created, lands on the drawing takeoff (see Takeoff from drawings).

Changing the sections of one project

  1. In the project header, open the ⋯ menu and choose Sections (owner and project managers).
  2. The Project Sections dialog shows the Kind of Project, a tick list of Sections and a tick list of Construction Features.
  3. Change the kind to reset the ticks to that kind's defaults, or tick and untick single sections. Press Save.

Hiding never hides data. A section that already holds records stays on whatever the kind or the ticks say. If a fit-out job already has a tender package, Purchases keeps showing it.

The six stages

The sections sit in six stages on one row across the page. The selected stage lists its sections in a rail beside the content, or in a strip above it on a narrow screen. A stage with one visible section opens that section directly. Going back to a stage reopens the section you left it on.

StageSections
OverviewOverview, Timeline (project events, requests and site activity in one list, with a filter), Assessment.
DesignRooms, Moodboards, Presentations (presentation and technical sheets together with client views).
SpecificationSelections (what the client picked, with allowances; order progress is read from the linked order line), Estimate (From Dimensions with a blueprint, or From Drawings with takeoff).
CommercialQuotes, Changes (the one variations register), Contracts, Invoicing, Purchases, Costs. See Commercial & cost control.
DeliveryTasks (List, Board, Calendar and the Schedule), Site, Documents (including Drawings), Equipment & Warranties (installed base).
ClientRequests (RFIs, submittals, approvals and client questions).

Every section has its own link (/projects/:id?tab=quotes), so a notification or a colleague can send you straight to it.

Who sees what

WhoWhat they get
OwnerEverything, including Delete and handing the project over.
Project managerEvery section the project offers. Invoicing and Costs need finance access as well. Edits the project, changes its sections, archives it, invites people and manages the team.
Team memberEvery section except Invoicing and Costs: the working surface without the money.
Invited guestNot the team's page at all. A guest gets their own project home (below).

Teammates are added on the Overview's Team panel. Guests are invited by email.

Inviting a client, architect or subcontractor

What it is — a passwordless email invitation (/projects/invite/:token) to someone outside your workspace. Why it matters — the people around a job can follow it, answer questions and sign approvals without a seat in your workspace.

  1. In the project header, press Invite client (owner and managers). The dialog is Invite People to This Project.
  2. Enter the Email Address and pick a Role:
    • Client — sees quotes, moodboards, documents and the progress; can ask questions and sign approvals.
    • Architect or Subcontractor — no prices; sees only the requests they raise or are assigned; can raise RFIs and submittals.
  3. Add an optional Personal Message and press Send invitation.
  4. The guest opens the link, signs in with a one-time code sent to that email, and accepts. The dialog lists each invite as Pending, Active, Expired or Revoked; you can copy the link, re-send it or revoke access.

What a guest sees

A guest opening the project gets one page marked Shared with you, with these tabs:

  • Updates — a timeline of the requests, inspections and snags they may see, a Waiting on You card listing what needs their answer (an approval to sign, or a question the team has answered), and the client and deadline.
  • Requests — the request register, with a count of what is waiting on them.
  • Design — rooms (without budgets), moodboards and presentations.
  • Quotes — the customer view of the project's quotes. Clients only; an architect or subcontractor never sees prices.
  • Documents and Progress (the tasks marked Client visible and the schedule).

Worked example. Add your site supervisor to the team as a Member, invite the homeowner as Client and the structural engineer as Architect. The supervisor works in the project like you do, minus the money. The homeowner sees the design, the quotes and the progress and signs approvals. The engineer sees the RFIs they raised or were assigned, and nothing priced.
