Projects · Other tabs

/projects/:id

A project's detail page is organised into tabs that cover the work and its deliverables. This guide summarises the remaining tabs — the ones beyond Rooms and the Client View — in one line each, and shows how to bring teammates and clients into a project with an invite link.

Where it is

Top navigation → Projects → open a project to reach its detail page (/projects/:id). The tabs run across the project's detail page. The set you see depends on your role — some tabs are owner-only or shown only when their module is enabled, and shared collaborators get a reduced set.

What's on the screen — the tabs

Each tab on the detail page does the following:

TabWhat it's for
OverviewA summary of the project — client, budget and deadline, status, key counts and a starting point.
PlanThe estimating engine owner-only — import a blueprint, enter measurements, and the plan prices itself (options, allowances, versions, quote/e-sign, material list, change orders).
ProductsThe products/materials gathered for this project owner-only.
MoodboardsThe moodboards attached to the project.
QuotesThe quotes for the project.
BillingBilling for the project owner-only.
FinanceProject-linked finance, when the Finance module is available — including the orders scoped to this project, so you see the deal money for this job alone.
SheetsPresentation sheets across the project's boards.
TasksTo-dos for the project.
TimelineActivity history owner-only — kept private from clients.

The two tabs covered in their own guides are Rooms (break the project down by room or area for organisation and budgeting) and the Client View (the client-ready deliverable, owner-only) — the headline capability the other tabs feed into.

Inviting collaborators & clients

What it is — a way to bring other people — teammates or the client — into a project via an invite link (/projects/invite/:token).

Why it matters — projects are collaborative: you may want a colleague working alongside you in the same boards and quotes, or the client looking in on progress. Invitations let you do that without handing over your whole workspace — shared users see the project marked as shared and get a reduced set of tabs, so the owner-only tabs (Products, Billing, Client View, Timeline) stay private.

How to invite a collaborator — step by step

  1. Create an invite for the teammate or client you want to bring into the project.
  2. Share the invite link (/projects/invite/:token) with them.
  3. The invitee lands on a public landing page and accepts the invite after signing in, gaining access to the project.

What it affects: accepted invitees gain access to the project's non-private tabs and see it flagged as shared; the owner-only tabs remain hidden from them.

Worked example — bringing in a colleague. Create an invite for the teammate → send them the link at /projects/invite/:token → they open the public landing page, sign in, and accept. They now see the project's non-private tabs (Overview, Moodboards, Quotes, Finance, Sheets, Rooms, Tasks) marked as shared, while Products, Billing, Client View and Timeline stay private to you.

In short: the tabs keep a project's many moving parts separated but reachable from one place; Rooms and the Client View have their own guides, and invite links bring teammates and clients in with a reduced, owner-safe set of tabs.
