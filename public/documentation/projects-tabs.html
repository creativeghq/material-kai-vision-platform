Projects · Other tabs

/projects/:id

A project's detail page is organised into tabs that cover the work and its deliverables. This guide summarises the remaining tabs — the ones beyond Rooms and the Client View — in one line each, and shows how to bring teammates and clients into a project with an invite link.

Where it is

Top navigation → Projects → open a project to reach its detail page (/projects/:id). The tabs run across the project's detail page. The set you see depends on your role — some tabs are owner-only or shown only when their module is enabled, and shared collaborators get a reduced set.

What's on the screen — seven stages

The detail page groups its sections into seven stages on one row (you don't have to use them in order — they just gather related sections). A stage you can only see one section of shows as that section.

StageSections under it
OverviewA summary of the project — client, budget and deadline, status, key counts.
DesignRooms, Moodboards, Sheets (presentation & technical plans).
SpecificationProducts owner, Plan (the estimating engine) owner, Purchases (made-to-order joinery + supplier sheet).
CommercialQuotes, Contracts, Billing, and Finance — which holds the construction money chain (BoQ, variations/CVR, valuations/retention, tenders, labour). owner · finance.manage
DeliveryTasks & Programme, Site (diary, inspections, snags), Drawings.
ClientClient View owner, Requests (RFIs & submittals), Handover (warranties).
ReviewAI Assessment, Timeline owner.

Several sections have their own guides: Rooms, the Plan & Estimating engine, Technical Plans, the Client View, and the whole Construction & Contracting toolset (Commercial, Delivery and Client stages). What you see depends on your role: a shared collaborator gets a reduced set (the owner-only sections stay private).

Inviting collaborators & clients

What it is — a way to bring other people — teammates or the client — into a project via an invite link (/projects/invite/:token).

Why it matters — projects are collaborative: you may want a colleague working alongside you in the same boards and quotes, or the client looking in on progress. Invitations let you do that without handing over your whole workspace — shared users see the project marked as shared and get a reduced set of sections, so the owner-only ones (Products, Plan, Purchases, Billing, Finance, Contracts, Client View, Handover, Timeline and Assessment) stay private.

How to invite a collaborator — step by step

  1. Create an invite for the teammate or client you want to bring into the project.
  2. Share the invite link (/projects/invite/:token) with them.
  3. The invitee lands on a public landing page and accepts the invite after signing in, gaining access to the project.

What it affects: accepted invitees gain access to the project's non-private tabs and see it flagged as shared; the owner-only tabs remain hidden from them.

Worked example — bringing in a colleague. Create an invite for the teammate → send them the link at /projects/invite/:token → they open the public landing page, sign in, and accept. They now see the project's non-private sections (Overview, Rooms, Moodboards, Sheets, Quotes, Tasks, Site, Documents, Requests) marked as shared, while the owner-only ones (Products, Plan, Purchases, Billing, Finance, Contracts, Client View, Handover, Timeline, Assessment) stay private to you.

In short: the tabs keep a project's many moving parts separated but reachable from one place; Rooms and the Client View have their own guides, and invite links bring teammates and clients in with a reduced, owner-safe set of tabs.
