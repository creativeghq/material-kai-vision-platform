Viva.com Pro add-on · BYOK

/finance → Settings → Payments finance.manage · owner to activate

Viva.com is the provider for Greek and EU merchants who want card payments and bank transfers. It's bring-your-own-key: you connect your own Viva merchant account, and payments settle directly to your Viva wallet — the platform never holds your funds. Its standout feature is the Greek RF bank-transfer: a structured reference that lets a customer pay by plain bank transfer and have the invoice reconcile itself. This page covers the BYOK setup (there's more to connect than the other providers), the methods, and how each one settles.

Where: enable the add-on at Finance → Settings → Payments (owner-only), then open Configure. The same card also appears under Profile → Keys. Credentials are write-only — once saved they're never sent back to the browser.

Connecting your Viva account — the four steps

  1. Credentials. Viva issues two pairs, both required:
    • Smart Checkout pair — Client ID + Client Secret (authorise payments).
    • Merchant pair — Merchant ID (a UUID from Viva → Settings → API Access) + API Key (used to look orders up and verify the webhook).
    Also set the payment source code (case-sensitive; default Default) and the environment — demo for testing, production for live money.
  2. Return URL. Viva has no per-payment return URL, so you set it once on your payment source in the Viva dashboard, to the address the card shows you (…/pay/return).
  3. Webhook + Verify. Register the shown webhook URL in Viva and subscribe to Transaction Payment Created (1796), Transaction Failed (1798), and — for RF — Account Transaction Created (2054). Then Verify. The card refuses to show “Connected” until the webhook is verified, because without it a payment could complete at Viva and never be booked here.
  4. Payment methods. Toggle the methods you'll offer and switch on Offer Viva at checkout (disabled until the connection is fully verified).

Why the webhook is trusted-but-verified. Viva's webhook is treated only as a trigger — the platform never takes the money facts from the message itself. It reads the transaction (or order) back from Viva with your credentials and books only what Viva confirms. That's why the Merchant pair is required even just to receive payments.

Methods

MethodWhat it is
Card (Smart Checkout)The customer is redirected to Viva's hosted Smart Checkout to pay by card, then returned to your pay page. Proven and live.
RF bank-transferA Greek ISO-11649 creditor reference the customer quotes on a plain bank transfer — no IBAN needed on their side — which auto-reconciles the invoice when the money lands (see below).

Viva supports EUR plus several EU currencies (RON, PLN, CZK, HUF, SEK, DKK, GBP); RF is EUR / Greek-merchant only. You choose which methods to offer with the per-workspace toggles in step 4.

RF at checkout is being rolled out. The RF code on your documents is live (see below), but the RF option on the online pay page may show as “coming soon” while its end-to-end settlement is finished being verified. Card payments are fully live.

The RF bank-transfer — pay by transfer, reconcile automatically

This is Viva's differentiator. A never-expiring RF order is minted per invoice for the amount due, and the same RF code appears on the invoice/proforma PDF and email and on the online pay page. When the customer's bank transfer arrives quoting that reference, Viva's Account Transaction (2054) webhook fires; the platform then polls the order and, once Viva marks it Paid, settles the invoice — no manual matching. It's amount-aware (edit a proforma's total before issuing and the code refreshes) and fails closed (if it can't be booked safely, the code is simply withheld rather than risk taking money it can't reconcile). Full detail on how it prints is in Invoices.

Reconciliation, failures & reversals

A successful card payment or a paid RF transfer settles through the shared payment ledger, so it books like any other payment and never over-collects. A failed payment marks the attempt failed (the customer can retry). A reversal is flagged to owners/admins, never auto-un-allocated — you settle it with a credit note. Everything is idempotent, so a re-delivered webhook repairs rather than double-books.

Recap: owner enables Viva → paste both credential pairs + source code + environment → set the source's return URL in Viva → register & Verify the webhook (events 1796 / 1798 / 2054) → toggle methods and offer Viva at checkout. Enable the RF code on your documents for auto-reconciling bank transfers. Compare with Stripe and Revolut on the overview.
