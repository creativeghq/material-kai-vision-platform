Account & Billing

/profile /billing/subscriptions /billing/credits

This is everything about you on MaterialsHub. Your profile page opens into a row of tabs — your public profile and the services you offer, your inbox, appointments and reviews, plus the subscription, credits and billing that power the AI and generation features used across the platform. This guide walks every tab: what it is, why it matters, exactly how to use it, and what it changes elsewhere.

How to open it: click your Profile menu at the top-right of any page, or go straight to /profile. The page is organised into tabs along the top; each one is covered below. The workspace switcher (showing your role and plan) sits next to the Profile menu.

Recommended setup order: Profile (complete it and list your services) → Business (enrich from ΑΑΔΕ if you're a Greek company) → Social Accounts (connect what you publish to) → Credits / Subscription (top up or change plan as your usage grows). If you're brand new, read Getting Started first.

Workspaces

What it is — the workspace switcher in the top-right of the app, showing your current workspace plus your role and plan within it. Why it matters — each workspace keeps its own data, members and billing, so the workspace you're in is the context for almost everything you see across the platform. Switching workspaces switches that whole context.

How to use it

  1. Open the switcher menu in the top-right.
  2. Switch between workspaces to change which set of data, members and billing you're working in.
  3. Create a new workspace for a separate business, team or context.
  4. Open Manage network to administer the people and connections in your workspace.

What it affects: every page on the platform reflects the active workspace — its catalogue, finance, CRM, members and billing all change when you switch.

Profile tab

What it is — the place where you set up how you appear to the rest of the platform. The Profile tab is your public profile: what you fill in here feeds a sharable public page. Why it matters — a complete profile is your first impression whenever you share your link or appear elsewhere on the platform — your name, business, role and listed services are how clients and partners size you up.

It's organised into a few sections:

  • Appearance & avatar — upload a profile photo and set how your name and role are presented.
  • Personal information — your name, contact details and role.
  • Business details — company name, address, VAT/tax details and other registration info. For Greek companies these can be enriched from the ΑΑΔΕ business registry: enter your Greek VAT number and pull official details in one step (see Greek e-invoicing for how the same registry data is used for tax documents).
  • Services you offer — list the services you provide using the Add Service button. These appear on your public profile so others know what you do.

Your public profile

What you put in the Profile tab feeds a sharable public page at /u/:userId Public. Use the Copy link button to grab the URL and share it with clients or partners — they can view your name, business, role and listed services without needing an account.

Tip: A complete profile with an avatar and a clear list of services makes a much stronger impression when you share your public link or appear elsewhere on the platform.

Credits

What it is — credits are the platform's currency for AI and generation actions. Why it matters — running an AI model or a generation job has a real cost, so those actions are metered in credits while everyday use stays free. Spending credits is how you turn your balance into images, sheets, scans and 3D worlds. What consumes them: generating images, presentation sheets, scans, 3D/VR worlds, and other metered tools.

The Credits tab shows:

  • Balance — how many credits you have available right now.
  • Usage history — a record of what consumed credits, so you can see where they went.
  • Percentage of your monthly allowance — your balance against your plan's allowance, so you can see at a glance how much of your plan you've used.

Everyday chatting and searching in the Agent Hub is inexpensive. Only explicit generation actions are metered, and the assistant tells you the cost before it runs one. A full list of which actions cost credits is in Tools & Capabilities.

What it affects: when your balance runs out, metered generation actions are blocked until you top up (below) or your monthly plan allowance refreshes.

Buying credits

What it is — the credit-pack store at /billing/credits. Why it matters — it's the fastest way to refill when you run low, and bigger packs earn bigger volume discounts, so buying in larger amounts stretches your money further.

How to buy a credit pack

  1. Open /billing/credits when you run low.
  2. Choose one of the available credit packs (each button shows exactly what you'll receive, e.g. "Buy 2,631 Credits for €25"), or enter a custom amount.
  3. Complete the purchase through secure checkout.
  4. Your new balance is reflected in the Credits tab right after payment.

Plans & subscriptions

What it is — your plan, shown in the Subscription tab and changed at /billing/subscriptions. Why it matters — plans are tiered by the monthly credit allowance they include, and higher plans raise your quotas and unlock additional capabilities across the platform's modules. If you're hitting limits or want more advanced features, this is where to move up.

TierMonthly credit allowance
Entry100 credits / month
Pro1,000 credits / month
Enterprise5,000 credits / month

How to change your plan

  1. Open the Subscription tab to see your current plan.
  2. Go to /billing/subscriptions and pick a plan.
  3. Use Manage Subscription, Cancel Subscription, or Subscribe to Enterprise as needed.
  4. Confirm to apply the change.

What it affects: your monthly credit allowance, your quotas, and which advanced capabilities are available across the platform's modules.

API Keys

What it is — credentials, also managed in the Subscription tab, that authenticate the external partner APIs for price tracking, mention tracking and job tracking. Why it matters — they let other projects call those services on your behalf. Keys are prefixed kai_ and are shown masked (e.g. kai_••••gBJd) once created.

How to generate and test a key

  1. Click Generate Key to create a new kai_* key.
  2. Note the Created and Last used details listed against each key.
  3. Use Test to verify the key works.
  4. Review the Changes Log for the history of key activity and changes.

What it affects: a valid key authorises external calls to the price, mention and job tracking APIs against your account.

Billing

What it is — your payment details, the invoices for your subscription, and your full ledger of credit transactions. Why it matters — it's the single record of money in and out: every top-up and every credit-consuming action is listed, so you can reconcile spend and download invoices.

The ledger lists every entry with these columns:

What it affects: nothing downstream — this is your audit trail. The Balance After column reconciles directly with the balance shown in the Credits tab.

Social Accounts

What it is — the tab where you connect social media platforms to your account. Why it matters — once a platform is connected, you can publish content to it directly from MaterialsHub instead of switching apps.

How to connect an account

  1. Click Connect to link a new platform.
  2. Use Refresh to re-sync a connected account if it falls out of date.
  3. Connecting and disconnecting accounts is managed entirely from this tab.

What it affects: connected platforms become available as publishing targets when you create and send social content.

Inbox, Appointments & Reviews

What it is — three personal-activity tabs. Why it matters — they keep your conversations, upcoming meetings and reputation in one place so nothing about you slips through the cracks.

  • Inbox — your personal messages and conversations in one place. For the workspace-wide, multi-channel messaging experience, see the dedicated Inbox documentation.
  • Appointments — meetings and bookings that have been scheduled with you, so you can keep track of what's coming up. You can filter by status (for example "all") to focus the list.
  • Reviews — feedback left by people who have worked with you, visible on your profile as your reputation. You can reply to each review.

How to reply to a review

  1. Open the Reviews tab to see feedback others have left.
  2. Find the review you want to respond to.
  3. Use the reply action to post your response — it appears alongside the review on your profile.

What it affects: your replies are part of your public reputation; appointment scheduling ties back to the Appointment Availability you set on your profile.

Step by step: complete your profile

  1. Open your Profile from the avatar menu in the top-right, or go to /profile.
  2. Under Personal Information, click Edit and fill in your name, contact details and role.
  3. Under Business, click Edit to add your company details. For a Greek company, enter the ΑΦΜ (VAT number) and pull official details from ΑΑΔΕ in one step — see Greek e-invoicing for how the same registry data is reused.
  4. Add the services you offer with the Add Service button, so they appear on your public profile.
  5. Use Copy link to share your public profile at /u/:userId.

Step by step: top up credits

  1. Open the Credits tab to see your current balance and usage history.
  2. Go to /billing/credits.
  3. Choose a credit pack that suits your usage.
  4. Complete checkout — your new balance appears in the Credits tab right after payment.

Step by step: change your plan

  1. Open the Subscription tab to see your current plan.
  2. Go to /billing/subscriptions.
  3. Pick the plan you want.
  4. Confirm to apply the change. New users may want to start with Getting Started first.

Putting it together: set up your Profile and services first, connect any Social Accounts you publish to, then top up Credits or change your plan as your usage grows.
