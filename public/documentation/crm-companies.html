CRM · Companies

/crm /crm/companies/:id crm.view

The Companies tab is where you keep the organisations you trade with — your customers, suppliers and partners. A company is your billing counter-party: the legal name, address and VAT number you confirm here are exactly what prints on an invoice, so this is the record that makes your documents legally correct. It is also where the Greek business-registry lookup (ΑΑΔΕ) and the EU VIES VAT validation live, and where you decide whether a company sits on your sales side, your purchases side, or both. This guide walks the list, the New Company form group by group — what each captures, why it matters, how to fill it in, and what it changes elsewhere on the platform.

Where it is

Top navigation → CRM → the Companies tab (alongside Pipeline, Contacts and Categories). A toolbar Add company button creates a new organisation, and a search box ("Search companies…") filters the list — it matches the name, the trading name and the text of the notes written on a company. Clicking any row opens that company's own detail page at /crm/companies/:id.

How to open it: top navigation → CRM → Companies tab. To add one, click Add company on the toolbar — it first asks “What Are You Adding?” (see CRM).

What this tab is for

What it is — the tab for managing company accounts and business relationships. Why it matters — a company is your billing counter-party. The legal name, address and VAT number you confirm here are exactly what appears on an invoice, so this is the record that makes your documents legally correct. It's also where the Greek registry lookup and the EU VIES validation live, so getting a company on file properly means no re-typing, no mismatched names, and no rejected myDATA submissions because a VAT number was wrong.

The company detail page — the Account tab

What it is — clicking a company opens its detail page, where a company is treated as an Account you trade with over time. Its tabs are Activity, Details, Account, Contacts, Work (everything booked against the company — see below), Social & Web and — for suppliers — Products and Market (documented below). Details has its own side list: Address & Phone, Tax & VAT, Commercial & VAT, Categories and, for suppliers, Supplier profile. Orders drive the Account, so everything money-related lives together on the one Account tab. The account overview is pinned at the top — the balance, aging and money on account stay on screen — and below it a tab strip holds the detail, so switching between, say, Orders and Payments never scrolls the balance away:

The pinned overview leads with the Account balance — a single, two-directional figure: outstanding invoices plus un-invoiced order cash, less any credit you're holding for them, less what you owe them as a supplier — shown as “due to us”, “in their favour” (they've paid ahead / you owe them) or “settled”, alongside aging and money on account. (“Account balance” is what used to be labelled “net”; the old one-way “Owed on orders” tile is gone — that cash is now part of this one balance.) The tab strip below it:

Account tab sectionWhat it shows
OrdersEvery order for this company. Invoices, payments and profit live per order (open an order to see them), so this is the company's order history and the source of the balance above.
PaymentsThe payments in and out for the account.
InvoicesThe invoices raised to this party.
Top itemsWhat this customer buys most — the items to push.
Payment rulesThis customer's pricing level and per-customer overrides (credit limit, payment terms, minimum order) — see the Pricing & Payment Rules section below.

The same account component is mounted on both the company and the contact record, so the two can't drift on what an account contains.

The detail page uses a two-column layout with collapsible sidebar sections, and a unified Activity/Notes view records what's happened.

Taking a customer's margin across all their orders. The account shows how much of this customer's margin you've taken and how much is left. You can now take it across every order at once from here — enter an amount and it spreads oldest order first, recording a share against each order (never a vague party-level lump), so each order still reports its own truth and each share stays separately reversible. The cap is the same figure the per-order control uses; a party trading in mixed currencies is refused rather than summed into a meaningless total.

Business vs solopreneur. A contact who runs a business can be promoted to (or linked with) a business Account; when that happens, the financials (orders, invoices, balance) roll up to the business rather than the individual, and the individual contact's financial tabs are hidden in favour of a “Check Company” link to the Account. This keeps one customer's money in one place instead of split across a person and their company.

The Contacts tab — people, agencies and the factories they represent

Attached Contacts lists the people at this company, with their job title. A person's title is kept in one place — on the person — so the title you type when attaching someone is the one shown on their own record too.

Agencies and the factories they represent suppliers

What it is — many factories sell in Greece through an agency. You can link the two records, with the agency person who handles each factory. Why it matters — when you open a factory, you immediately see who to call and how to reach them, instead of remembering which agent carries which brand.

  • On an agency (a supplier whose Type is Agent — see Supplier profile below): a Factories represented card — Factory, Country, Makes, Ask (the agency person to contact), Last contact — with Add factory.
  • On a factory: a Represented by card — “The agencies that sell this factory in Greece. Orders and prices go through them.” — with Add agency. It lists the person who handles the link first, then every other person attached to the agency, with phone and email. Agency and factory names are links to their records.
  • Adding a link — search the CRM for the other business (create it first if it isn't there), choose the Arrangement (Agent, Exclusive distributor or Distributor) and the Person at the agency.
  • The tab's Contacts (n) count includes the people you reach through an agency, so a factory with nobody of its own attached no longer reads 0.

The Market tab — competitive & market intelligence suppliers only

What it is — a market-intelligence view that appears as an extra tab on a company's detail page only when the company is flagged as a supplier. Why it matters — for the brands you buy from, it answers four questions — each of which costs money or a round-trip, so they're asked one at a time — organised as a four-item sub-tab rail: Demand, Pricing, Competitors and Financial. (This is where the platform's supplier analytics now live — the old standalone Supplier Analytics page was retired into here; a separate, platform-wide Market Trends page covers buyer demand across the whole platform.)

Demand

What it shows — how MaterialsHub users engage with this supplier's products in your catalogue over the last 90 days — a read-only analytics roll-up (no credit cost), populated once the supplier's products are linked to the company (the brand link on the Details tab). Panels: the headline Products / Views / Saves / Quote adds counts and the save→quote rate; “Where demand comes from” (geography); Most-engaged products; Category mix; the Quote pipeline; and Reviews. A second card, Attribute explorer, rolls up which material attributes buyers actually engage with. If the read fails, it says so plainly (“a query failure, not a statement about your data”) rather than showing an empty result.

Pricing

What it shows — the products you carry from this supplier (matched on the brand link), each with “Our cost”. Two actions per product:

  • Check market — runs a one-shot live price scan and returns the market min–max range, median and retailer count, then shows your Headroom / Underwater figure (median minus your cost) so you can see at a glance whether you're buying below or above the going rate. This debits credits (it calls out to live pricing sources) and is admin-only; if the product is already monitored and was scanned in the last few hours it reuses that snapshot for 0 credits.
  • Monitor — a toggle that enrols the product into ongoing price monitoring so its market price is refreshed on the daily cadence (turn it off to stop tracking).

Competitors

What it shows — who competes with this supplier, in three parts:

  • In your CRM — automatically finds other companies in your CRM whose ΚΑΔ activity codes overlap this one's. Each is a clickable link tagged Supplier / Customer. (No ΚΑΔ codes yet? It prompts you to run the ΑΑΔΕ/ΓΕΜΗ import first.)
  • Discover competitors — a button that finds competitors on the open web, trying Apollo → Gemini grounded on Google Search → a general web search (first source with results wins, and it labels which answered). Each result shows name, industry/location and a website link.
  • Similar in your CRM — “ranked by what they do, not by ΚΑΔ.” This is the new lookalikes list (#289): it compares the meaning of each company's registry activity text and surfaces the ones most alike, so a “tile importer” and a “ceramics wholesaler” can match even with different filed codes. Instead of a meaningless percentage it labels each as Very close, Close or Related, and only lists ones that genuinely stand out. Each row links to the company.

What costs credits here: only Discover competitors (paid Apollo / Gemini / web look-ups from your credit pool). The In your CRM and Similar in your CRM lists are free — the lookalike comparison uses a vector the company already carries, so asking costs nothing. Discovered competitors are reference only (no one-click “add to CRM”).

Financial

What it shows — the money side of the supplier relationship, drawn entirely from your own finance data (not the registry): Billed to us, Paid to them and We owe (outstanding) from your accounts-payable ledger. It also flags unbooked myDATA documents — received documents matched to this supplier's VAT that you haven't turned into a bill yet — with a link straight to Finance → Expenses, plus an Open ledger button to the supplier's full account.

How to add a company and enrich it from ΑΑΔΕ

  1. Open CRM from the top navigation and go to the Companies tab.
  2. Click Add company. In “What Are You Adding?” pick Customer · Business or Supplier — that sets the customer/supplier role for you.
  3. Enter the business name and, for a Greek company, its ΑΦΜ (VAT number), and run the lookup — ΑΑΔΕ fills the legal name, registered address, tax office (ΔΟΥ), activity codes (ΚΑΔ) and status, and ΓΕΜΗ adds the registry details.
  4. Press Continue: the company form opens with that identity filled in. Check it.
  5. For an EU company, use Validate (VIES) on Details → Tax & VAT to verify the VAT number before you trade on credit or issue documents.
  6. Save. The confirmed billing details flow straight through to documents in Finance.

Greek business lookup (ΑΑΔΕ / myAADE)

What it is — a one-click pull of a Greek company's official profile from the tax-authority registry (ΑΑΔΕ), using the company's ΑΦΜ. Why it matters — typing legal details by hand invites typos that get a myDATA submission rejected; the registry is the source of truth, so this is the recommended way to validate a VAT number before invoicing a Greek customer or supplier. On a company's detail page (Details → Tax & VAT), enter the ΑΦΜ with country EL and press Fetch from AADE + ΓΕΜΗ; the platform retrieves and fills:

  • Legal name and commercial title.
  • Registered address — street, number, postcode and city.
  • Tax office (ΔΟΥ).
  • Activity codes (ΚΑΔ) — the company's primary and secondary business activities.
  • Status — whether the VAT number is active or has been deactivated.

What it affects elsewhere: the same registry data underpins Greek e-invoicing — see Greek e-invoicing for how invoices are transmitted to myDATA / ΑΑΔΕ, and note that Finance settings → General can block issuance against a VAT number the registry shows as inactive.

Lookups are cached for about 90 days. Once you've fetched a company's details, repeating the lookup within that window is instant and doesn't re-contact the registry — so there's no penalty for re-opening a record.

Each fresh lookup writes a notification to the looked-up company's TaxisNet inbox. This is required by tax-authority policy and can't be turned off. For that reason the lookup is meant for verifying your own business or a legitimate customer/supplier you actually trade with — not for researching arbitrary companies.

ΓΕΜΗ chained after ΑΑΔΕ

What it is — when you look up a Greek ΑΦΜ in the Add Company flow, the platform now runs the ΓΕΜΗ (General Commercial Registry) open-data lookup immediately after the ΑΑΔΕ one, in the same click. Why it matters — ΑΑΔΕ gives you the tax profile (legal name, address, ΔΟΥ, ΚΑΔ, active/inactive), but ΓΕΜΗ adds the commercial-registry identity that ΑΑΔΕ never returns. You get a fuller record from one action.

The chained ΓΕΜΗ pass adds:

  • Γ.Ε.ΜΗ. number — the company's commercial-registry number, shown as a verified badge and on the Details → Tax tab.
  • Legal form and registry status — the ΓΕΜΗ legal form and standing.
  • Extra activity codes — any ΚΑΔ activities ΓΕΜΗ lists are merged in alongside the ΑΑΔΕ ones (nothing is overwritten).

Best-effort, never blocking. If ΓΕΜΗ has no record or is briefly unavailable, the ΑΑΔΕ result still stands — the status line shows “Checking ΓΕΜΗ registry…” during the pass and simply moves on if there's nothing to add. For non-Greek EU companies the flow uses VIES instead, then fills soft profile fields from a web-search enrichment.

How to validate a VAT number with VIES

What VIES is — the EU's official VAT Information Exchange System. The Validate (VIES) button checks the company's VAT number against that system and confirms it is real and active. Why validate — before you extend credit or issue a cross-border document, you want to be sure the counter-party's VAT number is genuine; an invalid number can mean a rejected or non-compliant invoice. (For Greek ΑΦΜs, the ΑΑΔΕ lookup above does the equivalent against the national registry.)

  1. Enter the company's Country Code and VAT Number.
  2. Click Validate (VIES).
  3. If it checks out, the number is confirmed valid; record it as the company's billing VAT.
  4. Save.

Is this company on Peppol?

What it is — Check Peppol (next to Validate (VIES) on Details → Tax & VAT, once the company is saved and has a VAT number) asks the public Peppol Directory whether this party has a Peppol electronic address. Why it matters — Peppol is the European network for exchanging e-invoices; knowing a customer can receive them tells you whether an electronic invoice will actually reach them. The result is kept on the record as a badge:

  • Peppol: receives invoices — listed, and accepts standard (BIS Billing 3.0) invoices. Hover to see the participant id.
  • Peppol: listed, no invoice type — listed, but not for invoices.
  • Not on Peppol · date — the directory has no participant for this VAT number (as of that date).

If the directory can't be reached you get a “Peppol check failed” error — never a false “not listed”.

Role — supplier vs customer

What it is — which side of the relationship the company sits on. Why it matters — marking a company a supplier is what feeds it into payables, expense documents and the supplier account overview on the Business page; marking it a customer puts it on the sales/receivables side. Both can apply at once for a party you buy from and sell to.

FieldNotes
This is a supplierMark the company as a supplier.
This is a customerMark the company as a customer (either or both can apply).

Why mark is supplier? Payables, expense documents and the supplier account overview only pull from parties flagged as suppliers — so this single checkbox is what connects a company to everything you owe and pay. Likewise is customer is what puts the company on the receivables side, so it can carry quotes, sales documents and the invoices you raise.

Bank accounts — where you pay them, and the IBAN read off their invoice

What it is — the Bank Accounts card (in the party's Details → Tax area, on both companies and contacts) holds the counterparty's own bank accounts — the ones you'd pay them on. Why it matters — these are exactly the accounts the Record Payment dialog offers under Send to when you pay a supplier, and the ones Revolut's Verify holder name-check runs against. They're separate from your own accounts (which live in Finance → Settings → Accounts).

An IBAN printed on their documents is added for you

When you scan or import a supplier document (an expense/bill, a receipt, or an attachment that arrives in the Inbox), the platform reads any bank details printed on it — IBAN, bank name and account holder — and files them against the CRM company or contact the document belongs to:

  • An IBAN that passes its checksum is added to the party's bank accounts straight away, and you're told so (“Bank account added”). It becomes the primary account only if the party had none — it never displaces the account you already pay.
  • If it differs from the one on file, it's added beside it and the message says so: “New bank account added — differs from the one on file … their primary account is unchanged.”
  • An IBAN that fails its checksum can't be stored as an account — a character was misread, or the document has a typo. It waits under “Seen on their documents” on the Bank Accounts card, flagged Fails its checksum, with Review (opens the editor headed “Read off {document}” so you can retype it against the document) or Dismiss.
  • A one-off payee has no CRM record to keep it on, so nothing is filed and the message says so — pick or create the party in CRM if you want it kept.

Before paying a new account, check it. A changed IBAN on a supplier invoice is the commonest invoice fraud. When a document brings in an account that differs from the one you've been paying, confirm it with the supplier on a phone number you already had for them — not one printed on the document — before you choose it under Send to.

Read their e-invoices

What it is — on a company, the Bank Accounts card has a Read their e-invoices button. It opens the e-invoicing provider page (or PDF) that their newest myDATA document links to and reads what the issuer printed there. Why it matters — a supplier's e-invoice usually carries their IBAN, phones and email; this collects them without retyping.

  • IBANs — checksum-valid ones are added as bank accounts, as above.
  • Phones, fax and email fill only empty fields, or are added as extra phone numbers — nothing you've entered is overwritten. A blank address is filled from the myDATA issuer address. (Contact details are read from provider web pages only, never from PDFs.)
  • The result says what was added — or “Nothing new on their e-invoice”, “No e-invoice link to read”, “Their e-invoice page did not open”, or “…cannot be read automatically” for providers that block automated reading. A page that couldn't be read is never reported as empty.

What it is — Details → Supplier profile describes the supplier itself. Why it matters — an agent, a manufacturer and a wholesaler are reached and priced differently; recording which one you're dealing with (and whose brands they carry) is what links agencies to factories and products to the right source.

  • ΣΕΒΕ exporter directory — when the company is matched to its entry in the ΣΕΒΕ (Exporters Association of Northern Greece) member directory, the card links to that entry and shows the industries and Intrastat product codes it lists.
  • Where the details came from — for the website, phone, email, industry and description, the card says whether each was entered by your team, from ΑΑΔΕ, from ΓΕΜΗ, from their invoice, found online and confirmed, or found online, not confirmed (highlighted).

Researched details are saved only once the website proves it belongs to them. When the platform researches a company online, it saves a website and the details from it only if that site shows the company's own ΑΦΜ, ΓΕΜΗ number, an invoice phone, its registered address, or matches its ΣΕΒΕ directory entry. Anything it couldn't confirm comes back as a suggestion instead of being written in — so a namesake in another city can't take over the record. A detail read off the company's own invoice may replace one found online; what your team typed is never overwritten.

Compliance flags — withholding & producer registration

Two settings on a company record feed compliance elsewhere:

  • “Withholds tax (φορέας γενικής κυβέρνησης)” — mark a public body that pays most of an invoice and remits the withholding to the state. Recording it here is what makes the withheld part settle as a leg of the payment rather than sit forever as a debt — see withholding.
  • Producer registration (EPR) — for a supplier, their packaging producer-registration is verified here; an unverified supplier can block a sale that depends on it, not merely warn. See packaging & EPR.

Worked example — onboarding a Greek supplier you'll buy on credit

Open CRM → Companies → click Add company → pick Supplier. Type the supplier's name and ΑΦΜ and run the lookup — the legal name, registered address, ΔΟΥ, ΚΑΔ activity codes and active/inactive status fill in from the registry, so you don't risk a typo — then press Continue. Confirm the Invoicing & VAT billing identity (billing name, ΑΦΜ, ΔΟΥ, billing address) and set Include in ΜΥΦ on so their transactions appear in your Greek clientele summary. Agree terms in Pricing — say a default discount and a credit limit. The supplier role was already set by the card you picked. Save. The company now appears on the purchases/payables side and in the Business page's supplier overview, and its confirmed billing details flow straight onto the bills you record against it in Finance.

The company detail page

What it is — the full record behind a list row, at /crm/companies/:id. Why it matters — this is where the whole relationship lives in one view, and where you apply the segments and confirm the billing details that the rest of the platform reads. From there you can:

  • Edit core information — name, email, phone, address, VAT/tax details, notes.
  • Run the ΑΑΔΕ lookup and the VIES validation — fetch or re-confirm the company's registry profile and EU VAT status.
  • Assign categories — on Details → Categories, the colour-coded Categories picker and the What they deal in product categories (see Categories).
  • See associated records — the contacts linked to the company, and the documents and activity tied to it, so the whole relationship is in one view.

A company's billing details flow straight through to documents, so the legal name, address and VAT number you confirm here are exactly what appears on an invoice in Finance.

The Validate (VIES) button on a company verifies EU VAT numbers; for Greek customers and suppliers, VAT validation also feeds Greek e-invoicing. Segments and VAT-exemption categories used in the Invoicing & VAT group are configured in Finance settings. For the rest of the CRM — Pipeline, Contacts and Categories — see CRM.

Permissions

Access to the CRM is gated by the crm.view capability — users without it won't see this area, including the Companies tab.
