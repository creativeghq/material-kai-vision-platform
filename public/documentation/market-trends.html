Supplier Analytics turns real platform activity into market-demand intelligence for suppliers/brands — what professionals are searching for, saving and specifying across catalogues, and which way the market is heading week over week. This guide explains every part of the dashboard: what each demand signal means, why it matters, how to read the momentum and rising/declining views, and — most importantly — what to do with each finding. It also covers the filters, CSV export, the sample-data state before you have activity, and which insights unlock on higher tiers.

How to open it: top navigation → Market Trends (workspace admins) — or open /market-trends. For one supplier’s own numbers, open that company in the CRM and choose its Market tab.

1. What this page is for

What it is — a market-intelligence dashboard built for factory and manufacturer accounts. Why it matters — as designers, architects and other professionals discover products, save them to moodboards and add them to quotes, those actions become demand signals. This page aggregates and trends them so you can answer real commercial questions: which materials and categories are in demand right now? who is doing the buying? what is gaining momentum, and what is fading?

How it's sourced — the intelligence is drawn from across the platform, not just from your own catalogue, giving you a true read on the market you compete in. What you can narrow to — you can also focus the view on your own products where higher-tier comparisons are available.

How to read it as a whole: start with the headline cards for the current state, drop to Where the market is going for direction, then use Rising & declining to decide concrete actions — promote, stock, or expand. Set the filters first so every section reflects the scope you care about.

3. Where the market is going (momentum)

What it is — a dedicated section that reads the direction of the market by comparing recent activity with the period before it. Why it matters — the headline cards are a snapshot of now; this section is the trend — it tells you where demand is heading next so you can get ahead of it rather than react to it. It surfaces:

  • Emerging materials — items whose interest is accelerating, including materials the market is starting to ask for that may not yet be widely catalogued. How to act: a chance to be early — consider adding or promoting these before competitors saturate them.
  • Shifting design contexts — which room types and project settings are growing or cooling. How to act: shows where your materials will be needed next, so you can position products for the contexts that are heating up.
  • Growing buyer segments — which professional types are increasing their activity fastest. How to act: highlights audiences worth courting while their attention is rising.

4. Rising & declining materials

What it is — a ranked view of materials by save-velocity change — comparing saves over the most recent four weeks against the four weeks before. Why it matters — velocity (the change in saves, not the total) is what separates a material on the way up from one quietly fading, which is the single most actionable view for stocking and promotion decisions.

How to read it

  • Materials trending upward are flagged as rising; those losing ground are flagged as declining.
  • Fast-accelerating items carry an “Emerging” badge to call out genuinely new demand.

How to act on it

Read this alongside the headline signals to decide what to promote (rising items already getting attention), what to keep in stock (steady risers you don't want to run out of), and where to expand your range (Emerging items signalling demand you don't yet serve). Declining flags are your cue to wind down stock or refresh a tired line.

5. Filters & export

What it is — the controls above the dashboard. Why it matters — every signal and trend on the page is computed for whatever scope, category and window you set here, so the filters are how you turn a broad market read into one that's relevant to your business.

How to focus the analysis

  • Category — limit the view to a single product category (tiles, wood, lighting, sanitary and so on) or see all categories together.
  • Time window — choose how far back to look (for example the last 12 weeks).
  • Export CSV — download the current material-trend table, including growth and a short forward projection, to work with the numbers in your own tools.

What it affects: changing any filter recomputes the headline cards, the momentum section and the rising/declining table together — so the whole page stays consistent with the scope you've chosen.

6. Sample data before you have activity

What it is — a clearly-labelled preview state. Why it matters — until enough real activity has been recorded for your scope and filters, you'd otherwise see an empty page — so the dashboard shows representative sample figures instead, letting you understand the layout before your own data arrives.

Until enough real activity has been recorded for your scope and filters, the page displays clearly-labelled sample data so you can see what the dashboard will look like. A visible badge marks this state. As genuine activity accrues, the sample figures are replaced with your live numbers automatically — no action needed.

7. Per-supplier analytics

What it is — the analyses that answer “how is this one supplier doing” rather than “what is the market doing”. Where they live — not on this page. Open the supplier in the CRM and choose its Market tab, which covers:

  • Demand — views, saves and quote adds on that supplier’s catalogue, plus where the interest comes from geographically.
  • Pricing — what you pay against the live market spread, with per-product price monitoring.
  • Competitors — businesses in the same market, matched from your CRM by activity code (ΚΑΔ) and discovered live.
  • Financial — what they have billed you, what you have paid, and invoices from them still sitting unbooked in your myDATA inbox.

These are keyed to the supplier’s company record, so a product counts towards them once it is attributed to that company — on import, or via the Factory Link pin on the company’s Details tab.

Demand here originates from how the rest of the platform interacts with products — see Discover for the buyer-side experience that generates these signals.
