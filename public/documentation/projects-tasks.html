Projects · Tasks, board & My tasks

/projects/:id?tab=tasks /projects/:id?tab=tasks&task=:taskId /projects/my-tasks

A project's work is a set of tasks — with subtasks, an assignee, a priority, dates and a discussion. You can look at the same tasks four ways (List, Board, Calendar, Schedule), open any one of them in a task drawer to see and change everything about it, and see everything on your plate across every project on one My tasks page.

Where: open a project → the Delivery stage → Tasks. My tasks is the button on the Projects list header, and a shortcut under Projects Hub in the Apps menu.

Four views of the same tasks

What it is — a view switcher at the top of the Tasks section. Why it matters — a list is best for writing tasks down, a board for moving work along, a calendar for seeing what falls due when, and the schedule for a timeline. They are four views of one set of tasks, so a change in one shows in all of them.

ViewWhat it shows
ListTasks and their subtasks, with quick add (“Add a task…”, “Add a subtask…”).
BoardOne column per status — To do · In progress · Blocked · Done. Drag a card to another column to change its status (“Drop a task here”). From the keyboard, Space picks a card up and Enter opens it.
CalendarA month grid placing each task on its due date (or its end date when it has no due date), with previous/next month arrows. Booked visits & meetings for the project appear on it too.
ScheduleThe Gantt timeline of start and end dates — see Programme.

The task drawer

What it is — click any task, in any view, and it opens in a drawer at the side. The drawer has its own link (?tab=tasks&task=…), so you can paste it to a colleague and it opens straight on that task. Why it matters — before, a task could not be opened at all; now everything about it is in one place.

  • Properties — Status, Priority (Urgent · High · Medium · Low, or Not set), Assignee (a workspace member or an HR employee, or Unassigned), Room, Start and Due dates, a Milestone switch and a Client visible switch (off by default — an internal task is never shown to the client).
  • Description — “What needs doing, and what does done look like?”
  • Subtasks — add and tick off subtasks; a subtask's drawer says which task it belongs to.
  • Time logged — the hours booked against the task, with a Log time button. Those hours feed the crew strip on the project's cost panel.
  • Comments — the task's discussion (below).

Mandatory steps still apply. If a task has mandatory steps outstanding, moving it to Done is refused with “Mandatory steps outstanding” — whether you do it from the list, the board, the calendar or the drawer.

Comments on a task

What it is — a discussion thread inside each task (“Start the discussion — a question, or a note for whoever picks this up”). It is marked internal, never shown to the client: a client you invited never sees it. You can delete your own comments.

Who is told — when someone comments, the task's assignee, the project owner and everyone who commented earlier get a notification — never the person who wrote it, and only people still on the project team. It is one of the platform's default notifications, so a workspace owner can switch it off or silence one channel in Automations → Platform defaults (Task Commented).

Priority

Every task can carry a priority — Urgent, High, Medium or Low — shown as a coloured tag on the card and in the list. Leave it unset when it doesn't matter; Not set is a real answer, not a default of Medium.

My tasks — everything on your plate

What it is — one page, /projects/my-tasks, titled “Everything on your plate, across every project.” Why it matters — if you work on several jobs, you no longer open each project to find out what is yours.

  • Two scopes. All my work — tasks assigned to you, plus unassigned tasks in projects you run. Assigned to me — only tasks someone gave you.
  • Grouped by when. Overdue · Today · Tomorrow · Next 7 days · Later · No date, each task showing its project.
  • Hide done / Show done keeps finished tasks out of the way.
  • Change status from here. Each row has a status picker, so you move your tasks along without opening the project. Clicking a task in a project you own opens it in that project's task drawer; a task you were only assigned opens its discussion right on this page.

An empty page reads “Nothing on your plate”, with a link back to Open Projects.

Who can see and change tasks

  • The project owner and the project team (managers and members) work on every task.
  • A workspace member who is not on the team but has been assigned a task can read and comment on that one task, and move its status from My tasks.
  • A client you invited sees only tasks marked Client visible, and never the comments.

Worked example. On the Board, drag “Order worktop” from To do to In progress. Open it, set Priority to High, assign it to Maria and write “Supplier says 3 weeks — confirm the template date” in the comments. Maria gets a notification, finds the task under Today on her My tasks page, and replies in the same thread — and the owner is told about her reply.
