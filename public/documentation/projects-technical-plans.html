Projects · Technical Plans

/projects/:id · Sheets tab

Technical plans are the services drawings for a project — electrical, plumbing and lighting layouts. You draw the plan on a floor-plan backdrop by dropping standardised symbols and connecting them with runs (circuits, pipes). Because every symbol can be linked to a real product and every run can be measured, the sheet stops being a picture and becomes something you can order from and check: it yields a schedule (a counted material list) and, for electrical, a voltage-drop check — then exports to a titled, branded PDF. This page covers building one end to end, and — importantly — where the tool deliberately refuses to give you a number it can't stand behind.

Not the same as “Plan & Estimating.” The Projects area has two things with “plan” in the name. The Plan & Estimating tab is the costing engine (measurements → priced bill of quantities). Technical Plans — this page — are drawings on the Sheets tab. They share nothing but the word.

Where it is

Open a project (/projects/:id) → the Sheets tab. Sheets are split into two groups: Technical plans (owned by the project — electrical, plumbing, lighting) and the presentation sheets that came from the project's moodboards. Click New technical plan to start one. (Any project member can open the tab; creating a sheet is owner-only.)

Building a plan — the two phases

A technical plan is created in a short wizard, then drawn on a canvas.

  1. Wizard. Choose the plan type (Electrical, Plumbing or Lighting), then pick a backdrop (below). This creates a draft — you're charged for it here (3 credits), and the draft opens on the canvas so you never pay for a sheet you can't then draw.
  2. Canvas. The drawing surface: place symbols, connect runs, link products, fill the title block, and render the PDF.

The backdrop — and why it decides what can be measured

This is the single most important choice, because it's what gives the drawing scale. You have three options:

BackdropWhat it isMeasurable?
Blank room (mm)A plain rectangle drawn from width × length you type in millimetres.Yes — real dimensions, so run lengths and voltage-drop compute.
A project roomOne-click backdrop from a room that already has dimensions set.Yes — inherits the room's millimetres.
Uploaded floor planAn image of an existing drawing.No — an image carries no scale, so lengths and checks stay blank (see the honesty note below).

If you want lengths and voltage-drop checks, use a sized backdrop — Blank room (mm) or a project room with dimensions. An uploaded floor-plan image is fine for laying out symbols, but the tool won't invent a scale for it, so run lengths show “—” and the electrical checks are omitted. (You can give a room real dimensions — or calibrate an uploaded plan by drawing a line of known length — on the Rooms tab.)

On the canvas

The canvas has a backdrop, a symbol palette, a legend that fills itself as you place symbols, a runs list, and a title block editor. You work in three explicit modes:

  • Place — click the backdrop to drop a symbol from the palette; drag to reposition.
  • Select — click a placed symbol to edit its label and link it to a product.
  • Connect — draw a run between two symbols (click the first, click empty space to add corners so the run follows walls, click the second to finish; Esc cancels).

The electrical palette includes sockets (single/double), switches (1-way, 2-way, dimmer), the distribution board, data/RJ45, TV/SAT, dedicated points, junction boxes and earth points — drawn as standard IEC-style glyphs in the PDF. Plumbing and lighting have their own symbol sets.

Symbols → products → a schedule

In select mode, a symbol can be linked to a product from your catalogue (search by name). A linked symbol shows a green dot, so across forty sockets you can see which still need a product. When you render, the PDF counts symbols per linked product and prints a SCHEDULE — qty × product name, most-used first — plus a note of how many symbols aren't yet linked. That schedule is a real take-off you can order from; it's the whole reason a symbol carries a product.

Runs

A run is the primitive that connects two symbols — a lighting or socket circuit, a dedicated line, a switch leg (electrical), or cold/hot/waste (plumbing). Runs are what let the sheet compute anything: on a sized backdrop the runs list shows each run's length in metres and a total. On an unscaled (image) backdrop it shows “—” and tells you to use room dimensions — it will not print a length it can't measure, because a confident wrong number in front of someone about to buy cable is worse than none.

Voltage-drop checks — and an honest refusal

For an electrical plan on a sized backdrop, each run has a small check: enter the design current (A), the cable size (mm²) and pick single- or three-phase, and the sheet computes the voltage drop from first principles (Ohm's law, copper at 70 °C) and marks it pass/fail against the conventional 3 % (lighting) / 5 % (power) limits. The same check re-derives in the PDF's CHECKS block.

What it will not do — by design. The tool computes only what it can derive honestly. It deliberately does not size cables or pipes, apply diversity factors, or quote current-carrying capacities — those need transcribed standards tables, and a fabricated table would produce an authoritative-looking wrong number. Every coefficient it does use (voltage, resistivity, the 3 %/5 % limits) is a stated default you can see, and the checks carry the basis line “conventional limits — not a substitute for design sign-off.” Nothing here replaces a qualified engineer's sign-off.

Title block & branding

Every sheet carries a title block at the foot of the PDF. On the canvas you set Prepared by, Checked by, a revision (e.g. “A”) and the issue date. Sensible defaults fill only the blanks — your name into Prepared by, today into the date — so a sheet someone else prepared is never silently re-attributed, and the date is stored (not stamped fresh) so re-printing an issued drawing doesn't move its date. The block also shows the project, client, sheet type, and your studio branding (company name, contact line and logo) pulled from your business identity.

Export

Click Render PDF — the canvas saves your work and generates the sheet: the plan area (backdrop with runs drawn under symbols), then a right-hand column with the LEGEND, a SERVICES key of the run types used, the electrical CHECKS, and the SCHEDULE, over the title block. The PDF can be shared via a link, and is rebuilt on demand if a storage-retention sweep has purged the file.

Cost

Each technical plan (electrical / plumbing / lighting) costs 3 credits, charged when the draft is created and refunded automatically if creation or rendering fails. Drawing, editing and re-rendering the same sheet don't cost more.

Worked example — an electrical layout you can order and check. Open the project → Sheets → New technical plan → Electrical → pick a project room (so it's scaled). On the canvas, place sockets, switches and the DB; in select mode link each to a catalogue product; switch to connect and draw the socket and lighting circuits back to the board. For each circuit enter the current and cable size to get a voltage-drop verdict. Fill Prepared by / Checked by / Rev, then Render PDF — you get the drawing, a counted SCHEDULE to order from, the voltage-drop CHECKS, and your branded title block.
