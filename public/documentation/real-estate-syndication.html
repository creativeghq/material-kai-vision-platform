Real Estate · Portal syndication

/properties Estate Agent

Syndication is how your listings leave MaterialsHub and appear on external property portals — the aggregator sites where buyers search. Instead of re-typing every property into each portal by hand, you expose your live inventory in a format the portal understands, and the portal ingests it automatically. It lives in its own dedicated “Portal syndication feed” tab inside the Real Estate workspace.

Where it is

Open /properties and choose the Portal syndication feed tab, alongside Overview, Listings, Leads, Buyers, Viewings and Sales. Syndication used to sit on the Overview dashboard; it was moved out into its own tab so the feed configuration has room of its own and doesn't clutter the agent dashboard.

What syndication actually does

A portal feed is a machine-readable export of your properties. You turn the feed on, pick the format the destination portal expects, and MaterialsHub renders your listings into that format. Two things are worth being clear about up front:

  • Only published, public listings are included. The feed respects each listing's visibility toggles — the same “Open for all” / public-listings switches described in Listings & workbench. A property you're still drafting, or one you've deliberately kept internal, never leaves the building. Publish a listing and it joins the feed; unpublish it and it drops out on the next fetch.
  • You choose one format per feed. Different portals speak different “languages”. You pick the one your target portal asks for — see the table below.

The three feed formats

The feed format (the feed_format setting) determines the shape of the exported XML. Match it to what the destination portal documents as its accepted import format:

Which one? Let the destination decide — every portal's import documentation names the format it accepts. If you're targeting a DACH portal, use OpenImmo; for a broad international portal, Kyero is the usual fit; when neither is mandated, Generic XML is the safe general-purpose export.

Two directions: pull vs push

Syndication works in two directions, and it helps to know which one your portal uses.

PULL — a tokenized feed URL the portal fetches

In the pull model, MaterialsHub exposes your live listings as a feed URL and the portal (or aggregator) pulls that URL on its own schedule to ingest whatever is currently published. This is the most common shape: you hand the portal one link, and it re-reads it periodically, so your inventory stays in sync without you re-uploading anything.

  • The feed is switched on with feed_enabled. Turn it on to make the URL live; turn it off to stop serving the feed entirely.
  • The URL is protected by a feed_token. The feed URL carries a token, so the address is private — it works only for the portals you deliberately give it to, rather than being an open, guessable link. Give the tokenized URL to a portal and it can fetch; keep it to yourself and nobody else can read your inventory.
  • Always current. Because the portal pulls the live feed, each fetch reflects your latest published set — new listings appear, sold/unpublished ones fall away.

PUSH — OpenImmo for the DACH market

The OpenImmo (DACH) format is a push-style syndication format for the German-speaking market — it's the format used to deliver listings to those portals rather than having them poll a URL. If your destination is a DACH portal, this is the format you select.

Turning it on — the shape of the setup

  1. Open Real Estate → Portal syndication feed.
  2. Choose the feed format — OpenImmo, Kyero or Generic XML — to match the portal you're targeting.
  3. Enable the feed (feed_enabled) so it starts serving.
  4. For a pull feed, take the tokenized feed URL (it includes the feed_token) and give it to the portal to register on their side.
  5. Confirm your listings are published and public — only those appear. Anything internal stays out of the feed.

Keep the feed token private. The token is what keeps your feed URL from being a public, guessable link — treat it like a shared secret and only hand it to portals you actually want ingesting your inventory. If a URL has been shared too widely, disabling the feed stops it serving.

Syndication is not the same as Discovery. Publishing a listing already makes it visible on the platform's own Discovery surface and your public agency page. Portal syndication is the separate step of pushing that same published inventory out to external third-party portals. The two are independent — you can publish to Discovery without syndicating, and syndication draws from the very listings you've made public.
