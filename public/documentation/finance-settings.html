Finance Settings

/finance finance.manage

Finance Settings is where you configure the whole money side of your business before you issue your first document. It is one tab on the Finance page, but it opens into twelve sub-sections. This guide walks every one of them: what it is, why it matters, exactly how to fill it in, and what it changes elsewhere in the platform.

How to open it: top navigation → Finance → the Settings tab. A second row of tabs appears — those are the twelve sub-sections below.

Recommended setup order (each step builds on the last): Business Identity → Documents → e-Invoicing → Categories → Pricing → Services → General defaults → Team. Online Store, Statement PDF and Finance Digest can wait until you're live.

1. Business Identity

What it is — the legal details of the business that issues your invoices: name, VAT number, tax office, registered address and contact details. Why it matters — every invoice and receipt prints these details, and they are sent to the Greek tax authority (myDATA) on each submission. If they're wrong, your documents are legally wrong. This is the very first thing to set up.

The section is split into 1. Billing, 2. Customer Contact, 3. Personal, 4. My Company and Bank & Logo, and every text field is bilingual — use the EN / GR toggle to fill each language (the right one prints depending on the document's language).

How to set it up

  1. Toggle to GR (for Greek businesses) and type your VAT number (e.g. EL123456789).
  2. Click Fetch from ΑΑΔΕ — the platform pulls your official Company name, Activity, Tax office and registered address straight from the tax registry, so you don't type them by hand (and they exactly match what the authority expects).
  3. Fill anything ΑΑΔΕ didn't return: GEMI number, Phone, Fax, Email, and the Bank & Logo (IBAN + the logo that prints on documents).
  4. Switch to EN and add English versions if you invoice international clients.
  5. Save.

What it affects: the header/footer of every PDF document, the issuer block sent to myDATA, and your public statements and storefront.

2. Documents

What it is — how your documents are numbered, branded and exchanged with the tax authority. Why it matters — Greek law requires each document type to run on its own numbered series, and the look of the PDF is your brand in the customer's inbox.

  • Establishments — register each physical premises/branch (Add establishment). myDATA tags documents with the establishment that issued them.
  • Document Types & Series — define the numbering series for each document type (invoices, receipts, etc.). This is what produces the legal sequential number when you press Issue.
  • Invoice design template — pick a template (e.g. Classic) and set the brand Colors: Accent, Header background, Header text, Table header, Text, Secondary text, and Lines/borders. Save template, or Reset to defaults.
  • EFT-POS Terminals — register card terminals (Add terminal) so card payments are tied to issued documents.
  • myDATA Inbox (Received Documents) — to pull documents others issued to you, enter your myDATA REST credentials: User ID (sent as aade-user-id) and Subscription Key (Ocp-Apim-Subscription-Key). This powers the "Sync from myDATA" buttons on the Invoices/Expenses tabs.

How to set it up

  1. Add at least one Establishment (your main premises).
  2. Create a series for each document type you'll issue.
  3. Open Invoice design template, set your brand colours, Save template.
  4. If you want incoming documents to appear automatically, paste your myDATA User ID + Subscription Key.

What it affects: document numbering and branding on every document you create, and the Sync from myDATA feature.

3. e-Invoicing (Novus → myDATA)

What it is — the on/off status of electronic transmission to the tax authority through the Novus connector. Why it matters — until this is active, documents are created but not electronically filed. How: set the Status (it starts at Not requested) as you progress the activation, add a Note (contract number, date submitted, who handled it) for your own audit trail, and Save status.

What it affects: whether Submit to myDATA on issue actually transmits — see Greek e-invoicing for the full transmission flow and VAT validation.

4. Categories

What it is — the income and expense buckets you tag documents with. Why it matters — categories are how your Reports (and the VAT summary, sales/purchases journals) break revenue and cost down — without them every figure is one undifferentiated lump.

How to add a category

  1. Click Import default categories first — it seeds a sensible standard set so you're not starting blank.
  2. To add your own, type a Name (e.g. Materials, Rent, Services).
  3. Choose the Kind: income, expense, or Both (use Both for a label that appears on either side, like a project name).
  4. Optionally set a default margin % for the category.
  5. Add it.

What it affects: the Income category / Expense category pickers on documents, the category filters on the Receivables/Payables/Invoices lists, and every report breakdown.

5. Pricing

What it is — automatic markup rules per product category. Why it matters — when you pull a catalogue item onto a document, you usually want to sell it above cost; a rule converts cost to sell price automatically so you don't recalculate every line.

How to set a rule

  1. Pick a product Category.
  2. Enter the Markup % (e.g. 25).
  3. Add the rule. Repeat per category.

What it affects: the price proposed when you Pick from products on an invoice/quote. A blanket default also lives in General → Default catalog markup %; category rules override it.

6. Services

What it is — your billable, non-material lines: labour, delivery, installation, consultation. Why it matters — services need a name and price so they can be added to documents and rung up at the till exactly like products. How: click New service, name it and set a price.

What it affects: services become pickable on invoices/quotes and, importantly, in the POS — items only appear in the register if they have a price, and services are priced here.

7. Team

What it is — who can work in this workspace, and at what role. Why it matters — roles decide which areas a person sees (CRM, Finance, Sales, etc.), so this is how you bring colleagues in safely. This is the platform's team-invitation surface.

How to invite a team member

  1. Open Team — it lists current Members.
  2. Under Invite Team Member, choose the Role to grant.
  3. Click Generate invite link (under Invite & referral links).
  4. Send the link to your colleague; when they accept they join this workspace with the role you chose.

What it affects: who appears in CRM → Users and what each member can access platform-wide (see Roles & access).

8. General

What it is — the defaults applied to every new document, plus the safety rules that protect you at issue time. Why it matters — good defaults save you re-typing on every invoice, and the rules stop you issuing documents that will be rejected or never paid. Set this after Business Identity/Documents so the defaults make sense.

Defaults

  • Default email subject / body — pre-fill what's sent when you email a document.
  • Default payment terms (days) — sets the due date offset on new invoices.
  • Default VAT % — the VAT applied to new line items.
  • Default catalog markup % — fallback markup when no category Pricing rule applies.

Issuance & credit-limit safety rules

These are toggles — turn them on once you're trading seriously:

  • Block issuance when the buyer VAT number is ΑΑΔΕ-inactive — stops you invoicing a VAT number the registry shows as inactive (a common cause of rejected myDATA submissions).
  • Block issuance when the buyer VAT was never validated (stricter) — forces every buyer's VAT to be validated before you can issue.
  • Warn when the invoice pushes the buyer over their credit limit — shows a warning (the credit limit is set per customer in CRM).
  • Block (instead of warn) when over the credit limit — turns that warning into a hard stop.

Click Save settings to apply. What it affects: every new document, and the checks that run when you press Issue.

9. Online Store

What it is — the public storefront customers can browse at /store/:slug. Why it matters — it turns your priced catalogue into a shareable shop. How: set the Headline and Subheadline, confirm the product search, and Save store settings. What it affects: the public store page; only priced products appear.

10. Statement PDF

What it is — the branding of the account-statement PDF you email to a customer (the running total of what they owe). Why it matters — a branded statement looks professional when chasing payment. How: upload a Cover / page backdrop and an optional Footer image. What it affects: the statement generated from Customers & Suppliers (click a party → email statement).

11. Finance Digest

What it is — a recurring email summary of your finances. Why it matters — it keeps you (or your accountant) on top of cash flow without logging in. How: set the Frequency (e.g. Weekly), Day of week, Hour (UTC, 0–23) and one or more Recipients (Add), then Save digest settings. Use Send test to preview it, or Send now to recipients to dispatch immediately.

12. Payments

What it is — how customers can pay you online. Why it matters — it controls the payment methods/links offered on the public pay page (/pay/:token) that a customer opens from an emailed invoice. What it affects: the options a customer sees when settling a document online.

Once Business Identity, Documents, e-Invoicing, Categories and Services are done, you can issue compliant invoices and run the POS end-to-end. Everything else here is polish you can add as you grow.
