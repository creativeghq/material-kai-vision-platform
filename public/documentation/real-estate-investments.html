Real Estate · Investments Pro add-on

/real-estate

Investments is an investment-analysis tool for property: you describe a deal — what you'd pay for it, what it costs to get in, how it's financed and what rent it earns — and it works out whether the numbers stack up. It computes the standard property-investment returns (yields, cap rate, cash-on-cash and monthly cash flow) for each property, then rolls every property you've modelled into a single portfolio view. It answers "is this a good buy, and how is my whole portfolio doing?" without a spreadsheet.

Where: Investments is a sibling paid add-on to Real Estate — a separate Pro-tier module (manifest name "Investments", parent "real-estate"). Enable it from the App Launcher; it then surfaces as an extra Investments tab inside the Real Estate workspace at /real-estate. It requires the Real Estate module — it extends that workspace rather than standing alone.

How it fits

Real Estate ships with two sibling add-ons. Property Management handles lettings — the actual rent you collect, tenancies and statements. Investments is the other one, and it works the other way round: it's forward-looking analysis, modelling the expected returns of a deal before (or while) you hold it. Enabling either one adds its own tab to the same Real Estate workspace; you can run both together, use the modelled rent in Investments alongside the real rent tracked in Property Management, and feed real outcomes into your books through Finance.

The inputs you provide

For each property you model, you fill in a set of investment inputs. These are the assumptions the whole analysis is built on — change any of them and every metric below recalculates.

InputWhat it is
Purchase priceWhat you pay for the property — the headline acquisition figure the returns are measured against.
CostsThe extra money needed to acquire and ready the property — for example acquisition costs and renovation costs — on top of the purchase price.
FinancingHow the deal is funded — the loan / mortgage terms (the borrowed amount and its cost), which determine how much cash you actually put in and the loan repayments that eat into cash flow.
RentThe expected rental income the property generates — the return side of the equation.

The metrics it computes

From those inputs, Investments derives the standard property-investment return measures. Each looks at the deal from a slightly different angle, which is why it computes several rather than one — a property can look strong on one and weak on another.

MetricWhat it tells you
Gross yieldAnnual rent as a percentage of the purchase price, before any costs — the quick, headline return.
Net yieldAnnual rent as a percentage of the total invested, after costs are taken into account — a truer picture than gross yield.
Cap rateThe capitalisation rate — the property's income return relative to its value, the standard way to compare one property's earning power against another.
Cash-on-cash returnThe return measured against the actual cash you put in (after financing), so a leveraged deal reads differently from an all-cash one.
Monthly cash flowWhat's left each month once rent has covered the loan repayments and running costs — positive means the property pays you, negative means it costs you to hold.

Why several metrics? Gross yield flatters a deal because it ignores costs; net yield corrects for them. Cap rate lets you compare properties like-for-like. Cash-on-cash tells you how hard your own money is working once the mortgage is factored in. And monthly cash flow is the reality check — a property with a great cap rate can still bleed cash each month if the financing is heavy. Reading them together is the point.

The portfolio roll-up

Modelling one property answers "is this a good buy?". The portfolio (InvestmentPortfolio) answers the bigger question — "how is my whole property portfolio performing?" — by aggregating every property you've modelled into one portfolio-level view. Instead of checking each deal in isolation, you see your holdings together, so you can judge overall performance and spot which properties are pulling their weight and which aren't.

Modelled, not actual. Investments works from the assumptions you enter — expected rent and estimated costs — so its returns are projections, only as accurate as the numbers you feed it. For the rent you actually collect, tenancies and the rent ledger, use the Property Management add-on; for money that has genuinely moved through your books, use Finance.
