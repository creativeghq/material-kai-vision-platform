Warehouse Pro add-on

/warehouse warehouse.manage

The Warehouse module (formerly “Stock Management”) is your real inventory back-office: what you hold and where, what it's worth, every movement, storage locations and putaway, physical and year-end counts, landed-cost, supplier rebates, ageing write-downs, goods intake, inbound tracking, an AI resupply forecast and dispatch. It grew from a simple stock list into a proper warehouse system, so a business that lives by inventory has a home for the parts a general ledger skips. This page covers each tab, plus the two shipping integrations — ShipsGo tracking (BYOK) and SeaRates quotes.

Where: enable Warehouse from the App Launcher (Pro add-on) and open it at /warehouse; it needs the warehouse.manage capability. Everything is per workspace. (Inventory once lived under Finance → Warehouse and then a “Stock” module — same data, now this dedicated home.) The tab strip runs: Overview · Inventory · Locations · Valuation · Resupply · Inbound · Dispatch · Movements · Stock Counts.

Overview — what you hold

An at-a-glance health panel: Stock items, Warehouses, Units on hand, Units reserved, plus the numbers that need action — Low stock, Out of stock, Open counts and Pending intake. When all's well it reads “Nothing below its reorder point. Inventory is healthy.”

Movements — receive, issue, adjust, transfer

What it is — the ledger of every change to stock; on-hand is only trustworthy because every in and out is recorded here. Each movement records the item, quantity, a reason and a source:

  • Receive — stock coming in (a supplier, an inbound container).
  • Issue — stock going out (sold, consumed).
  • Adjust — a correction (breakage, a count difference).
  • Transfer — moving stock between your warehouses.

Each movement now also carries its cost: a receipt lands at the purchase-line cost, and an issue is stamped with the valuation in force at that instant — so cost of sales is a recorded fact, not a figure re-derived later.

Valuation — what your stock is worth

The Valuation tab answers “what is my inventory worth, and what did the goods I sold cost me?” — with figures you can trust because each is derived from the movement ledger, never a hand-typed total.

Stock value & cost of goods sold

The Stock value and cost of sales card shows the current Inventory value and the Cost of goods sold across a date range you pick. A figure the platform can only value in part reads “(part of the warehouse)” in amber with the reason; a figure it can't value at all shows as “—”, never €0 — an unvalued item is stated, not counted as free.

The costing method — a dated setting

How stock is valued is a choice with a date, not a hidden default: Weighted average (μέση σταθμική, the default), FIFO, or Standard cost. Set the method and an “In force from” date and Record it; the history of methods is kept. Changing it re-values sales already invoiced under the old method, and the card says so before you do.

“Available” is the largest pool, not the sum

For materials sold in matched batches, the Lots, tones and calibres card makes an important distinction: available is the largest single homogeneous pool, never the total. The table breaks each item into Lot / Tone / Calibre / Grade with On hand and Free (free = on-hand − reserved), flags an opened pack, and warns when a SKU is split across several pools. On a quote line this surfaces as a note — “From one pool — lot … · tone …”, or an amber warning when the quantity would have to be met from several pools — so you don't promise a single-batch order you can't fill from one batch.

Landed cost — the import folder

What it is — the Import folders card (Valuation tab) apportions the freight, duty and clearance on a shipment across the products in it, so the heavy tile carries its real landed cost instead of everything sharing it evenly. The container is the allocation object — one folder, many purchase orders.

  • Create a folder with a reference and an Apportion by basis — weight (the default), volume, value, quantity or equal. A basis it can't measure (weight, when a line has no weight) is refused, not silently swapped.
  • Attach purchase orders, then add each cost with a kind (freight / duty / customs / clearance / demurrage / insurance / handling / inland) and a stage — Forecast or Actual invoice.
  • The allocation table shows the on-cost and per-unit added to every line, and an “actual against forecast” variance once the real invoices land.

Supplier rebates & stock ageing

Two more Valuation-tab cards handle the money a general ledger books too late:

  • Supplier rebates — a retrospective rebate is booked as an accrual as you earn it (not when the cheque arrives), with a “crossing the next band is worth X on the period to date” prompt. Raise the claim records it at the accrued amount; the claims table shows Settled as “—” until it actually clears.
  • Ageing and obsolescence — aged per lot, with a dated write-down policy you can adopt (e.g. 0% / 25% / 75% at 6 and 12 months) and a worst-pools table showing the provision. The leading signal is entered on the product itself: a “Discontinued by the supplier” date on the product's fiscal card.

Locations & putaway

What it is — the Locations tab holds your storage places — bins and the like — so stock has an address, not just a warehouse. Why it matters — a scannable, walkable warehouse is what makes picking fast and counts honest.

  • Each location has a Code, its own location barcode (distinct from an item's barcode), a walking path order, and a kind — bin, bulk, pick-face, staging, quarantine or van. Warnings count locations with no barcode (“cannot be scan-verified”) or no walking path (“a pick list cannot be ordered”).
  • “Where does this go?” previews putaway for an item, states the rule that chose the spot, and Make that the rule saves a fixed putaway rule (most specific wins: product beats category beats a blanket rule).

Intake — “this product exists” vs “this stock arrived”

The Inventory tab is where goods and paperwork come in. Its guiding rule: creating a product is not the same as receiving stock, and the platform refuses to conflate them.

  • The intake queue lets you set, per batch, whether to catalogue only — no stock, receive the stock too, or decide from the document date (a document within the last 30 days receives stock; an older one opens the product at zero, since a year-old delivery note is not stock arriving today). The bulk-confirm dialog names the mode, so “add all 431 lines” can't silently invent a year of inventory.
  • Added from intake is the trail of what was booked in — with Undo, which reverses the stock with a compensating movement but keeps the product.
  • Run enrichment fills in a product's attributes from a document it already holds and reports what it could not do (enriched / no source / low confidence / failed) rather than failing silently.
  • Names CRM doesn't know. When supplier, manufacturer or category names on the queue match nothing in CRM, a banner offers Resolve names → Propose matches: the assistant suggests a target as a question, and you Confirm, reject, or mint a new record. (Promoting a match to the platform-wide vocabulary is operator-only.)

Stock counts — stocktake & the year-end Απογραφή

What it is — physical counts reconciled against the system, on the Stock Counts tab.

A stocktake you can freeze

New count snapshots every item into a blind count sheet (system quantity hidden while you count). Freeze before counting captures what the system believed at one instant; a drift banner then reports anything that moved since the freeze, so a sale that happened mid-count is reconciled rather than blamed on the counter. Post count writes the differences as adjust movements. Counts in progress show as Open counts on the overview.

Απογραφή — the year-end count

The Απογραφή card records the legal year-end inventory (and its myDATA submission). It separates the counted-on date from the reference date (deliberately, per άρθρο 6 §2), and a roll-forward reconciles the movements between the two. Lines carry a quantity (with a per-storage-space breakdown), a unit value and a total; third-party stock is quantity-only and reads “not ours to value.” Note the panel records the count; line valuation and the actual AADE transmission happen server-side — there's no “Transmit” button here yet.

Resupply — forecast, container fill & commitments

Reorder points & the resupply bridge

Each item can carry a reorder point; when on-hand falls below it, it shows under Low stock. The reorder bridge turns a low-stock item straight into a purchase in the sourcing flow, without re-keying.

AI resupply forecast · credit-metered

A trend-based forecast of what to reorder and when: it reads your movement history and projects it, showing per item a Days cover, a stockout risk and a Recommendation. Because it runs an AI/analytics pass it's metered against your credits.

Container fill & commitments

  • Container fill — a weight-first packer that flags when a planned order is overweight (red) or has an unweighed line (amber), for a chosen container type; it doesn't consolidate across suppliers.
  • Commitments, holds and processing — five facts a general ERP keeps as bare integers: forward/blanket contracts (with an allowance and released-to-date), holds (reserved for a customer, with a mandatory expiry — nothing auto-releases), cutting/processing jobs (offcuts come back as their own pool), a supplier scorecard (on-time / quality / price-stability, weighted), and a price-file update that accepts one JSON row per line and rejects any row carrying more than a price.

Inbound container tracking (ShipsGo) BYOK

What it is — live tracking of shipments coming to you, by container or Bill of Lading, so you see where inbound stock is and when it'll land — then Receive it into a warehouse on arrival. Tracking uses ShipsGo and is bring-your-own-key.

In the module's shipping credentials card, enter your ShipsGo API key and the Base URL (default https://api.shipsgo.com/v2). Once Configured, add a shipment with its Container number (e.g. MSCU1234567) or Bill of Lading, an optional carrier/reference, and optionally link it to a purchase order; Refresh re-checks and Receive into warehouse books it in. Tracking refreshes are credit-metered.

Dispatch & freight quotes

  • Dispatch — the outbound tab for getting stock out to customers. It's read-only unless you also have finance.manage, since a dispatch is the start of a fiscal delivery note.
  • Freight quotes (SeaRates) — on a quote under Extras, Get shipping quote fetches freight offers and adds the chosen one as a line. This is operator-provided (you request, the operator answers) — no key of your own, unlike ShipsGo tracking.

Nearby — where a few warehouse jobs actually live

A handful of things that belong to a shipment or a customer are recorded where that record is, not in this module:

  • Who gets the pallet — a received purchase line is allocated to the waiting orders (oldest-confirmed-first) on the purchase order's Customs tab in Finance.
  • Delivery evidence & goods-receipt disputes — proof of delivery on a sales order, and a goods-receipt inspection (a rejection opens a supplier claim) on a purchase order, both on the order's Customs tab (needs finance.manage).
  • Samples out — the Samples out card on a CRM company tracks what a customer borrowed and whether it came back or converted.
  • Marketplace listing — list surplus/last stock straight to the Marketplace.

Day-to-day: watch the overview for Low stock → check the Resupply forecast for what's actually at risk → reorder via the bridge → track the inbound container (ShipsGo) and Receive into warehouse on arrival, apportioning its freight in an Import folder → keep it honest with a frozen stock count, and the Απογραφή at year end.
