MoodBoards

/moodboard /moodboard/:id

A MoodBoard is where a project starts to take shape. You gather materials, finishes and inspiration images into one board, let the platform suggest matching products, and then turn that board into something a client can hold: polished presentation sheets, a priced quote, or a shareable public page. This guide walks every part of the board — creating it, the Items and Tools tabs, the action toolbar, every presentation-sheet type, and sharing — explaining for each one what it is, why it matters, how to do it, and what it changes elsewhere in the platform.

How to open it: top navigation → MoodBoards. Click a board to open it, or New MoodBoard to create one.

The natural workflow (each step builds on the last): create a board → Add Products and/or Import from Pinterest → run Find Products to round it out → ask the Agent Hub to build presentation sheets → either Generate Quote for pricing or assemble the sheets into a project-level Client View for the final hand-off.

What a MoodBoard is for

What it is — a working collection: one place to gather the products and inspiration for a single room, project or concept. Why it matters — it is the bridge between the three things you do most on the platform — browsing the catalogue, chatting with the Agent Hub, and delivering a finished proposal. Everything you collect on a board can be re-used downstream: the products become a quote, the board becomes sheets, the sheets feed a Client View. How — you add catalogue materials, pull in images (from Pinterest or from the AI chat), and let the platform suggest complementary products; when the board is ready you render sheets, generate a quote, or publish it as a link. What it affects: for larger deliverables that combine sheets from several boards, MoodBoards feed into Projects → Client Views.

The list page

What it is — the home for every board you own. Why it matters — it is your overview of in-flight concepts at a glance, and the launch point for creating, opening, renaming and managing boards.

Each board appears as a card showing:

  • Name — what the board is called.
  • Visibility — a Public or Private badge that tells you at a glance whether the board can be opened by anyone with its link.
  • Item count — how many materials and images are on the board.

How to use it

  1. Click New MoodBoard to start a fresh board.
  2. From a card you can open it, rename it, change its visibility, or delete it.

What it affects: deleting a board removes it and its generated sheets — but it does not delete the underlying catalogue products, so the materials remain available for other boards and quotes.

The board detail page

What it is — the screen you reach when you open a board: two tabs (Items and Tools) plus an action toolbar. Why it matters — this is your workspace for the board — everything you do to build, enrich, price and publish a board happens here.

Items tab

What it is — the board itself: the materials and images you've collected, arranged as a visual grid. Why it matters — this is the canvas you keep coming back to — it's how you judge whether the board actually reflects the concept. How — review what's on the board, open an individual item to inspect it, arrange items, or remove ones you no longer want. What it affects: the products visible here are what feed Generate Quote and the product-based presentation sheets, so curating the Items tab directly shapes those deliverables.

Tools tab

What it is — board-level helpers. The key one is Find Products. Why it matters — a board often starts with a handful of items and a clear direction but gaps to fill; Find Products closes those gaps without manual searching. How — open the Tools tab and run Find Products; it looks across the catalogue for materials that match what's already on the board and surfaces complementary items you can add. What it affects: the Tools tab is also where you reach New Tool to start a presentation sheet (covered below), so it's the entry point for both enriching the board and producing its deliverables.

Add Products

What it is — the direct route to put catalogue materials on the board. Why it matters — the products you add are the raw material for everything downstream — a quote, a material board, an FF&E schedule all draw on them. How — open Add Products, search the catalogue, and add the materials you want. What it affects: every added product increases the board's item count, enables Generate Quote (which needs at least one product), and becomes available to the product-based sheet types.

Import from Pinterest

What it is — a way to pull inspiration images into the board from Pinterest. Why it matters — clients and designers often gather references on Pinterest first; this brings those references in and — crucially — connects them back to real, purchasable products. How — paste a single pin URL, or connect your Pinterest account to browse your boards and import images in bulk. What it affects: imported images are automatically matched against the catalogue, so the platform suggests the products that look closest — turning loose inspiration into candidate materials you can add to the board.

Find Products

What it is — the board-level catalogue matcher in the Tools tab. Why it matters — it rounds out a concept with complementary items so you don't have to guess what else pairs well. How — open the Tools tab and run Find Products; it reads what's already on the board and suggests matching catalogue materials. What it affects: suggestions you accept become board products — feeding the same downstream quote and sheets as anything you add manually.

Generate Quote

What it is — the action that converts the board's products into a quote. Why it matters — it is the moment a visual concept becomes a priced proposal — no re-entering products. How — once the board contains at least one product the button is enabled; click it to generate the quote from those products. What it affects: it creates a quote you then refine in the Quotes area; that quote can in turn supply the items for an FF&E schedule sheet, closing the loop between board, pricing and presentation.

Public / Private

What it is — the visibility toggle for the board. Why it matters — it decides whether the board is a private working space or a shareable reference. How — flip Public / Private. What it affects: a public board can be opened by anyone with its link at /board/:id (see Sharing below); a private board is visible only to you and your workspace.

Saving from the AI chat: when the Agent Hub returns a product or generated image you like, you can save it straight into a moodboard. A copy of the image is kept on the board, so it survives even if you later delete the conversation it came from.

Presentation sheets

What it is — polished, client-ready A3 pages produced from a board. Why it matters — sheets are how a board stops being an internal collection and becomes a deliverable a client understands — a material grid, a colour scheme, a lighting plan, a full deck. How — sheets are generated through the Agent Hub: you describe the sheet you want, the assistant assembles it, and the finished PDF is attached to the board so you can re-open and re-render it later. What it affects: each sheet is stored with the moodboard and can later be bundled into a project-level Client View. The table below is the overview; the next section gives the inputs in detail.

Interactive vs passive: some sheet types — Lighting plan, Annotated render and Elevation / render pair — open an in-chat canvas first. You place fixture symbols, draw dimensions, or position callouts directly on the image, and only then is the PDF rendered. You choose interactive types when the value is in where things sit on an image. The passive sheet types render immediately because their content is just a list or layout. The full list of what the assistant can build is in Tools & Capabilities.

Presentation sheet types — inputs in detail

What it is — the full reference for every sheet type. Why it matters — knowing each sheet's purpose, whether it is interactive, its credit cost and the inputs it expects lets you pick the right one for the job and prepare what you need before you start. How — open the moodboard's Tools tab and choose New Tool, or simply ask the assistant for the sheet you want. Passive sheets render to PDF immediately; Interactive sheets open an in-chat canvas so you can finish the placement before the PDF is built.

Use the type column to decide your approach. Reach for the passive sheets (Material board, Color palette, Concept board, FF&E schedule, Area breakdown, Full deck) when the content is a list, a layout or a roll-up that the assistant can assemble outright — they cost less or nothing and need no manual placement. Reach for the interactive sheets (Lighting plan, Annotated render, Elevation / render pair) when the whole point of the page is precise positioning on an image — where each light sits, which product a callout points to, where a dimension runs.

When to use each sheet

  • Material board — when the client just needs to see the chosen products together, with a word or two of context per item. The everyday "here's the palette of materials" page.
  • Color palette — when the colour scheme is the point: a clean strip of named swatches you can sample straight from images already on the board.
  • Concept board — early in a project, when you're selling a mood or story rather than specific products: a free-form image collage with captions.
  • Lighting plan (interactive) — when fixture positions matter: drop light symbols onto a backdrop and add a legend so an electrician or client reads the intent.
  • Annotated render (interactive) — when you want a render that explicitly points at the products in it, so the viewer knows exactly what they're looking at.
  • Elevation / render pair (interactive) — for the technical side-by-side: an elevation next to a render with dimensions and tile callouts you draw on.
  • FF&E schedule — when you need the formal furniture/fixtures/equipment table, typically pulled straight from a quote so pricing and the schedule agree.
  • Area breakdown — when one page must say everything about a room: hero render, plan, elevation, materials & finishes, fittings and a palette strip together.
  • Full deck — at hand-off, to combine several finished sheets behind a cover page into one PDF you can send as the proposal.

About the inputs: on interactive sheets every symbol, callout and dimension is placed relative to the backdrop image, so the canvas and the rendered PDF stay in sync whatever the image size. Every sheet outputs an A3 PDF stored with the moodboard, so you can re-open and re-render it later — and the same sheets can be bundled into a project-level deliverable via Projects → Client Views. The complete catalogue of what the assistant can build is in Tools & Capabilities.

Sharing & deliverables

What it is — the two ways a board reaches a client: a quick public link, or a richer Client View. Why it matters — they cover the two real situations — sending a fast visual reference, versus presenting a finished, branded proposal.

The quick link. A board you make Public can be opened by anyone with its link at /board/:id — handy for sending a quick visual reference to a client or colleague without giving them an account. How — use the Public / Private toggle, then copy the link.

The branded deliverable. When you need something richer that bundles sheets across multiple boards — with an embedded 3D walkthrough, lighting moods, a live FF&E table and inline client approval — use a Client View. What it affects: Client Views live at the project level, so they pull together sheets from any of a project's boards; see Projects → Client Views.

Tip: build the board first, then ask the Agent Hub to generate the sheets you need, then either Generate Quote for pricing or assemble the sheets into a Client View for the final hand-off.

Step by step: create a board and add materials

  1. From the top navigation open MoodBoards, then click New MoodBoard.
  2. Enter a title for the board (and an optional description), then confirm.
  3. Open the new board to reach its detail page with the Items and Tools tabs.
  4. Use Add Products to search the catalogue and add materials to the board.
  5. Optionally use Import from Pinterest to bring in images — they are auto-matched to catalogue products — or open the Tools tab and run Find Products to surface complementary materials.
  6. Arrange the items on the Items tab until the board reflects your concept.

Step by step: produce a presentation sheet

  1. From the board, choose a sheet type — for example a material board, colour palette, lighting plan, FF&E schedule or full deck.
  2. The Agent Hub drives the sheet: describe what you want and the assistant assembles it.
  3. For an interactive sheet, complete the in-chat canvas — place fixture symbols, draw dimensions, or position callouts directly on the image.
  4. The finished sheet renders as a PDF attached to the board, ready to re-open and re-render later. See Tools & Capabilities for the full list of sheets the assistant can build.

Step by step: share a board

  1. Set the board to Public using the Public / Private toggle.
  2. Copy its share link at /board/:id and send it to your client or colleague.
  3. For a richer, project-level deliverable, bundle sheets across boards into a Projects → Client View, or turn the board's products into a quote.
