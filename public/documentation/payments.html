Payment Providers

/finance → Settings → Accounts & Payments finance.manage · owner to activate

Payment providers are the third-party integrations that move money into (and, with Revolut, out of) your workspace. Three are supported — Stripe, Viva.com and Revolut — and each connects your own account, so funds settle directly to you, never through the platform. Whatever you connect and enable is what a customer is offered on the public pay page; whichever method they use, the invoice is reconciled through one shared money path, so a card payment and a bank transfer book the same way. This page compares the three and explains how they're gated; each has its own page for the deep setup.

Where you set them up: Finance → Settings → Accounts & Payments → Payment providers. Each provider is a separate Pro add-on — only a workspace owner can activate one; other finance managers can request it.

On the public pay page the enabled methods are offered in a fixed order — Stripe, then Viva, then Revolut — with the first preselected, and Direct bank transfer last when you offer it (see below). A customer never needs a MaterialsHub account; they follow the token link from their invoice email — or land there straight after a storefront checkout — and pay.

Revolut is two things. Under this category, Revolut covers both Revolut Checkout (a customer payment method, above) and Revolut Business — your own bank account, with a transaction feed, reconciliation, money-out and team cards. Stripe and Viva are checkout-only; Revolut also does your banking.

How a provider becomes usable

A provider is only offered to your customers once three things are true — a deliberate ladder so a half-set-up provider never shows a broken pay button:

  1. Published — the operator has the provider enabled platform-wide (a global kill-switch).
  2. Enabled — your workspace owner has activated the add-on for your workspace.
  3. Connected — you've actually completed the provider's setup (Connect onboarding for Stripe; credentials + a verified webhook for Viva and Revolut).

Turn every method off and the provider simply drops from the customer's choices. Each provider re-checks all three gates at the moment money moves, so a client can never trigger a charge through a provider you haven't fully set up.

What they settle

Every provider writes into the same payment ledger through one shared routine — so a Stripe card payment, a Viva RF bank transfer and a Revolut Pay charge all book identically against the invoice, allocate correctly, and never over-collect. Refunds and disputes are flagged to owners/admins, never auto-reversed — you settle them with a credit note, keeping the books deliberate.

The pay page — what the customer sees

The public pay page at /pay/:token reads as an order confirmation. After a storefront checkout it opens with "Thank you — we have received your order." It shows the Order number (or Invoice number), date, customer, total and what's left to pay; a Payment panel (Deposit when one applies, Pay in full or Another amount, then the method); Order details — every line with its discount, the net amount, VAT and any withholding, deductions, fees, stamp duty or other taxes; and, once the document is transmitted, a Filed with ΑΑΔΕ (myDATA) panel with the number, MARK, UID, authentication code and issue time, plus Filed copy (your e-invoicing provider's copy) and Verify at ΑΑΔΕ links. A cancelled or credited document says so, with nothing to pay. If no method is set up, the customer reads "Online payment isn't available yet".

Direct bank transfer & transfer receipts

What it is — alongside the card providers, the pay page offers Direct bank transfer whenever you have an active bank account marked Show on invoice in the document's currency. No provider is needed. Why it matters — many Greek buyers pay by transfer; this gives them your details in a copy-ready form and lets them hand you the proof, so you can confirm the payment without chasing it.

  1. The customer picks Direct bank transfer. The Bank transfer details panel shows the beneficiary, IBAN, BIC / SWIFT, the Reference (the order number) and the Amount — each with a copy button.
  2. After transferring, they use Upload proof of payment: drag in or browse to the receipt (PDF, JPG, PNG, WEBP or HEIC, up to 5 MB), add an optional note and click Send proof of payment.
  3. The page then shows each receipt's state — Checking…, Receipt checked — waiting for the bank, Received — the seller will review it, Payment confirmed or Not accepted. The customer never sees the checks themselves.

The automatic check — every uploaded receipt is read by AI (billed to your workspace's credits) and compared with the order: amount (the due amount or the deposit), currency, paid to one of your IBANs, beneficiary, reference, date (after the order), that the transfer was executed, and visible signs of editing. The verdict is Matches the order, Needs review, Not a transfer receipt or Unreadable. Owners and admins get a notification carrying the verdict.

Confirming it — on the invoice's page, the Bank transfer receipts card lists each receipt with what was read and every check, and offers View, Confirm payment, Check again and Reject. Confirm payment opens Confirm bank transfer, prefilled from the receipt (amount, date received, the matching account); Record payment books it. If that pays a storefront order in full, the order's draft document is issued (numbered and transmitted) before the payment is applied, so a paid order always has a real document. Recording twice is safe — a retry returns the payment already recorded.

A receipt is evidence, not money. A receipt can be edited, so confirm once you see the money in your bank. Nothing is booked from a receipt on its own: it books only through Confirm payment, or when the bank feed matches the transfer — a transfer quoting the order number now matches a storefront order, and the receipt only ranks the suggestion, never settles it automatically.

Retention — once the invoice is paid, cancelled or credited and its order is fulfilled or cancelled, the receipt file is deleted overnight and the payer name and IBAN read off it are blanked. The receipt entry and its checks stay; the card shows "File deleted <date>, after the order completed", and it can no longer be re-checked.

Pick by what you need: want the widest card reach with the least setup → Stripe; a Greek merchant who wants card and auto-reconciling bank transfers → Viva; already bank with Revolut and want your account, cards and reconciliation in one place → Revolut. You can enable more than one; the customer chooses at checkout.
