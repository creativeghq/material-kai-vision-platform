Documents · Orders

/finance finance.manage

An Order is the spine of a deal. Instead of tracking a quote, an invoice, payments and a delivery as separate, disconnected documents, an order ties them together: invoices, payments, dispatch and profit all hang off the one order. Open an order and you see, in one place, what was sold, what's been invoiced, what's been paid, what's left to deliver, and what you actually made on it. This page explains what orders are, the two kinds, their statuses, and how to create and work one.

Where: top navigation → Finance → the Orders tab (the first tab in the Documents area). A company's own orders also appear on its CRM record under the Account tab.

Sales orders vs purchase orders

Every order is one of two types, chosen when you create it:

TypeWhat it isParty
SalesSomething you're selling to a customer.A customer (company / contact).
PurchaseSomething you're buying from a supplier.A supplier (company / contact).

The two statuses on every order

An order carries a fulfilment status (how far along delivery is) and a payment status (how much has been paid) — kept separate because a deal can be paid but not yet delivered, or delivered but not yet paid.

Fulfilment status

StatusMeaning
Pre-orderDrafted, not yet confirmed — still editable.
ConfirmedAgreed and committed.
Partially deliveredSome lines (or quantities) have shipped, not all.
CompletedFully delivered.
CancelledCalled off.

Payment status

StatusMeaning
UnpaidNothing paid yet.
Partially paidSome payment received/made.
PaidSettled in full.

What's on the screen

  • Type filter — All / Sales / Purchase.
  • Status filter — All statuses, or one of the fulfilment statuses above.
  • Search — by party (customer/supplier) or order number (“Party or number”).
  • The list — columns for Order (number), Type, Party, Status, Payment, Total and Created.
  • Create buttons — New order (a sales order), Pre-order (a sales order that starts in the Pre-order state), and Purchase order (an order to a supplier). Each opens the create modal pre-set to that kind.

How to create an order — step by step

  1. Click New order (sales), Pre-order, or Purchase order — the create modal opens pre-set to that type.
  2. Pick the party (the Customer for a sales order, or the supplier for a purchase order — searchable). Choose the Currency (defaults to EUR) and optionally link a Project.
  3. Add Items — type to search the catalogue and pick a product (this links the line to the warehouse, so delivery moves stock), or type a new product name to add an off-warehouse line. Set Qty, Unit price and VAT per line; the Net, VAT and Total foot at the bottom.
  4. Click Create. The order appears in the list with its number and a starting state.

Catalogue line vs ad-hoc line. A line added from the catalogue can move warehouse stock when the order is delivered; an ad-hoc (typed-in) line stays off-warehouse. This matters for the dispatch/warehouse flow below.

Editing line items. While an order is still a draft / un-invoiced, you can edit it freely — change a line's quantity, price or VAT, and add or remove lines; the totals recompute as you go. Once an invoice has been raised against the order, the lines are locked so the order and its invoice can't drift apart.

Inside an order — the detail view

Click any order in the list to open its detail view. This is where an order earns its keep: everything about the deal — what was sold, how much has shipped, what's been invoiced and paid, and what you made — is consolidated on one screen. From top to bottom you get:

1. The header

The order's number, a type badge (sales / purchase), the fulfilment status badge, the payment status, and the order total.

2. Line items (with delivery progress)

A table of the order's lines — Item, Qty, Delivered, Unit price and line Total. The Delivered column shows how much of each line has actually shipped (so a part-shipped order shows, line by line, what's still outstanding), and a catalogue-vs-typed line is marked “(off-warehouse)” when it isn't linked to stock. While the order is still a draft / un-invoiced, an Edit items button turns this into an editable grid (change qty / price / VAT, add or remove lines).

3. The action row

A Status dropdown (plus a one-click Mark completed) and the context-sensitive buttons — Create invoice, Record supplier bill, Receive into warehouse, Record payment and Dispatch board — covered in the next section.

4. The money grid — Received · Paid · Profit

Three cards summarise the order's economics: Received (customer payments in, green), Paid to suppliers (payments out, red), and Profit (Received − Paid to suppliers, green when positive, red when negative). This is the per-order profit, computed live from the payments linked to the order.

5. Attached documents

Below the money grid, the order lists everything financial that hangs off it — each appearing only when present:

SectionWhat it lists
InvoicesEvery invoice raised against the order — number, status, total and amount still due.
Supplier bills(Purchase orders) the supplier bills recorded against the order — number, status, total and amount due.
PaymentsEach payment linked to the order — date, direction (In / Out), method and amount (in green/red by direction).

A footnote on the detail reminds you that dispatch is invoice-driven (see below).

Actions on an order

Open an order and the available buttons depend on whether it's a sales or a purchase order:

ActionOnWhat it does
Create invoiceSales orderRaises an invoice for the order's lines in one click (even for a manually-created order) and links it back to the order.
Record supplier billPurchase orderRecords the supplier's bill against the order (the purchase-side equivalent of an invoice).
Receive into warehousePurchase orderReceives the ordered goods into Warehouse stock (catalogue lines), so on-hand quantities go up when the delivery arrives.
Record paymentBothLogs a payment on the order — money received (sales) or paid out (purchase) — which updates the payment status and the Received / Profit figures.

Orders on a project

Orders can be scoped to a project: pick a project when you create the order, and that order then shows in the project's Finance tab — so on a project you see exactly the orders (and their money) that belong to it, not the whole workspace.

Customers see their own orders

Each customer can see the orders you've raised for them — with line-item detail, status and any invoices/receipts to download — in their Client Portal. You don't send anything extra; issuing the order is enough.

Dispatch is invoice-driven

An order's Dispatch board button takes you to the dispatch queue. The flow is deliberate: once the order's invoice is paid and flagged for shipping, the order appears on the Dispatch board, which cuts the delivery note and moves warehouse stock — for catalogue lines only (ad-hoc lines stay off-warehouse). So you don't ship straight from the order; you raise and settle the invoice, and dispatch picks it up from there.

POS sales become orders automatically

You don't have to create an order by hand for counter sales. Every POS checkout automatically creates a sales order in a Completed, Paid, Delivered state — so even till sales show up in the orders list and roll into the customer's account and your profit figures, with no extra step.

Orders on a customer's account

A company in CRM is treated as an Account: its detail page has an Account tab (the running balance + ledger) and an Orders tab (all of that company's orders). Quotes, invoices and payments now live per order, so the Account tab is the money summary and each order is where the documents sit. (The customer's running balance is shown as the Account balance.)

What it affects

  • Invoices (Invoices) and payments link to an order, which is how Received / Paid / Profit are computed.
  • Paid, ship-flagged invoices flow to the Dispatch board → delivery note + warehouse movement (catalogue lines).
  • POS sales auto-create completed orders.
  • Each order rolls up to the customer's Account in CRM.
