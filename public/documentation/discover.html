Discover & Network

/discover /network

Discover is the community directory — one search box across people, manufacturers, products and the surplus marketplace. Network is where you keep track of the business relationships you build along the way. This guide walks each part in turn: what it is, why it matters, exactly how to use it, and what it changes elsewhere on the platform.

How to open it: top navigation → Discover. Switch the Profiles, Factory, Products and Marketplace tabs; reach the network at /network (or the workspace switcher → Manage network).

How to read this page: Discover has four tabs (Profiles, Factory, Products, Marketplace) that all share one search box. Network is a separate page for your reseller relationships. Each section below is taught the same way — What it is → Why it matters → How → What it affects → tips — so you can jump straight to the part you need.

What Discover is for

What it is — the public-facing side of MaterialsHub: the place to see who and what is on the platform beyond your own workspace. Whether you are looking for a designer to collaborate with, a manufacturer whose ranges fit a project, a specific material, or a one-off bargain on surplus stock, you start in the same place.

Why it matters — everywhere else on the platform your data stays inside your own workspace. Discover is the deliberate window out — the only way to find people, factories and products that belong to other organisations. A single search field at the top — “Search by name, service, location…” — filters whichever tab you are on, so you can switch contexts without retyping what you are looking for.

The unified search and its four tabs

The search box works across four tabs. The pattern is always the same: pick the tab that matches what you want to find, then type a name, a service, a location or a keyword to narrow the results. The table below is the quick map; the sections after it teach each tab in depth.

Profiles

What it is — the community member directory: designers, consultants, professionals and other users who have a public presence. Why it matters — projects are rarely solo. Profiles is how you find the right person to collaborate with or hire before you ever leave the platform — opening a member's card takes you to their public profile, where you can judge their services and a sample of their work, then decide whether to reach out or connect.

How to use it

  1. Open Discover and select the Profiles tab.
  2. Use the All types filter to narrow the directory to a particular kind of member (for example a profile type or a professional type), or leave it on All types to see everyone.
  3. Type into the Search box (“Search by name, service, location…”) — it matches on a member's name, the services they offer or their location, so you find the right person without scrolling the whole directory.
  4. Open a profile: each result is a card; click it to open that member's public profile and review their services and work.

All types — a type filter to narrow the directory to a particular kind of member, or leave it on All types to see everyone.

Search — a search box (“Search by name, service, location…”) that matches on a member's name, the services they offer or their location.

What it affects elsewhere: every profile you open is the same public page that lives at a stable, shareable link — see Public pages. Reaching out to a member starts a conversation that lands in your Inbox.

Factory

What it is — the manufacturers directory. Each entry leads to that manufacturer's material range and the product categories they cover. Why it matters — it's the quickest way to discover who makes the kind of material you need. Rather than searching loose products, you start from the source — the factory — and browse its full range and the categories it produces.

How to use it

  1. Select the Factory tab in Discover.
  2. Use the All categories filter to narrow the directory to manufacturers that produce a given category, or leave it on All categories to see every factory.
  3. Type into the Search box (“Search factories…”) to jump straight to a specific manufacturer.
  4. Open a factory: click a card to open the manufacturer and browse its material range and the categories it produces.

All categories — a category filter so you can narrow the directory to manufacturers that produce a given category, or leave it on All categories to see every factory.

Search — a search box (“Search factories…”) that matches on the manufacturer, so you can jump straight to a specific factory.

Open a factory — click a card to open the manufacturer and browse its material range and the categories it produces.

What it affects elsewhere: the ranges you find here are the same products that feed the Products tab and the conversational search in the Agent Hub — Factory is simply the source-first way in.

Products

What it is — a single list of all materials and products published across the platform, rather than searching workspace by workspace. Why it matters — it's the fast way to see what's available before you commit to a specific manufacturer or listing — a flat catalogue you can scan, filter and page through. For richer, conversational product search you can also use the Agent Hub.

How to use it

  1. Select the Products tab in Discover.
  2. Use the All categories filter to restrict the list to a single material category, or leave it on All categories to browse everything.
  3. Choose how you scan with the View toggle — a grid of visual cards, or a compact list of rows.
  4. Type into the Search box (“Search materials by name or factory…”) to match on the product name or the factory that makes it.
  5. Step through the results with the Pagination controls (1, 2, 3 …) to work down a large catalogue.

All categories — a category filter to restrict the list to a single material category, or leave it on All categories to browse everything.

View toggle — switch between a grid view (visual cards) and a list view (compact rows), whichever suits how you're scanning the catalogue.

Pagination — results are paged (1, 2, 3 …); step through the pages to work down a large catalogue.

Search — a search box (“Search materials by name or factory…”) that matches on the product name or the factory that makes it.

What it affects elsewhere: products you find here are the same items you specify on quotes and documents in Finance and search conversationally in the Agent Hub — Discover is the browse surface, the others are where you act on what you found.

Surplus Marketplace

Surplus & last-stock materials listed by dealers, operators and architects — contact the seller directly, zero platform commission.

What it is — the Marketplace tab is the platform's surplus and last-stock marketplace. Workspaces list items they want to move — overstock, discontinued ranges, end-of-line pieces — and these are offered to the whole community at 0% commission. Why it matters — stock that would otherwise sit idle can find a buyer beyond your own organisation, and because the platform takes nothing, the whole margin on the deal stays between buyer and seller. You browse the listings just like any other tab, and when something fits you send an inquiry directly to the seller.

Finding your way around the Marketplace

The Marketplace gives you a few controls to cut through the listings — the same filter/search pattern as the other tabs, plus a standing alert so matching stock can come to you:

All categories — a category filter so you can narrow listings to the kind of material you're after, or leave it on All categories to see everything.

Search — a search box (“Search surplus by name or seller…”) that matches on the item name or the seller, so you can hunt for a specific product or a specific workspace's stock.

Alert me for these filters — set your category and search filters, then use the Alert me for these filters button to be notified whenever new surplus that matches those filters is listed. You don't have to keep checking back — matching stock comes to you.

Where listings come from

What it is — Marketplace listings are surplus drawn from a seller's Warehouse stock — the inventory tracked in the finance module. Why it matters — the Marketplace isn't a separate inventory you maintain by hand; it reads from the same warehouse you already keep for finance, so listing is a quick toggle on stock you're already tracking. When a workspace has no surplus to show, the tab reads “No surplus listings yet — be the first to list from your warehouse”. To put your own stock up for sale you list it from your Warehouse; see Finance & operations for how Warehouse stock works.

How inquiries reach the seller — buyers contact the seller directly. The inquiry doesn't open a separate messaging system: the conversation is handled through your Inbox, alongside your other platform messages, so you can negotiate, ask for details and arrange the deal in one place. Listings and inquiries work across workspaces, and the platform takes 0% commission on whatever you agree.

The Marketplace is deliberately the one place where listings are visible across workspaces. Everywhere else on the platform, your data stays inside your own workspace — surplus listings are the intentional exception so that stock can find a buyer beyond your own organisation.

Step by step: list surplus stock

What this does — turns idle warehouse stock into a public listing the whole community can find and inquire about, with no commission deducted.

  1. Go to your Warehouse in the finance module and find the stock you want to move — overstock, discontinued ranges or end-of-line pieces.
  2. List the item as surplus from your Warehouse so it appears in the Marketplace.
  3. Set its category so buyers can find it with the All categories filter.
  4. Once listed, it's visible to the whole community across workspaces at 0% commission — buyers contact you directly.

What it affects elsewhere: the listing reads live from Warehouse stock, and buyer inquiries arrive in your Inbox. Tip: a clear name and the right category are what make your stock turn up in a buyer's filtered search and in their Alert me for these filters notifications.

Step by step: find & inquire

What this does — finds surplus across every workspace and opens a direct line to the seller.

  1. Open Discover from the top navigation and switch to the Marketplace tab.
  2. Use the All categories filter and the “Search surplus by name or seller…” box to narrow the surplus and last-stock listings shared across workspaces. Tip: use Alert me for these filters to be notified when new matching stock is listed.
  3. Open a listing to see its details.
  4. Contact the seller directly by sending an inquiry.
  5. Continue the conversation in your Inbox, where the inquiry and the seller's replies are handled alongside your other platform messages. See also Public pages.

What it affects elsewhere: the whole exchange lives in your Inbox — there's no second app to check. Tip: set your filters first and click Alert me for these filters once, so future matching stock finds you instead of the other way round.

Network

/network network.manage

Your dealers, architects and their commission — you set the cut on each node's operator-catalog sales.

What it is — while Discover is about finding people and businesses, Network is your B2B reseller / dealer network. Each entry is a downstream workspace — a dealer or architect — that sits beneath you: you grant them access to your catalogue and set the commission you take on the sales they make from your operator catalogue. The page is a table of those sub-workspaces, so you can see at a glance who is in your network, where they sit, what they can sell and what each relationship earns you.

Why it matters — this is how a manufacturer or operator runs a distribution channel on the platform. Instead of every dealer or architect keeping a private copy of your range, you grant them controlled access to your catalogue and let them sell from it — and the commission and catalog access columns are the two levers that make that channel work: catalog access decides what each dealer or architect is allowed to sell, and commission decides what you earn when they sell it. Setting these per node means a high-volume dealer and a one-project architect can carry completely different terms.

Network is capability-gated by network.manage. If you don't see it, your role or plan may not include connection management; an administrator can adjust this.

The network table

Each row is one sub-workspace (a dealer or architect) in your network. The columns describe how that node fits into your structure and what you take on its sales:

ColumnWhat it means
WorkspaceThe downstream dealer or architect workspace — the node in your network.
RankWhere the node sits in the network hierarchy — its level relative to you and to other nodes.
ParentThe workspace directly above this node — who it hangs off in the tree (you, or another node you've placed it under).
Catalog accessHow much of your operator catalogue this node may sell — for example Full catalog, or a narrower slice you've granted.
Commission %The cut you take on that node's operator-catalog sales. You set this per node, so different dealers or architects can carry different commission rates.
e-InvoicingThe node's e-invoicing status — whether that downstream workspace is set up to issue compliant invoices for the sales it makes.

Why commission, catalog access and e-Invoicing matter

Catalog access is your control over reach. Granting Full catalog turns a dealer into a full-line reseller; granting a narrower slice lets you give an architect only the ranges relevant to their projects, or keep premium lines for selected partners. It's how you tailor the channel without maintaining separate price lists.

Commission % is your control over economics, set per node. Because each dealer or architect can carry a different rate, you can reward a high-volume distributor with keener terms while a low-touch architect carries a standard cut — and you can read, at a glance, what every relationship in the channel is worth to you.

e-Invoicing status tells you whether a downstream node can actually issue compliant invoices for the sales it makes. A dealer or architect that isn't set up for e-invoicing can't close the loop legally on their end, so this column is your readiness signal for each node before you lean on them for volume. See Greek e-invoicing for what that setup involves.

What it affects elsewhere: catalog access governs what each node sees of your products; commission flows through onto the sales they make from your operator catalogue; and e-invoicing readiness ties back to each node's own Finance settings. Tip: set catalog access and commission deliberately per node rather than uniformly — that per-node flexibility is the whole point of running the channel here.
