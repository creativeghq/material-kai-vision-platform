Public & Shared Pages

The screens your customers, clients and prospects see — usually opened from a link you send them, on any device, without ever signing in.

Many of the things you create in MaterialsHub are meant to leave the workspace and land in front of someone else: a quote to approve, an invoice to pay, a project deck to sign off, a catalogue to browse. Rather than exporting a file and emailing it, the platform turns each of these into a live, link-based page. The recipient clicks the link, sees an up-to-date view, and acts on it directly — and whatever they do (accept a quote, leave a comment, pay an invoice) flows straight back into the record it came from.

These pages sit outside the signed-in app. There is no login wall and no menu — just the single deliverable you shared. If you are looking for the screens you use to create them, follow the cross-links beside each entry below.

What each page does

🧾 Public quote

A clean, read-only presentation of a quote: every line item with its description and specification, sub-totals, taxes and the grand total. The recipient can accept it with one click — turning a proposal into a confirmed order without back-and-forth email.

💳 Pay invoice

Shows the invoice and an online payment option, so a customer can settle the amount the moment they open the link. The payment is reconciled back against the invoice in your Finance workspace.

🎨 Public moodboard

A shareable view of a moodboard — the images, materials and products you've gathered — for a client or collaborator to browse at their leisure.

📐 Client view

The richest deliverable: a project sign-off page that bundles the deck as a PDF, an explorable 3D walkthrough, switchable lighting moods over the hero render, a live FF&E list, and inline approve/comment controls so the client can respond in place.

🗂️ Shared sheet

A single presentation sheet on its own page — handy when you want to send just one board (a material schedule, a colour palette, an annotated render) rather than a whole deck.

📚 Public catalogue

A published, browsable catalogue of products. Some catalogues are open; others ask the visitor for an email address first, which also lets you see who's looking.

🏬 Online storefront

A seller's public shop page, where prospects can browse what's available and find their way into an enquiry or quote.

👤 Public profile

A member's public-facing page — who they are and the services they provide — useful as a shareable business card.

💬 Public inbox thread

A shared conversation that an outside party can read and reply to without registering, so they can take part directly in a discussion you've opened to them.

🔍 AR material preview

An augmented-reality view of a material. Usually reached by scanning a QR code on a desktop screen so the visitor can preview the material in their own space on a phone or tablet.

How sharing works

  • Generated on demand — you create each link from the screen it belongs to: a quote, an invoice, a moodboard, a project, a catalogue or a conversation. Nothing is public until you choose to share it.
  • Token-protected — most links carry a unique, hard-to-guess token (the :token in the routes above). Only someone holding that exact link can open the page.
  • Revocable and time-limited — you can revoke a link at any time, and many can be set to expire on a date. Once revoked or expired, the page stops working for everyone who has it.
  • No account needed — recipients interact straight away: view, accept, pay, browse or comment. They never see your dashboard or anyone else's data.
  • Responses come back to you — an accepted quote, a paid invoice and a client's approvals or comments all flow back into the originating record, so your workspace always reflects the latest state.

Keeping shared links safe

Because these pages are public by design, anyone with the link can open them. Send links only to the people they're meant for, avoid posting them anywhere they could be picked up by others, and revoke or let expire any link to a deliverable that's no longer current — for example an old quote, a superseded project deck or a paid invoice.

If you suspect a link has been forwarded further than intended, revoking it from the originating screen and issuing a fresh one is the quickest way to cut off access while keeping the underlying record intact.
