Finance & Invoicing

/finance /finance/invoices/:id finance.manage

A complete back-office: revenue and profit, receivables and payables, the full set of financial documents, Greek e-invoicing, warehouse and dispatch, planning, and reporting — all in one workspace.

How to open it: top navigation → Finance. The Dashboard tab opens first; use the tab bar for everything else.

What this area is for

Finance is the accounting and billing centre of your workspace. The Dashboard tab gives you the financial health of the business at a glance, and the remaining tabs let you create and manage every kind of document, transmit invoices to the Greek tax authority, run the warehouse/dispatch side, plan ahead and report. Because it's deep, this guide splits Finance into focused sub-pages — use the list below or the sidebar.

Finance at a glance (Dashboard)

The default Dashboard tab summarises:

  • Revenue & profit for the period.
  • Receivables — money customers owe you.
  • Payables — money you owe suppliers.
  • Follow-up queue — items that need action (e.g. overdue documents).

The four KPI cards

Across the top of the Dashboard, four cards give you the headline numbers:

  • AR outstanding — the total amount (€) customers currently owe you, with a status indicator (e.g. "All on schedule") summarising whether receivables are on track.
  • AP outstanding — the total amount (€) you currently owe suppliers, alongside a count of open bills.
  • This month revenue (net) — net revenue recognised for the current month.
  • DSO (rough) — a rough Days Sales Outstanding figure in days (how long, on average, it takes to collect what you're owed), plus a count of quotes needing follow-up.

Monthly cash-flow table

Below the cards, a month-by-month table breaks down money movement and profitability:

ColumnWhat it shows
MonthThe calendar month for the row.
InMoney received during the month.
OutMoney paid out during the month.
NetIn minus Out — the net cash movement.
RevenueRevenue recognised in the month.
COGSCost of goods sold for the month.
MarginRevenue minus COGS.
%Margin as a percentage of revenue.
