Projects

/projects /projects/:id

A project is the top-level container that ties together everything for one piece of work — the moodboards, presentation sheets, quotes and people involved — and gives you a single place to assemble and share a polished deliverable with the client. This guide walks you through it the way you'd actually use it: how to create one, what each tab does, why you'd break it into rooms, how to bundle everything into a Client View, and how to bring teammates and clients in. Each part is taught the same way — what it is, why it matters, exactly how to do it, and what it changes elsewhere.

How to open it: top navigation → Projects (module-gated — if it isn't shown, the Projects module isn't enabled for your workspace).

Why projects exist

What it is — the container that sits one level above the rest of your design work. Where a moodboard is the working surface and a quote is a single document, a project wraps both — it groups all the boards, sheets and quotes for one client or job, keeps the people involved in one place, and is where you produce the finished package you hand over.

Why it matters — design work for a single client rarely lives in one artifact. You sketch ideas across several boards, price line items in one or more quotes, and eventually need to present a coherent story. Without a container, those pieces drift apart. The project keeps them together so that, when it's time to deliver, everything you need is already in one home base. If you run work by client or by room, the project is that home base.

Projects are module-gated — if you don't see Projects in the navigation, the module isn't enabled for your workspace.

The list page

What it is — the /projects page, which shows every active project as a card and is your jumping-off point. Why it matters — it's the index of all your work; from here you start new jobs, find old ones, and step into any project's detail page.

How to use it

  • New Project — create a project and name it for the client or job.
  • Show archived — reveal projects you've archived so they're out of the way but still findable.
  • Open a project — click any card to go to its detail page.

What it affects: creating a project here gives you a fresh detail page with all its tabs ready to fill in; archiving simply hides a card from this list (see Archiving).

Step by step: create a project

  1. From the top navigation, open Projects, then click New Project.
  2. Enter a name for the client or job, and add any scope / briefing notes to describe the work.
  3. Create it, then open the project to reach its detail page.
  4. Work across its tabs — rooms, products, moodboards, quotes, sheets, and the rest — to assemble everything in one place.

Name the project for who or what it's for (the client, the address, the job) rather than for the design idea — the design ideas live in the moodboards inside it. A clear scope note up front saves you re-explaining the brief to collaborators you invite later.

Inside a project: the tabs

What it is — a project's detail page (/projects/:id) is organised into tabs that cover the work and its deliverables. Why it matters — the tabs are how a project's many moving parts stay separated but reachable from one place; the set you see depends on your role — some tabs are owner-only or shown only when their module is enabled. Shared collaborators get a reduced set (the owner-only tabs stay private).

The headline capability — the reason most of the other tabs exist — is the Client View. Everything you assemble (boards, sheets, a quote for the FF&E list) feeds into it.

Rooms

What it is — a way to split the project's scope and budget per space, each room captured as its own entry under the Rooms tab. Why it matters — most real jobs aren't one undifferentiated space — a home is a master bath, a kitchen, a living room. Breaking the project into rooms lets you organise the work the way the building is actually laid out and, crucially, allocate a budget to each space so you can see where the money is going room by room instead of as one lump sum. It mirrors how clients think and talk about their own project.

How to add a room

  1. Open the Rooms tab and click Add a room.
  2. Enter the Room name — e.g. Master Bath.
  3. Choose the Type — the kind of space.
  4. Set the Budget — the amount allocated to that room.
  5. Set a Date for the room, and save.

What it affects: rooms give the project a per-space structure and per-room budgets, so the project is organised and costed by area rather than as a single figure. Repeat for each space in the job.

Client Views owner-only

What it is — a project-level deliverable that bundles selected presentation sheets — drawn from any of the project's boards — into one client-ready package: a PDF plus a revocable online page. It's the thing you actually send to the client.

Why it matters — by the end of a project your best work is scattered across several boards as individual sheets. The client shouldn't have to receive five separate boards and stitch the story together themselves. A Client View bundles the sheets you choose, in the order you choose, into a single coherent presentation — so the client gets one polished thing instead of a pile of parts. It sits one level above a single board's deck: a board's deck is the lightweight, single-board PDF; the Client View combines sheets from several boards and adds an interactive online surface on top of the PDF.

That online page is the real payoff, because it can carry things a static PDF simply can't:

  • 3D walkthrough — an explorable Marble world embedded right in the page.
  • Lighting moods — switch the hero render between lighting presets so the client sees the space in different light.
  • Live FF&E table — a furniture, fixtures & equipment list pulled live from a linked quote, so it stays accurate.
  • Inline approve / comment — the client can sign off or leave feedback directly on the page.

What the client experiences at /cv/:token

When you share a Client View, the client opens a public page at /cv/:token — no login required, just the link. They see the bundled deck (the PDF you generated) plus whichever interactive extras you switched on: an explorable 3D walkthrough, the hero render with switchable lighting moods, and the live FF&E table from the linked quote. They can approve or comment inline, right on the page. Because access is tied to that token, you can revoke the share link at any time to cut off access — useful once a project is closed or if a link was sent in error. For more on how the public-facing page behaves, see Public pages & sharing.

What it affects: anything the client does on the online page — an approval or a comment — comes back into the project, so you read their feedback in one place rather than chasing it across email.

Building a Client View (every field)

Open the Client View tab and click New Client View. The builder offers these fields:

FieldNotes
TitleThe deliverable's title (e.g. Modern Bathroom Design).
SubtitleA secondary line under the title.
Client nameShown as "Prepared for…".
DateThe date for the deliverable.
Sheets to includePick and order the presentation sheets — drawn from any board in the project.
FF&E source quoteChoose a quote to render its live FF&E table.
3D worldChoose a generated Marble world to embed an explorable walkthrough.

When you're done, Save & generate PDF — or Save draft to come back later.

  • The online client page (/cv/:token) can also show switchable lighting moods on the hero render.
  • Clients can approve or comment inline directly on the page.
  • The share link is revocable — disable it at any time to cut off access.

Step by step: deliver a Client View

  1. Open the project → Client View → New Client View.
  2. Set the Title, Subtitle, Client name and Date.
  3. Select and order the sheets to include — you can draw them from any of the project's boards.
  4. Optionally pick an FF&E source quote (to render its live FF&E table) and a 3D world (to embed a walkthrough).
  5. Save & generate PDF to produce the downloadable version of the package.
  6. Copy the share link and send it to the client; revoke it at any time to cut off access. See Public pages & sharing for how the public page behaves.
  7. Read the client's inline approvals and comments back in the project.

The Client View is one level above a single board's deck: it can combine sheets from several boards in the project, and adds the interactive online surface on top of the PDF. Switch on only the embeds you actually have ready — there's no point enabling a 3D walkthrough or lighting moods if you haven't generated the underlying world or render yet.

Inviting collaborators & clients

What it is — a way to bring other people — teammates or the client — into a project via an invite link (/projects/invite/:token). Why it matters — projects are collaborative: you may want a colleague working alongside you in the same boards and quotes, or the client looking in on progress. Invitations let you do that without handing over your whole workspace — shared users see the project marked as shared and get a reduced set of tabs, so the owner-only tabs (Products, Billing, Client View, Timeline) stay private.

Step by step: invite a collaborator

  1. Create an invite for the teammate or client you want to bring into the project.
  2. Share the invite link (/projects/invite/:token) with them.
  3. The invitee lands on a public landing page and accepts the invite after signing in, gaining access to the project.

What it affects: accepted invitees gain access to the project's non-private tabs and see it flagged as shared; the owner-only tabs remain hidden from them.

Archiving

What it is — a way to put a finished project out of sight without deleting it. Why it matters — completed jobs clutter the active list and slow you down, but you rarely want them gone for good — you may need to reference them later. Archiving hides a project from the active list while keeping everything intact. How: archive a project when it's finished; use Show archived on the list page to bring archived projects back into view at any time.

In short: create a project per client or job, do the design work in moodboards and price it in quotes, break the scope into rooms if it helps with budgeting, then assemble the chosen sheets into a Client View and send the share link. The client opens it at /cv/:token, explores the interactive extras, and approves or comments — and their feedback returns to the project.
