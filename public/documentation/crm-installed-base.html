CRM · Equipment & Service Installed base

/crm/companies/:id?tab=equipment /crm/contacts/:id?tab=equipment crm.view

The Equipment tab tracks your installed base — the units you've sold and installed at a customer's site that keep needing service: an air-conditioner whose filters need cleaning, a boiler, a water heater. It answers "what have we got out there, is it still under warranty, and what's due next?" — and it does the scheduling for you, so the AC you sold two summers ago still gets its service booked. This is recurring, planned maintenance on a customer's own equipment, distinct from the internal Company Assets register (your own vehicles/tools) and from reactive repair tickets.

Where: open a CRM company or contact and pick the Equipment tab (the wrench icon). It's part of the CRM — no extra module or credits. Service-reminder notifications link straight to this tab.

What's on the tab — three cards

  • Service coming up — a worklist that appears only when something is due, with a count of what's overdue and what's inside the reminder window. Each row has one-click Done and Skip buttons.
  • Equipment — the register of units (name, serial, installed date, next service, status), with an Add equipment button.
  • Service history — every visit across all this customer's units in one ledger (when, which unit, what service, outcome, who, cost) — so you can read the whole relationship at once, not one unit at a time.

Registering a unit

A unit gets onto the register two ways:

  • Automatically, when you deliver a serviceable product. If a product is marked serviceable (see below) and it's delivered on a sales order, a unit is created for that customer automatically — inheriting the customer, project, purchase date and supplier from the order. This is how "the AC you sold" lands in the installed base without anyone re-typing it.
  • Manually, with Add equipment — for a unit you didn't sell them, or one delivered before you started tracking. You fill in name, brand, model, serial, install date, location and notes.

Each unit carries a status — Active, Removed, Replaced or Decommissioned — and can be tied to a project and room (e.g. "the AC in the second-floor bedroom of the Kolonaki renovation").

You retire a unit, you don't delete it. Once a unit has any service history, deleting it is blocked — erasing the unit would erase the record of what you did to it. Instead, set its status to Removed / Replaced / Decommissioned: the unit stops appearing as live, but its history stays intact.

Warranties & certificates

Each unit can carry one or more warranties — manufacturer, extended, installer or insurance — with the provider, policy number, start/end dates, coverage notes and reminder offsets. A product's default warranty length is copied onto the manufacturer warranty at registration, counted from the install date, so you don't set it each time. The tab shows each warranty's state (active / expiring / expired) computed from its end date.

The “certificate” is the document you attach. A warranty certificate here is the scan you upload (PDF or image, up to 5 MB) — the platform doesn't generate one. It's stored privately and opened through a short-lived signed link, so the URL can't leak or rot. Attach it with the paperclip on the warranty row; view or detach it there too.

The service schedule — one thing due at a time

A unit's service schedule is what makes this more than a list. You set, per unit: what needs doing (“Clean the filters”), how often (every N months), how many days before to remind you, and whether to email the customer too.

The key idea: a schedule always has exactly one open occurrence — the next service. There's no separate “next due” field being calculated; the next date is that one open row. When you mark a service Done (or Skip), the platform closes it and opens the next one in the same step, anchored on when the work actually happened — so a service done late pushes the whole cycle along rather than silently drifting. That's why completing a service is the only thing that schedules the next: the worklist can never show two of the same job, and it can never run dry while a unit is still in service.

History outlives the schedule. If you remove a schedule, the visits it already produced stay in the service history (tagged so you can see the schedule was removed). You lose the future occurrences, never the past record.

Reminders & the customer email

A daily job raises a service-due (and overdue) event as each occurrence enters its reminder window, and a warranty-expiring event ahead of a warranty's end. These flow through Flows, so how they're delivered is yours to edit — an in-app notification to the responsible person by default, and, when you tick “Email the customer too” on the schedule, an email to the customer as well (“your AC is due for a filter clean on …”). Because it's a Flow, an admin can retarget or add a channel without a deploy.

Note: the warranty-expiring reminder goes to you (a prompt to call about renewal), not to the customer — only the service-due reminder has a customer email branch, and only when you've asked for it.

Making a product serviceable — the defaults

What it is — on a product you own, a Warranty & service defaults tab lets you mark it serviceable and set the defaults every installed unit should inherit: the default warranty months and a default service schedule (what/every-how-often/remind/email-the-customer). Why it matters — set it once on the product and every future sale of it registers a unit with the right warranty and service plan already attached. Editing this tab needs a management permission (pricing, warehouse or finance) — using a customer's equipment doesn't.

What it connects to

  • CRM — the whole feature is a tab on the customer record; reminders fall back to the customer's responsible sales users.
  • Orders — delivering a serviceable product on a sale creates the unit and remembers the source order and supplier.
  • Projects — a unit can be tied to a project and room.
  • Flows — service-due, overdue and warranty-expiring events are delivered through editable flows.

A completed service records a cost, but this feature doesn't auto-issue an invoice — bill a service the normal way in Finance if you charge for it.

Worked example — the AC you installed. Mark your AC product serviceable with a 24-month warranty and a “Clean filters, every 6 months” schedule. Sell and deliver one to a customer → a unit appears on their Equipment tab with the warranty and the first service already scheduled. Six months on it shows in Service coming up; you (and, if you asked, the customer) are reminded; you click Done and the next one is booked automatically. Attach the manufacturer's warranty PDF to the unit so it's on file if a claim comes up.
