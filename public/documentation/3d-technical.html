3D & Rendering — How It Works

Technical referenceScene Studio module

The technical side of 3D Rooms, Product 3D Models and the Room Planner: every AI model we call and why it won, everything that runs in your browser and the technique behind it, the open-source projects it is built on, and what each step costs. Written for technical buyers, partners and our own team.

The split, in one line: the cloud does what needs a large model (reading a plan, photoreal renders, turning a photo into a 3D mesh); the browser does everything that is geometry or light (the live 3D room, path-traced Photo Mode, baked light, video), so those are free and private.

The pipeline

  1. Read — a floor plan, room photo, render or drawing page is read by Claude into walls, openings, rooms and objects with real sizes.
  2. Edit — the room is a real 3D scene in the browser (three.js). Every part's finish is a catalogue product in one of its real colourways.
  3. Render — the current view is captured and sent to Gemini with the object list, the finishes and their product images; a photoreal picture comes back. An object-ID pass is saved with it.
  4. Make 3D — each piece is cut out of the render where it is most visible, with its exact mask, and SAM 3D turns it into a textured GLB mesh that replaces the stand-in shape.
  5. Light & picture — the browser path-traces the room (Photo Mode), denoises it with Intel OIDN, bakes light into the surfaces, and records walkthrough video frame by frame.
  6. Share — the client walkthrough link reads the saved renders, Photo Mode pictures and baked light; nothing is recomputed for the visitor.

Why the object-ID pass matters

Every render stores an ID pass beside it. It is what lets an object edit change only that object (the edit is composited back through its mask, so every other pixel stays exactly as it was), and what lets Make 3D cut a piece out cleanly. Two rules make it reliable:

  • Colours are matched exactly. With 30+ objects, neighbouring ID colours are close; a loose match picks up the anti-aliased edges of other objects. The match tolerance is 6 of 255.
  • The render that shows the piece best is used — measured by how many pixels the piece covers across all saved renders. A piece that no render shows is refused before anything is charged.

Removing one object, emptying and furnishing a room

  • Remove one object (masked): Finegrain Eraser was cleanest; Bria and FLUX Erase were clean but removed or invented something nearby; fal Object Removal failed. Our object edit keeps every pixel outside the mask exact, which none of the prompt-only models do.
  • Empty a room: Nano Banana Pro followed the removal list; Nano Banana 2 left lamps behind.
  • Furnish an empty room: Nano Banana Pro kept the architecture; Nano Banana 2 deleted a wall and a FLUX staging LoRA furnished the next room as well.

What we tried and retired

  • Gaussian splats (Spark.js, and our own Idol service) — photo-like, but no real geometry: they could not take a material, cast correct light or be measured. Replaced by SAM 3D meshes.
  • Hosted photoreal rooms (World Labs) — replaced by our own path-traced Photo Mode, which runs on real geometry and costs nothing per picture.
  • splat-transform — evaluated for shrinking splats; no longer needed once splats were retired.

Known limits

  • A mesh made from one picture has the right proportions but no measured size; in a 3D room it takes the object's box, and in the Room Planner it takes a size someone stated (otherwise a 0.60 m placeholder, flagged as such).
  • A mesh cut from a render carries that render's lighting in its texture; a clean product photo gives truer colour.
  • Photo Mode, baked light and video need the browser tab in front; the denoiser needs WebGPU (recent Chrome or Edge).
