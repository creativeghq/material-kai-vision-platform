CRM

/crm /crm/contacts/:id /crm/companies/:id crm.view

The CRM is your relationship hub — the single place to keep track of the people and organisations you do business with, alongside the users who belong to your workspace. It is not a stand-alone address book: clean CRM records feed directly into quotes, projects and Finance, so an invoice always carries the right legal name, VAT number and address, payables list the right suppliers, and an outreach email reaches the right segment. This guide walks each of the four tabs and each major form-group — what it is, why it matters, how to fill it in, and what it changes elsewhere on the platform.

How to open it: top navigation → CRM. Switch between the Users, Contacts, Companies and Categories tabs.

Recommended order if you're starting fresh: create your Categories first so you have segments to file people under → add Companies (enrich Greek ones from ΑΑΔΕ, validate VAT) → add Contacts and link them to their company and, where needed, to a login. Users mostly arrive on their own once you invite them.

What this area is for

The CRM brings together three kinds of records that the rest of the platform relies on. Each one answers a different question, and each one is referenced by another part of the app:

  • Platform users — the people with login access to your workspace, their roles and subscriptions. Why it matters: a user's role decides what they can see and do, and a contact can be linked to a user so the person you deal with also has a way in.
  • Contacts — the individual people you deal with (a buyer, an architect, a site contact). Why it matters: contacts carry the pricing, billing and VAT details that an invoice or a quote draws on.
  • Companies — the organisations you trade with: customers, suppliers and partners. Why it matters: a company is your billing counter-party; its confirmed legal name, address and VAT number are exactly what prints on a document.

Keeping these tidy means that when you raise a quote, start a project or issue an invoice, the counter-party is already on file with verified details — no re-typing, no mismatched names, no rejected myDATA submissions because a VAT number was wrong.

The four tabs at a glance

The work is organised into four tabs. The rest of this guide takes each one in turn:

  • Users — the platform users in your workspace. Shows each user's role and subscription, with a search box to find someone quickly by name or email.
  • Contacts — individual people. Add a person, store their contact details, tag them with categories, and link them to a company (and optionally to a login).
  • Companies — organisations, suppliers and customers. This is where your billing counter-parties live, including the Greek business-registry lookup described below.
  • Categories — reusable labels you create to segment users, contacts and companies (for example by relationship type, region or industry). Apply them on a record's detail page, then use them to filter, group and target outreach.

1. The Users tab

What it is — where you manage platform users, roles, and subscriptions. A toolbar Add User button opens the new-user flow, and a search box ("Search users by email…") finds someone quickly. The list is a table where each row exposes the user's role, subscription tier and credit balance at a glance, with per-row Actions.

Why it matters — a user's role is the gate on what they can do (CRM, Finance, Sales, dealer/factory portals…), so this tab is how you keep access correct. The subscription and credit columns tell you, at a glance, what each account can spend.

2. The Contacts tab

What it is — the place for managing non-user contacts and relationships — the individual people you deal with. A toolbar Add Contact button opens the New Contact form, and a search box ("Search contacts…") filters the list. Each row shows the person's headline details, with per-row Actions.

Why it matters — a contact is the person an invoice, quote or project is addressed to. Because a contact carries its own pricing, billing and VAT details, getting the record right here is what lets Finance issue a correct, ready-to-send document without anyone re-keying details.

How to add a contact

  1. Open CRM from the top navigation and go to the Contacts tab.
  2. Click Add Contact (also labelled New Contact) to open the New Contact form.
  3. Fill in Full Name (required), then Email, Phone and any other details you have.
  4. Optionally assign a category to segment the contact (the labels you manage in the Categories tab) and link them to a company.
  5. Save. The person is now on file and can be linked to companies, quotes and projects.

What it is — the bridge between a CRM contact (a record) and a platform user (a login). How to link a contact to a login (Create & Invite User)

  1. Open the contact's record and go to the Linked User Account group.
  2. If the person already has a login, use Search by email to find and attach it.
  3. If they don't, click Create & Invite User — this creates a login for the contact and invites them in as a user.
  4. Save. The contact and the user are now the same person on the platform.

What it affects elsewhere: a linked user appears in the Users tab; if you grant them the dealer or factory role there, they automatically fall into the role-linked categories described below.

How to add a company and enrich it from ΑΑΔΕ

  1. Open CRM from the top navigation and go to the Companies tab.
  2. Click Add Company and enter its core details.
  3. For a Greek company, enter its ΑΦΜ (VAT number).
  4. Run the ΑΑΔΕ lookup to auto-fill the legal name, registered address, tax office (ΔΟΥ), activity codes (ΚΑΔ) and status from the official tax-authority registry.
  5. For an EU company, use Validate (VIES) to verify the VAT number before you trade on credit or issue documents.
  6. Save. The confirmed billing details flow straight through to documents in Finance.

Greek business lookup (ΑΑΔΕ / myAADE)

What it is — a one-click pull of a Greek company's official profile from the tax-authority registry (ΑΑΔΕ), using the company's ΑΦΜ. Why it matters — typing legal details by hand invites typos that get a myDATA submission rejected; the registry is the source of truth, so this is the recommended way to validate a VAT number before invoicing a Greek customer or supplier. On a company's detail page, enter the ΑΦΜ and run the lookup; the platform retrieves and fills:

  • Legal name and commercial title.
  • Registered address — street, number, postcode and city.
  • Tax office (ΔΟΥ).
  • Activity codes (ΚΑΔ) — the company's primary and secondary business activities.
  • Status — whether the VAT number is active or has been deactivated.

What it affects elsewhere: the same registry data underpins Greek e-invoicing — see Greek e-invoicing for how invoices are transmitted to myDATA / ΑΑΔΕ, and note that Finance settings → General can block issuance against a VAT number the registry shows as inactive.

Lookups are cached for about 90 days. Once you've fetched a company's details, repeating the lookup within that window is instant and doesn't re-contact the registry — so there's no penalty for re-opening a record.

Each fresh lookup writes a notification to the looked-up company's TaxisNet inbox. This is required by tax-authority policy and can't be turned off. For that reason the lookup is meant for verifying your own business or a legitimate customer/supplier you actually trade with — not for researching arbitrary companies.

Contact & company detail pages

What they are — the full record behind a list row. Clicking any contact or company opens its own detail page — /crm/contacts/:id for a person, /crm/companies/:id for an organisation. Why they matter — this is where the whole relationship lives in one view, and where you apply the segments and confirm the billing details that the rest of the platform reads. From there you can:

  • Edit core information — name, email, phone, address, VAT/tax details, notes.
  • Assign categories — apply the segment labels you defined in the Categories tab.
  • See associated records — the contacts linked to a company, and the documents and activity tied to the record, so the whole relationship is in one view.

A company's billing details flow straight through to documents, so the legal name, address and VAT number you confirm here are exactly what appears on an invoice in Finance.

4. The Categories tab

What it is — where you group platform users, CRM contacts and companies into lists. Why it matters — those lists are what the rest of the platform targets for outreach — most directly, the "Send to Customers" action on catalogs and other email outreach send to a category rather than to individuals. A well-kept set of categories is therefore the backbone of your segmentation: get them right and outreach reaches exactly the audience you intend.

Each category is shown as a stat card whose member counts are split by Users / Contacts / Companies, so you can see at a glance how many of each kind of record a category covers.

Toolbar

  • New Category — create a new category (name it and set its type / role).
  • Edit — modify an existing category.
  • Resync auto — refreshes the auto-derived (role-linked) categories so their memberships reflect the current set of users.
  • Search categories — filters the list to find a category quickly.

How to create a category

  1. Open CRM from the top navigation and go to the Categories tab.
  2. Click New Category.
  3. Name the category and set its type / role.
  4. Save.
  5. Assign the category on a contact or company record (from its detail page).

What it affects elsewhere: the category becomes a selectable audience for the Send to Customers action and other email outreach, and a filter for grouping your contacts and companies.

Role-linked categories (auto-populated)

What they are — two special categories derived automatically from user roles rather than assigned by hand. Why they matter — they keep your dealer and factory audiences correct without manual upkeep — membership simply follows the role.

  • Dealer Accounts — users who hold the dealer role.
  • Factory Accounts — users who hold the factory role.

Because their membership comes from roles, you don't add people to these by hand — use Resync auto to refresh them so they reflect the current set of dealer and factory users (the roles you set on the Users tab).

Permissions & the Admin sub-view

Access to the CRM is gated by the crm.view capability — users without it won't see this area. Operators get an additional Admin sub-view that adds platform-wide user management on top of the standard CRM, for administering accounts, roles and subscriptions across the platform.
