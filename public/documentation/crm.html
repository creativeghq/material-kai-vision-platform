CRM

/crm /crm/contacts/:id /crm/companies/:id crm.view

Your relationship hub — the single place to keep track of the people and organisations you do business with, alongside the users who belong to your workspace. Clean CRM records feed directly into quotes, projects and Finance, so an invoice always carries the right legal name, VAT number and address.

What this area is for

The CRM brings together three kinds of records that the rest of the platform relies on:

  • Platform users — the people with login access to your workspace, their roles and subscriptions.
  • Contacts — the individual people you deal with (a buyer, an architect, a site contact).
  • Companies — the organisations you trade with: customers, suppliers and partners.

Keeping these tidy means that when you raise a quote, start a project or issue an invoice, the counter-party is already on file with verified details — no re-typing, no mismatched names.

Tabs

The work is organised into four tabs:

  • Users — the platform users in your workspace. Shows each user's role and subscription, with a search box to find someone quickly by name or email.
  • Contacts — individual people. Add a person, store their contact details, tag them with categories, and link them to a company.
  • Companies — organisations, suppliers and customers. This is where your billing counter-parties live, including the Greek business-registry lookup described below.
  • Categories — reusable labels you create to segment contacts and companies (for example by relationship type, region or industry). Apply them on a record's detail page, then use them to filter and group.

Contact & company detail pages

Clicking any contact or company opens its own detail page — /crm/contacts/:id for a person, /crm/companies/:id for an organisation. From there you can:

  • Edit core information — name, email, phone, address, VAT/tax details, notes.
  • Assign categories — apply the segment labels you defined in the Categories tab.
  • See associated records — the contacts linked to a company, and the documents and activity tied to the record, so the whole relationship is in one view.

A company's billing details flow straight through to documents, so the legal name, address and VAT number you confirm here are exactly what appears on an invoice in Finance.

Greek business lookup (ΑΑΔΕ / myAADE)

For Greek companies, MaterialsHub can fill in the business profile automatically from the official tax-authority registry (ΑΑΔΕ) using the company's ΑΦΜ (VAT number). On a company's detail page, enter the ΑΦΜ and run the lookup; the platform retrieves and fills:

  • Legal name and commercial title.
  • Registered address — street, number, postcode and city.
  • Tax office (ΔΟΥ).
  • Activity codes (ΚΑΔ) — the company's primary and secondary business activities.
  • Status — whether the VAT number is active or has been deactivated.

This is the recommended way to validate a VAT number before invoicing a Greek customer or supplier, so your documents are issued against accurate, registry-confirmed details. The same data underpins Greek e-invoicing — see Greek e-invoicing for how invoices are transmitted to myDATA / ΑΑΔΕ.

Lookups are cached for about 90 days. Once you've fetched a company's details, repeating the lookup within that window is instant and doesn't re-contact the registry — so there's no penalty for re-opening a record.

Each fresh lookup writes a notification to the looked-up company's TaxisNet inbox. This is required by tax-authority policy and can't be turned off. For that reason the lookup is meant for verifying your own business or a legitimate customer/supplier you actually trade with — not for researching arbitrary companies.

Permissions & the Admin sub-view

Access to the CRM is gated by the crm.view capability — users without it won't see this area. Operators get an additional Admin sub-view that adds platform-wide user management on top of the standard CRM, for administering accounts, roles and subscriptions across the platform.

CRM records are the backbone of billing and projects. For what happens to a company once you start invoicing it, see Finance & Invoicing; for transmitting those invoices to the Greek tax authority, see Greek e-invoicing.
