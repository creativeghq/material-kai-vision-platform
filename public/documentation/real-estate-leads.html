Real Estate · Leads, buyers & matching

/real-estate Estate Agent

This is the demand side of the agency: the people who want to buy, and the people who want to sell. It turns a public property inquiry into a tracked CRM lead; it records what each buyer is looking for and auto-matches your listings against those requirements; it can alert a buyer the moment a matching listing appears — by email and WhatsApp, but only where they've consented; and it captures vendor (seller) leads from valuations. Every lead is AI-scored on the same scale as the rest of your CRM pipeline.

Where: open /real-estate and use the Leads and Buyers tabs. Recent leads also surface on the Real Estate Overview. Because leads and buyer data live in the shared CRM, the same records appear on the relevant CRM contact pages.

The lead lifecycle: public inquiry → CRM lead

Everything starts with a published listing. Each property you publish gets its own public page with an inquiry form (see Listings & workbench for how listings are published to Discovery and your agency profile). When a visitor fills that form in, they submit their name, email, phone and a message — and they must tick a GDPR consent box before it can be sent. That submission is a property inquiry.

An inquiry lands in the Leads tab as buyer/seller interest against that specific property. From there you convert the inquiry to a CRM lead: the interest is promoted into a proper record in the shared CRM / sales pipeline, so it's tracked, assigned, scored and progressed exactly like any other lead the business handles — not stranded in a real-estate-only inbox.

  1. A visitor opens a published listing's public page and submits the inquiry form (name, email, phone, message, GDPR consent).
  2. The property inquiry appears under Leads, tied to that listing.
  3. You review it and convert the inquiry to a CRM lead, moving it into the shared sales pipeline.
  4. The lead is progressed in CRM — assigned, scored, and worked through your normal stages.

Buyers & buyer requirements

The Buyers tab is where you record what a buyer actually wants — a buyer requirement. Rather than a loose note, it's a structured profile of their search: budget, the area or town they're targeting, the property type, and how many bedrooms. These requirements can be recorded directly on the person's CRM contact page, so a contact's buying brief lives alongside the rest of their record (their properties, activity and history).

Auto-matching

You don't run matching by hand. Whenever a listing is created or edited, the system automatically checks it against every buyer's requirements. A new or updated property that fits a buyer's budget, area, type and bedroom criteria is surfaced as a match — so the moment a suitable property comes into your book, the buyers who want it are already identified.

Why record requirements carefully: the more precisely you capture a buyer's budget, area, type and bedrooms on their contact record, the more accurate the auto-match — and, if they've consented, the more relevant the alerts they receive.

New-listing alerts — direct to the buyer

Matching isn't only an internal signal. When a new listing matches a buyer's requirements, that buyer can be alerted directly — reaching them over email and WhatsApp. These alerts are sent through an operator Flow, so the delivery is automated and configurable rather than a manual send.

Crucially, this is GDPR-gated: only buyers who have consented are alerted. The consent captured on the original inquiry form is what authorises reaching out. The feature is opt-in and privacy-respecting by design — no consent, no direct alert.

Consent is the gate. Direct-to-buyer email/WhatsApp alerts only go to buyers who gave GDPR consent. Buyers without recorded consent are simply excluded from the alert Flow — auto-matching still surfaces them internally, but the platform won't message them.

The seller / vendor side

Not every lead is a buyer. A seller lead captures a vendor — someone who wants to sell or get a property valued. These commonly come from the instant valuation widget: a prospective seller runs a valuation, and that interest is captured as a seller lead you can follow up. The valuation side itself is documented under Viewings, offers & sales (instant AVM valuations).

Two demand streams, one place: buyer leads (from listing inquiries) and seller leads (from valuations) both feed the Leads area, giving you both sides of every potential transaction — the buyer looking, and the vendor with stock to sell.

AI lead scoring

Every lead is given an AI lead score. Rather than a real-estate-only metric, this is unified into the shared CRM / sales lead-scoring system — so a property lead is prioritised on the same scale, by the same logic, as every other lead across the business. The AI scoring is credit-metered: it consumes workspace credits like the platform's other AI features.

End to end: a consented public inquiry becomes a scored CRM lead; a well-captured buyer requirement earns automatic matches; and — where consent allows — the matching buyer hears about the property directly by email and WhatsApp, all without leaving the pipeline your team already works in.
