CRM · Categories

/crm?tab=categories crm.view

Categories are the labels you use to group the contacts and companies you work with into lists. They aren't just tidy tags: those lists are what the rest of the platform targets — the "Send to Customers" action on catalogs, email and WhatsApp campaigns and workflows all send to a category rather than to individuals — and they're a filter on the CRM lists. Your workspace's own Custom categories sit in the same table as the shared lists every workspace uses (industries and the lead vocabularies), each tagged with its type.

Where it is

Top navigation → CRM → the Categories tab. If you're starting fresh, it's worth creating a few categories first, so you have lists to file people and organisations under as you add them.

Two kinds of category

TypeWho sees itWho can change it
CustomOnly the members of your workspace — another workspace never sees your custom categories or who is in them.The workspace owner or an admin creates, edits and deletes them.
Industry, Lead status, Lead source sharedEvery workspace — they're the common vocabulary behind the Industry field and the Lead Status / Lead Source dropdowns.The platform operator only.

The platform operator also sees derived lists (Access role, Employment, Professional type) marked auto — their members are worked out from other records, never typed by hand. Ordinary members don't see them.

What's on the screen

One card with a filter bar and one table — your own categories first, then the shared lists.

ColumnWhat it shows

Above the table: the filter bar (with a Search categories box) and New category. If your workspace has no custom category yet, a one-line nudge offers Create your first.

How to create a category — step by step

  1. Open CRM → Categories and click New category.
  2. Enter a Name (for example VIP customers or Architects in Athens).
  3. Pick a Colour from the swatches — a preview chip shows how it will look.
  4. Optionally add a Description.
  5. Click Create. It's a Custom category, private to your workspace.

Editing adds an Active checkbox — untick it to retire a category without deleting it.

Create it where you need it. You don't have to come to this tab first: the category picker on a contact or company has a Create “…” option at the bottom — type a name that doesn't exist yet, pick a colour, and it's created and assigned in one go (owners and admins).

New pre-fills the filter. If you've filtered this table to one Type before clicking New category, the dialog opens with that type already chosen.

Adding people to a category

  • From the record — open a contact or company → Details → Categories. The Categories card is a searchable, colour-coded multi-select (“Add categories…”): tick the ones that apply.
  • From the category — click a row in this table to open its members. Search contacts and companies by name or email and press Add; the list underneath shows who's already in.
  • In bulk — select rows on the Contacts or Companies list and use Add to category.
  • From a filtered list — when the Contacts or Companies list is filtered to one category, a party you add there joins that category on first save.

Industries are the product-import categories

What it is — “what a supplier deals in” and “what a product is filed under when it's imported” are one vocabulary. Each shared Industry is matched to one of the catalogue's product categories (Tiles, Sanitary, Electrical, Plumbing & Pipework…), so tagging a supplier with Tiles and importing their tile catalogue use the same word. Why it matters — a hand-typed list next to the catalogue drifts (misspelt twins like “Sanity” split “who supplies sanitaryware” in half). An industry with no product category — haulage, scaffolding hire — is still legitimate and stays pickable under Other.

  • On a record — the What they deal in card (contact or company → Details → Categories) shows Product categories and Other as chips. On a company it also marks (with a package icon) the categories they already have products filed under, and warns when they have products in a category they aren't tagged with.
  • The Industry field in a company's sidebar edits the same industry list as What they deal in.
  • Operator tools — the operator sees which product category each industry matches, can change the match from Edit, and has Match product categories to bring new product categories in as Industry lists (an existing industry with the same meaning is adopted, keeping its members, rather than duplicated).

What categories affect

  • Send to Customers on catalogs — sends a catalog to a chosen category in one step.
  • Campaigns and workflows — email and WhatsApp campaigns and agent workflows pick their audience from your workspace's categories.
  • Filtering — every category is a filter on the Contacts and Companies lists.

Worked example — an "Architects in Athens" list to email a catalog. Open CRM → Categories → New category → name it Architects in Athens, pick a colour → Create. Click the new row, search for each architect and press Add (or tag them from their own pages). When the list is built, run Send to Customers on the catalog and pick Architects in Athens — every member receives it, and nobody outside it does.

Access is decided by a person's workspace role, not by category membership — categories are purely lists for outreach and filtering. For the records you file under them, see Contacts and Companies.
