Tools & Capabilities

Everything the AI assistant can do for you. This guide explains the toolkit model — how tools are loaded — and then teaches each capability group: what it is, why it matters, how to use it, and what it produces elsewhere. Ask in plain language, or type / to invoke a tool directly; results appear as interactive cards in the chat and, where relevant, as real artefacts in their module.

This page is a catalogue. You rarely need to name a tool — describe the outcome you want and the assistant picks the right one. Tools marked with a credit cost only charge when you confirm the action.

The toolkit model: Core + loadable toolkits

What it is — tools are grouped into toolkits. A Core toolkit is always loaded — the knowledge base, catalogue and visual essentials (around four tools) — and the assistant can load additional toolkits mid-conversation when a task needs them. Why it matters — keeping only Core loaded by default keeps the assistant fast and focused, while letting it reach deeper capabilities exactly when the work calls for them.

Examples of additional toolkits include a Projects toolkit and a suite of SEO research toolkits (SEO Research, SEO Domain Intel, SEO Backlinks, SEO Content & Tech, SEO Multi-Engine + Niche, SEO Composite Audits). Some toolkits are restricted to admin/owner roles.

How to manage toolkits

  1. Open the toolkit panel from the composer to see what's currently active.
  2. Enable additional toolkits there when you need them — or simply describe a task and let the assistant load the right toolkit on its own.

What it affects elsewhere: the toolkit you see may be larger or smaller than another user's, because access depends on your role and plan.

Search & discovery

Material & product search

What it is — the platform's multi-signal search (text + visual + an AI “understanding” of each item + colour/texture/style/material characteristics) to find materials and products by what they are and what they look like. Why it matters — it finds the right item even when you can't name it precisely, matching on appearance and meaning, not just keywords. How: ask things like “matte black porcelain tiles, large format” or “something like travertine but darker”. Affects: results render as product cards you can open, save to a moodboard, or refine further in the same thread.

Explainable search spec

What it is — a Search Spec card the assistant can show above the results — the colours (with swatches), materials, styles and specifications it used. Why it matters — it tells you why items matched, so you can trust the results and refine precisely. How: it appears automatically alongside a search; read it, then adjust your request to tighten the match. Affects: the same search results, made transparent and tunable.

Inspiration from a URL

What it is — paste any web page (a product page, a blog, a Pinterest pin) and the assistant turns it into a search. Why it matters — it lets a real-world reference drive your catalogue search instead of you having to describe the look in words. How: use the composer's 🌐 button to paste the URL; the assistant scrapes it, extracts the design tokens — colour palette with hex codes, materials, textures, style, room type — and runs a visual search for matching catalogue products. Affects: renders as an Inspiration card with the palette and hero image, plus the matched products.

Web & manufacturer search

What it is — search beyond your own catalogue. Why it matters — some questions need the wider web or a manufacturer/supplier you don't yet stock. How: ask a question that goes beyond your catalogue and the assistant searches the web and finds manufacturers/suppliers. Affects: returns summarised, sourced results in the chat.

Create & visualise

Image generation & editing

What it is — generate new visuals or restyle existing ones (e.g. re-render a room in a different style). Why it matters — it produces imagery on demand for concepts and deliverables. How: describe the image you want, or ask the assistant to edit one already in the thread. Affects: generated images can be saved to a moodboard or used in deliverables. (Metered — the assistant tells you the cost.)

Virtual staging (before / after)

What it is — turn an empty or dated room into a staged scene. Why it matters — it shows a space's potential without a real photoshoot. How: provide the room image and ask to stage it; results open in a before/after slider so you can compare source and staged image, and you can run an “Analyze Quality” pass. Affects: the quality pass scores lighting, perspective, scale, materials and edge-blending so you can judge the result.

Presentation sheets

What it is — client-ready A3 sheets attached to a moodboard — material boards, colour palettes, concept boards, lighting plans, annotated renders, elevation/render pairs, FF&E schedules, area breakdowns, and full decks. Why it matters — it produces polished deliverables straight from your work in chat. How: ask for the sheet type you want; interactive sheet types open an in-chat canvas so you can place symbols, draw dimensions or position callouts before rendering the PDF. Affects: the finished sheet attaches to the moodboard and appears in that module.

3D & VR worlds

What it is — generate an explorable 3D world (Gaussian-splat “Marble” world) from an image. Why it matters — it offers an immersive walkthrough that a flat image can't. How: provide an image and ask the assistant to generate the world. Affects: the world can be embedded into a project's Client View for an immersive walkthrough.

AR & lighting preview

What it is — preview materials in augmented reality and under different lighting conditions. Why it matters — it shows how a finish actually reads in a real space and across the day. How: view a swatch in your space (with a QR hand-off from desktop), and switch lighting conditions — daylight, golden hour, showroom spots, evening, night. Affects: helps you judge how a finish reads in context before committing.

Monitor & research

Price monitoring

What it is — track a product's price across retailers. Why it matters — it keeps you informed of where an item sells and how its price moves, automatically. How: ask the assistant to track a product; it discovers where it's sold, verifies live prices, and keeps watching on an adaptive schedule. Affects: you get min/median/max, a price history, and alerts on price drops, new retailers and promotions. (Try a free one-shot version under Free Tools → Price scan.)

Mention monitoring

What it is — track where a brand or product is talked about across news, blogs, RSS and YouTube. Why it matters — it surfaces reputation signals and competitive context you'd otherwise miss. How: ask the assistant to monitor a subject; it gathers mentions with sentiment, top outlets, and spike/negative-sentiment/new-outlet alerts. Affects: it also measures LLM visibility — whether (and how favourably) AI assistants recommend the subject, and which competitors get co-mentioned. (Free taster: Free Tools → Mention scan.)

Job research

What it is — a recurring job-discovery search across multiple sources with AI keyword expansion and relevance filtering. Why it matters — it automates ongoing discovery instead of repeated manual searching. How: set it up from chat — manage cadence, sources and exclusions all in the conversation. Affects: findings post back into your conversation and arrive as one consolidated daily (or weekly) digest.

Content & utilities

Catalogue & document translation

What it is — translate an uploaded source PDF/catalogue into another language for a branded output. Why it matters — it makes your catalogue usable for clients in other languages without re-authoring it. How: ask the assistant to translate the uploaded source. Affects: produces a branded output in the target language.

Content generation

What it is — draft product descriptions, summaries and other copy. Why it matters — it speeds up writing while staying accurate to your data. How: ask for the copy you need. Affects: the output is grounded in your catalogue data.

Save to moodboard

What it is — promote any image or product surfaced in chat into a moodboard. Why it matters — it preserves the work outside the conversation. How: save the item from its card. Affects: a copy is kept, so it survives even if the chat is deleted.

Invoking tools

  • Natural language — just describe the outcome; the assistant chooses the tool.
  • “/” menu — type / in the composer to pick a specific tool or workflow.
  • Composer buttons — attachments, voice and the inspiration-URL analyser are one click away.

Availability varies by role & plan. Core tools are open to everyone; some research, B2B and SEO tools are restricted to higher roles or plans, and module-gated tools (e.g. price/mention/job monitoring) require the relevant module to be enabled for your workspace.
