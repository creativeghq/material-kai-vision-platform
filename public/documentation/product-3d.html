3D, textures & options on a product

/admin/materials-data

Everything the platform can show a customer about a product — a 3D model they can turn, a material they can light, finishes they can pick, a room they can place it in, and an embed on someone else's website — comes from a handful of things you set on the product itself. This page is what they are, in the order worth doing them.

Where: the catalogue at /admin/materials-data → Products → click a product. The cards below live on the product's 3D tab, and only for products in your own workspace.

Start with the checklist

The Ready to sell online card at the top tells you what this product still needs, and — more usefully — what stops working without each thing. The catalogue list carries the same number in its Sellable online column, so you can see at a glance which products are finished.

Nothing here blocks selling. A product with none of it still reaches customers — the spec builder captures them as a quote request, and the assistant can price or log the enquiry. The checklist says what is missing and why it matters, never "you may not publish this".

When a key covers the product, the same card hands you the snippet, pre-filled and ready to paste.

Upload a 3D model

Drop a .glb or .gltf on the 3D Model card. It is measured on the way in — real width, height and depth in metres, read from the file — and those measurements are what the room planner and AR use to draw the product at its true size. You never type them.

Once a model exists, the card also offers Place in a room, which opens the Room Planner with the product already placed.

Android AR works from the .glb. iPhone AR (Quick Look) needs a USDZ, which the platform does not produce and does not accept — so an iPhone gets the 3D viewer rather than a button that leads nowhere.

Material texture — for things that are a surface

A tile, a fabric or a wood floor is the surface, so it never has a 3D model. For those, the Material texture card turns the product's own photo into a seamless tile the AR and lighting previews can render as a real surface you can light and orbit.

It is derived from your photo, not invented: the generator flattens that product into a tileable version of itself rather than imagining a material the customer will not receive. Two providers are offered — generate with both and keep whichever is closer. Each costs credits.

Once you pick one, + relief derives a normal map from it. That is what makes light fall into a weave or a grout line instead of sliding across a flat picture of one. It is computed from the texture, costs nothing, and is instant.

Without a texture, the material preview stretches the raw product photo across a plane. It repeats visibly and carries the lighting from the day it was shot — the preview says so, rather than passing it off as a material.

Options — finishes a customer can pick

An option group targets one material name inside the 3D model, so choosing "Emerald Velvet" repaints every part that uses that material at once — one choice, four chair legs. The editor lists the model's own material names, so a target cannot be mistyped into something that matches nothing.

Each value can carry a colour, a roughness and a metalness. It can also carry its own texture: without one, a velvet and a linen at the same colour render identically, because three numbers cannot tell them apart. Use the For selector on the Material texture card to generate a texture per option value.

Prices follow automatically. An option's price difference is added by the same pricing rules the rest of the platform uses, and the total is worked out server-side — the configurator never does arithmetic on money.
