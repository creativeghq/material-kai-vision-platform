Quotes & Requests

/quotes /quotes/:id /quotes/requests

A quote is how you turn the products and ideas you've gathered into a priced, client-ready document — and a quote in MaterialsHub does more than show a total. It captures the full FF&E specification for each item, renders a branded PDF, and produces a secure link your customer can open and accept without ever logging in. This guide teaches the whole flow: what a quote is and why it works the way it does, how to build and send one end to end, and what every field and action affects downstream.

How to open it: top navigation → Quotes. Use the My Quotes and Requests tabs at the top.

What a quote is — and why it's built this way

At its simplest a quote is a list of line items with quantities and prices, and a total the platform calculates for you. But the same document is designed to carry the specification detail your customer needs to actually make a decision — the room each item is for, its dimensions, how it gets installed, and when it lands on site. That extra detail is what turns a price list into a proper furniture, fixtures & equipment (FF&E) schedule, and it's why the quote can be both your sales document and your spec sheet at once.

Everything you enter flows into two customer-facing outputs: a branded PDF and a public, token-protected web page the customer can open and accept. Because both are generated from the same quote, you only ever maintain one source of truth — edit the quote, and the PDF and the public page reflect it the next time they're opened.

The two tabs

Quotes is split into two views you switch between at the top of the page. They answer two different questions: "what have I sent?" and "what's come in that I need to price?"

  • My Quotes /quotes — everything you've created, with a KPI strip and status filtering. This is where you spend most of your time.
  • Requests /quotes/requests — quote requests that have come in from customers, waiting to be reviewed and converted into real quotes.

How to build and send a quote (end to end)

Here is the whole flow once, top to bottom. The deep subsections that follow expand each step — start here to see how the pieces connect, then drop into the detail you need.

  1. Open Quotes and click New Quote. From the top navigation → Quotes, then New Quote.
  2. Name it, and optionally link a project. Give the quote a name (it defaults to "Untitled Quote" and can be renamed inline later) and optionally add notes and link it to a project so it's grouped with related work.
  3. Add line items. On the Items tab, add each item as either a catalogue product or a custom product, and set its quantity and price. The total recalculates as you go.
  4. Capture FF&E specs per item. Where it helps the customer, add room, dimensions, installation requirements and delivery date so the quote doubles as a spec schedule.
  5. Add Extras and a Timeline (optional). Use the Extras tab for add-on lines and the Timeline tab for a visual delivery/milestone plan the customer will see.
  6. Preview the branded PDF. Open Preview /quotes/:id/preview to check exactly what the customer will receive.
  7. Send it. Use Email Quote or Share to deliver the quote.
  8. The customer accepts. They open the token-protected public page /q/:token Public with no login and accept it. After acceptance the work can flow into your Finance & Invoicing.

My Quotes — your working list

What it is — the main tab lists every quote you've created, newest first. Why it matters — it's your pipeline at a glance: the KPI strip across the top tells you where your quotes stand without opening any of them, and it updates as you work.

MetricWhat it counts

What you can do here

  • New Quote — start a quote from scratch.
  • Filter by status — narrow the list (e.g. Draft, Pending, Accepted, Rejected, Expired). The filter has a small search box so you can jump straight to a status; the active filter is shown next to the list title.
  • Open a quote — click any row to open its detail page /quotes/:id and continue editing, previewing or sending.

Creating and naming a quote

What it is — the New Quote dialog is the starting point for a quote from scratch. Why it matters — the name is how you'll recognise the quote in your list (and it appears on the document), and linking it to a project keeps related quotes, moodboards and work together. How: give it a name, optionally add notes and pick a project, then create it — you land on the quote's detail page to build it out. The title stays editable inline afterwards (it defaults to "Untitled Quote" if you don't set one).

What it affects: the detail page is the hub for everything that follows — line items, Extras, Timeline, preview and sending.

Shortcut — generate from a moodboard. Open a moodboard and use its Generate Quote button. The products on the board become the line items of a new quote, ready for you to set quantities, prices and FF&E detail.

Line items — the heart of the quote

What it is — line items are the priced rows that make up the quote, built on the Items tab. Why it matters — they are what the customer pays for and what the total is calculated from; each row also carries the spec detail that makes the quote useful as a schedule.

The items table has these columns: #, Product, Room, Qty, Price, Unit, Total and Actions. You can edit Room and Qty inline per row, and remove rows via Actions. You add items with Add Products, which offers two modes — Catalog Search and Custom Product. The two modes exist for two genuinely different situations, so it's worth knowing when to reach for each.

Catalog Search — for materials already in your knowledge base

What it is — a search across your existing catalogue. When to use it — whenever the item is a real product you've already added to MaterialsHub. Why — it reuses the data you've already captured (name, brand, material, colour and any imagery), so the line is consistent with the rest of your catalogue and you don't retype anything. How: search by name, brand, material or colour, tick the items you want, and confirm — the button shows "Add N Products" for the number you've selected.

Custom Product — for one-off items that aren't in the catalogue

What it is — a form where you type an item in by hand. When to use it — for anything that isn't (and won't be) a catalogue product: a bespoke piece, a one-off supply, a subcontracted item. Why — it lets you put a fully-specified line on the quote — including FF&E detail — without having to first create a permanent catalogue entry. How: fill the fields below and click "Add Custom Product". Every field maps to something the customer sees on the quote or the PDF, so they're worth understanding rather than skipping:

FF&E specifications per item — and where they end up

What it is — the fit-out detail you can add to any item so the quote doubles as an FF&E schedule. Why it matters — these fields are exactly what a contractor, installer or client needs to act on the quote, and they're surfaced specifically on the PDF so nothing gets lost. You can add them to catalogue and custom items alike. Each field has a clear job:

  • Room — where the item belongs (the space it's specified for). It becomes a dedicated Room column on the PDF items table, so the schedule reads room by room.
  • Dimensions — the size detail for the item. On the PDF, dimensions are appended to the product name so the size travels with the item wherever it appears.
  • Installation requirements — what's needed to fit or install it. This is pulled into the PDF's "Specifications & Delivery" section.
  • Delivery date — when it's expected on site. Also collected into the "Specifications & Delivery" section so the customer can see timing per item.

How: add the detail on the item, then expand the line to review its detail row — notes, installation requirements and delivery — so you can check everything before sending without cluttering the main table.

What it affects: the branded PDF. The Room column, the dimensions-in-name treatment, and the "Specifications & Delivery" section are all driven directly by these fields — fill them in and the schedule writes itself.

Extras & Timeline

Beyond the Items tab, a quote has two further tabs that round out what the customer sees.

Extras (Extras & Add-ons)

What it is — optional add-on lines beyond the main items. Why it matters — it lets you present upsells or optional extras without mixing them into the core item list, so the customer can see what's included versus what's optional. What it affects: the add-on section presented alongside the main items.

Timeline (Project Timeline)

What it is — a visual delivery/milestone timeline. Why it matters — it sets expectations on when things happen, which is often as decisive as price for a customer choosing to accept. What it affects: a timeline shown to the customer on the quote.

Quote actions — sending and finalising

What it is — the buttons on the quote header that take the quote out to the customer. Why it matters — this is the hand-off: each action produces a different way for the customer to receive and act on the quote.

  • Submit Quote Request — submit the quote.
  • Share — produce a shareable public link (token-protected, /q/:token).
  • Email Quote — email it to the customer.
  • Preview — preview the branded PDF (also at /quotes/:id/preview).
  • Download PDF — download the generated PDF.

The quote title is editable inline (it defaults to "Untitled Quote").

Previewing the branded PDF

What it is — a branded PDF preview at /quotes/:id/preview. Why it matters — it shows you exactly what your customer will receive, so you can catch anything missing before you send. The PDF is laid out for your customer and reflects everything you entered:

  • An items table that includes a Room column, with each item's dimensions appended to the product name.
  • A dedicated “Specifications & Delivery” section that pulls together the per-item notes, installation requirements and delivery dates.
  • Your company branding, totals and any applicable VAT, applied from your workspace settings.

How: open Preview as often as you like while drafting — it always shows the current state of the quote. What it affects: nothing changes by previewing; it's a read-only view of the live quote.

Sending & acceptance — what the customer sees

What it is — when you send a quote, your customer receives a link to a public, token-protected page at /q/:token Public. Why it matters — there's no friction for the customer: they don't need an account, and the link is unique to that quote so only people you share it with can see it. How it works for them: they open the page with no login required, review the items, specifications and totals, and accept it directly from that page. For more on these shareable surfaces, see Public & Shared Pages.

Once a customer accepts, the quote can flow into your back office — see Finance & Invoicing for turning accepted work into invoices and tracking what you're owed.

Requests — and converting them into quotes

What it is — the Requests tab /quotes/requests collects quote requests that come in from customers. Each request is something a customer has asked you to price. Why it matters — it's the front door for inbound demand: instead of starting from a blank quote, you start from what the customer actually asked for. How:

  • Review a request to see what the customer is after.
  • Convert it into a quote — start a real quote pre-populated from the request, then add line items, prices and FF&E specifications as usual.
  • Filter the list to focus on what still needs attention.

What it affects: converting a request creates a normal quote that then follows the exact build-and-send flow above — the request just gives you a head start on its contents.

Step by step: build and send a quote

  1. Open Quotes from the top navigation, then click New Quote.
  2. Give the quote a name, and optionally add notes and link it to a project.
  3. Add line items — pick catalogue products or add custom items — and set the quantity and price for each.
  4. For each item, optionally fill in the FF&E specs: room, dimensions, installation requirements and delivery date.
  5. Open Preview /quotes/:id/preview to check the branded PDF.
  6. Use Email Quote or Share to send it to your customer.
  7. The customer opens the token-protected public page /q/:token Public with no login and accepts it. See Public & Shared Pages, and Finance & Invoicing for what happens after acceptance.

Step by step: turn a request into a quote

  1. Open Quotes and switch to the Requests tab /quotes/requests.
  2. Open a request from the list.
  3. Review what the customer is asking for.
  4. Create a quote from it — a real quote pre-populated from the request.
  5. Continue as above: add line items, prices, FF&E specs, preview, then send.

Step by step: quote from a moodboard

  1. Open a moodboard.
  2. Click Generate Quote.
  3. The board's products become the line items of a new quote.
  4. Finish in Quotes: set quantities, prices and FF&E detail, then preview and send.

Quotes is a module-gated area. Administrators have additional configuration that shapes how quotes look and behave — status tags, upsells, timeline steps, PDF templates and company/VAT settings — managed in the admin area. That configuration is outside the scope of this end-user guide.
