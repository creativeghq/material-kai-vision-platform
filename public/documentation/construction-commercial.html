Construction · Commercial & Cost Control

/projects/:id?tab=finance owner · finance.manage

The Finance section of a project's Commercial stage is the contractor's cost desk: the priced bill of quantities that becomes the contract sum, variations and the cost-value reconciliation that reads them, applications for payment with retention, job cost and labour against the plan, and stock held for the job. Every figure on this screen is derived by the platform from your real data — the cards format and total, they never re-multiply — so the numbers reconcile by construction. (Tenders → subcontracts live here too and have their own page.)

Where: a project → the Commercial stage → the Finance section (?tab=finance). It needs the project owner plus finance access (the Finance module + finance.manage).

Bill of Quantities (the priced schedule)

What it is — a schedule of described items, each with a unit, quantity and rate; the line amount is computed by the platform, never typed. The one rule that matters: a project has at most one contract schedule, and once it's accepted it IS the contract sum — from then on the CVR measures against it and ignores accepted quotes. You build it from typed lines and/or the takeoff.

  • An unpriced line shows “—”, never 0.00 (which would understate the sum), and Accept is disabled until every line is priced.
  • Provisional sums are flagged and totalled separately, so a guess isn't mistaken for a firm price.
  • Lifecycle: draft → issued → accepted → superseded, plus Make contract to mark which schedule is the contract one.

Takeoff — from a drawing's printed schedules

Takeoff reads the schedules a drawing already prints (door schedules, window schedules, finishes) into proposed BoQ lines. Its defining rule: it transcribes, never measures — every proposed row cites the schedule row it came from, and it will refuse to measure geometry off a plan. Proposed rows arrive unticked; you tick the ones you've checked against the sheet and add them to a schedule. A quantity the sheet didn't state stays “not stated” (never 0), and the rate comes in blank — so a takeoff lands in the BoQ as an honest, unpriced gap to close. It's reached from a drawing's Read printed schedules action (Delivery → Documents) and costs a credit.

Cost codes — the trade classification

Cost codes are the workspace's trade taxonomy that every cost and priced line hangs off. There's no platform default — you seed yours with Install starter library or type your own; codes form a hierarchy and are archived, not deleted (so history holds). A code is attached on priced-schedule lines, tender items, variations, supplier bills, expenses and timesheets — and the project's Cost by code report (and the CVR below) group by it. Manage them in Finance → Settings → Cost Codes (workspace-level, owner/admin).

Job cost & labour

Job cost shows what the job has cost by cost code, with margin. Labour compares two numbers honestly:

  • At the typed rate — hours logged × the rate on the time entry.
  • Payroll actual — what payroll says those people actually cost (gross + employer contributions).
  • Variance — the difference.

Where a worker on the job can't be costed (not on the HR roster, or payroll hasn't run yet), the actual and variance read “Not comparable”, never €0 — and the card states its coverage (“covers Xh of Yh; Zh could not be costed”) with the reason. The payroll actual is an allocation, so it never overwrites the estimate and this card doesn't feed the P&L.

Variations & CVR (cost-value reconciliation)

Variations are scope changes, kept in one register but never netted: a client variation is money in, a subcontractor variation is money out, and the value is signed — a negative is an omission (work taken out), stored as-is. Only approved variations reach the CVR; pending ones are called out as “not yet decided, so not in the CVR.”

The CVR reconciles, per cost code, what a job is worth against what it has cost: columns for Contracted, Variations, Value, Actual (cost), Committed (open orders/subcontracts) and Margin. Because every figure comes from one derivation, the reconciliation cannot disagree with the cards it draws from; an uncoded bucket is explained rather than hidden, and a null margin reads as no-percentage (distinct from 0%).

Applications for payment & retention

Applications are interim valuations: each states the cumulative value of work done to date, and what's due is the difference from what was certified before. You raise one with a valued-to date and the cumulative figure, then record what was certified (the payer's answer — the variance is recorded, not hidden), then Mark paid. A certified amount counts as outstanding, not settled.

  • Retention — the percentage and its cap are frozen when an application is raised. What's held is derived from the latest application (one source, so one answer).
  • Release — “retention is let go of, not just held”: set up the two standard tranches — half at practical completion, half at the end of the defects period (dates derived from the project's terms) — and Release each when due. The footer keeps a running Certified to date / Retention held / Outstanding / Retention released.

One fiscal boundary. An application is a commercial claim; a myDATA tax document is a fiscal act. The two meet at a single, explicit point (an application can be marked invoiced) — there's no second path and no netting, keeping the contract ledger and the fiscal ledger cleanly separate.

Stock held for the job

The stock held card shows the material reserved for this job — derived from the job's quote and sales order rather than being a new kind of reservation you maintain by hand.

From chat: the assistant can read this cost picture — the CVR, applications and variations — but it never raises a variation, certifies a valuation or releases retention. Those stay human decisions.
