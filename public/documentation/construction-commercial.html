Construction · Commercial & Cost Control

/projects/:id?tab=quotes ?tab=changes ?tab=billing ?tab=purchases ?tab=finance

The Commercial stage of a project answers one money question per section: what we charge (Quotes), what changed (Changes), what is coming in (Invoicing), what is going out (Purchases) and whether the job makes money (Costs). Every figure is derived by the platform from your real records — the cards format and total, they never re-multiply — so the sections cannot disagree with each other or with the Money card on the Overview. Contracts sit in the same stage.

Where: a project → the Commercial stage. Invoicing and Costs need the project owner or a manager plus finance access (the Sales & Finance module and finance.manage). In Purchases, only a finance viewer sees the amounts. The construction features — valuations & retention, tender packages and CVR columns — appear on Construction Contract projects, or wherever they are switched on under Sections.

Quotes — what we charge

The project's quotes, grouped by revision. A quote made here is filed under the project, so its budget tracks against actual. A drawing takeoff reaches the customer through a quote line (see Takeoff): when a newer drawing revision changes an area a line was measured from, the Quotes section warns “A quote still uses an older drawing revision” and offers Review in Takeoff. A person chooses to update the quantity, and only on a draft quote — nothing changes a quoted figure by itself.

Changes — the one variations register

Variations are scope changes, kept in one register — whether typed in or recorded by a change order on the estimate — and never netted: a client variation is money in, a subcontractor variation is money out, and the value is signed (a negative is an omission). Each has a title, origin, value, extra days, a raised-on date and a cost code, and can be Approved, Rejected or Withdrawn. Only approved variations reach the contract value and the CVR.

Invoicing — money coming in

The project's invoices, its sales orders and — on construction projects — Applications for Payment. An invoice raised elsewhere can be linked to the project, or detached from it.

Applications for payment & retention

Applications are interim valuations: each states the gross value to date, and what is due is the difference from what was certified before. Raise one with New application (a Valued To date, the cumulative figure and a Payment Due By date), then record what was certified (the payer's answer — any difference is recorded, not hidden), issue an invoice for the valuation, and mark it paid. A certified amount counts as outstanding, not settled.

  • Retention — the percentage and its cap are frozen when an application is raised; what is held is derived from the latest application.
  • Retention release — the two standard tranches, half at practical completion and half at the end of the defects period, each released when due.

One fiscal boundary. An application is a commercial claim; a myDATA document is a fiscal act. They meet at one explicit point — issuing the invoice for a valuation — so the contract ledger and the tax ledger stay separate.

Purchases — money going out

Everything going out on the job, from what still needs ordering to what is owed:

  • Made-to-order items — joinery, doors and windows still to be ordered, with the supplier sheet.
  • Purchase orders — with separate delivered and billed columns, so a delivery that has not been invoiced yet is visible.
  • Tender packages on construction projects — send a package out, compare bids, award.
  • Supplier bills and expenses charged to the project; a bill linked by mistake can be detached.

Signed approvals

If your workspace has an approval rule for Project Variation, Project Application or Project Purchase Order, approving a variation, certifying an application or confirming a project purchase order is refused until the approval is signed. The dialog raises the approval request for you — once; pressing again does not raise a duplicate.

Costs — is the job making money

A read-only cost report with three cards:

  • Job cost — margin on what was sold and the forecast against what the client agreed: Contracted, Revenue, Supplier Bills, Cost of Goods on Orders, Labour, Expenses (Pending Claims are listed but not counted yet), On Order and Forecast Margin. Its labour part compares hours At the Typed Rate with the Payroll Actual and shows the Variance; a worker who cannot be costed reads “Not comparable”, never zero.
  • Cost codes — per code: Budget, Spent, On Order, Draft Orders (when any), To Complete, Forecast and Budget Variance; on a construction project also Value and Margin (the cost-value reconciliation). Set a budget with Set budget; costs with no code show as Not coded rather than disappearing. Behind the numbers lists the records each figure comes from.
  • Stock held for this job — material reserved for the job, derived from its quote and sales order.

Cost codes — the trade classification

Cost codes are the workspace's trade taxonomy that every cost and priced line hangs off. There's no platform default — you seed yours with Install starter library or type your own; codes form a hierarchy and are archived, not deleted (so history holds). A code is attached on tender items, variations, supplier bills, expenses and timesheets — and the project's Cost by code report (and the CVR below) group by it. Manage them in Finance → Settings → Cost Codes (workspace-level, owner/admin).

From chat: the assistant can read this cost picture — the CVR, applications and variations — but it never raises a variation, certifies a valuation or releases retention. Those stay human decisions.
