Room Planner

/room-planner

The Room Planner is a top-down floor-plan planner, driven entirely by your own product catalogue. You draw a room to real dimensions, drop catalogue products onto it at their true size, apply finishes (flooring, paint, tiles) to the walls and floor, and then turn the whole arrangement into a quote in one click — with the surface quantities worked out for you. It's a standalone design surface, not a tab inside Projects or MoodBoards, and it's free to use.

Where: the Room Planner item in the Studio area of the navigation (alongside MoodBoards and Interior Design), at /room-planner. It's part of your workspace — no extra module, no credits.

Two ways in

  • From the menu — open Room Planner and start a blank room.
  • From a product — a product that has a 3D model shows a “Place in a room” link, which opens the planner with that product already dropped in the middle of the room. (The link only appears when the product has a model, because without one the planner can't place it to scale.)

The room

A layout starts as a rectangular room you size in metres — width, depth and height. Everything on the canvas is drawn to that scale, so what you see is what will fit. You can keep several named layouts, each its own room.

Placing products — objects at real size

Add a product from your catalogue and it drops onto the floor at its real footprint. On the 2D canvas you:

  • Drag to position (it snaps to a grid and stays inside the room; the move is saved when you let go).
  • Rotate in 45° steps and delete from the controls on the selected item.
  • See overlaps flagged in red, so two things can't silently occupy the same spot.

“Measured” vs “placeholder” size. An item's footprint is taken from its 3D model's real dimensions where it has one, or an explicit size override, otherwise a neutral default — and the planner labels which. A product with no model is shown at an assumed size marked placeholder, so you never mistake a default box for a real measurement.

The 3D view

Toggle to a 3D walkthrough of the same layout — the products rendered from their models (ones without a model appear as simple placeholder boxes). You still arrange in 2D; 3D is for seeing it. You can pick and save a lighting preset, because a room reads very differently under different light.

Surfaces — finishes you apply, not place

A tile, a paint or a wallcovering has no footprint — you apply it to a face of the room rather than dropping it on the floor. The Surfaces panel lists the room's six faces — floor, ceiling and the four walls — and for each you pick a catalogue product (or leave it as None).

The quantity is worked out for you. Each applied surface's ordered area is derived from the room's dimensions — you never type square metres — and it automatically adds +10% for waste (cuts and breakage). So a floor finish on a 4×5 m room orders the right amount without a hand calculation.

Configurable products

If a product has options (a finish, a fabric, a colour), you configure it with swatches that repaint the model live and update the price as you pick — the price is always re-checked on the server, never guessed in the browser, and any invalid combination is blocked before it can be added. Because materials render from real AI-generated textures (not just a flat colour), a velvet and a linen in the same navy actually look different on screen. The merchant sets these options up on the product; see the embed configurator for the same engine on your own website.

Turning the plan into a quote

When the room has products or surfaces on it, Add to quote does the commercial part:

  • It adds every item and surface to your most-recent open quote, or — if you have none — creates a new quote named after the room, so you're never asked “which quote?”.
  • Prices are read and frozen onto the lines together; repeated items collapse into one line with a quantity.
  • You land on the quote ready to send.

“Call for price” lines. If a product has no price the resolver can use, it's still added — as a call-for-price line that contributes nothing to the total (rather than being dropped or erroring). The confirmation tells you how many lines came through that way, so a quote that looks finished but is missing prices doesn't slip out under-quoted. Fill those prices, or send them as an RFQ.

How it relates to Projects & MoodBoards

The Room Planner is its own thing, deliberately separate from:

  • Projects → Rooms — a budgeting unit inside a project (name, type, budget, deadline), not a scale layout.
  • Technical Plans (the electrical/plumbing/lighting sheets) — services drawings, not a catalogue layout.

The Room Planner is the one that arranges real products to scale and turns them into a priced quote.

Worked example — a living room. Open Room Planner → set the room to 5 × 4 m → Add a sofa and an armchair (they drop in at their real size), drag them into place, rotate the sofa to face the window. In Surfaces, set the floor to an oak plank and a wall to a paint — the areas (plus 10% waste) are computed for you. Configure the sofa's fabric with the swatches. Click Add to quote → a quote named after the room opens with the furniture and the finishes on it, ready to send.
