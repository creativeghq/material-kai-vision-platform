Connected Websites & Search Console

/profile?tab=websites /websites/:id

Connecting a website is the foundation of the whole SEO feature. You point the platform at your site, it reads your sitemap, fetches each page and builds a semantic index of your content, without ever publishing or changing anything on your site. Each connected site then gets its own page: a dashboard that gathers your search performance (Google Search Console), your audience (Google Analytics), your rankings, your visibility in AI assistants, your articles and your technical health. Every section says plainly when a figure is unknown rather than showing a zero.

Where: your Profile → Websites (/profile?tab=websites) lists your sites. Click a site to open its own page at /websites/:id, with breadcrumbs back to My Profile › Websites. The list is open to any signed-in user; the paid research sections need the SEO Toolkit add-on, and crawling spends credits.

The website list

The Your Websites card lists each site with Status (Indexed, No pages found, Crawl failed with the error shown under the URL, or Not crawled), Pages and Last crawl. On each row:

  • Click the row to open the website's page. Older links that used ?website= redirect there.
  • The recrawl icon runs Recrawl now (full).
  • The More menu has Preview (sample 5 pages), Edit and Remove. Remove deletes the site and all its indexed pages.

Adding a website

Click Add website and fill in:

FieldWhat to enter
Website URL *Your domain, e.g. https://yourdomain.com.

Preview before the full crawl

The crawler is charged per page read, so saving with Add & Preview doesn't crawl everything straight away. It opens a Sitemap Preview showing Total URLs, Will crawl up to, and Sample success for 5 sample pages, so you can check that titles and descriptions come through. A warning appears if the sitemap holds more pages than your cap. If it looks right, click Proceed with full crawl. You never spend credits crawling a site whose sitemap was wrong.

After that the crawl keeps the index fresh on its own. It reads the pages search demand points to first, then uses spare capacity on each run to work through sitemap pages it hasn't read yet. The article writer also reads unread pages on its topic before writing.

The website page

The header shows the domain, the page count and last crawl, with Visit and Recrawl. A failed crawl shows as a red banner. Four counters (Articles, Keyword research, Toolkit runs, Tracked domains) each open their section. A rail on the left (a strip on a phone) groups the sections. The open one is in the address as ?section=…, so any section can be bookmarked or linked.

A figure is a number, or the reason there isn't one

What it is — every tile on these pages either shows a number or says why it can't. Why it matters — "we couldn't fetch your backlinks" and "you have no backlinks" are very different, and a zero would hide which one you're looking at. Where a number would be, you'll see:

WordMeaning
NoneThe source answered and genuinely has nothing for this site yet.
Unknown (amber)The source failed, so the figure is unknown. It is not zero. A refused data-provider call reads this way too.
Not measuredNever collected for this site. Run it to fill it in.
Not connectedNeeds a connection (e.g. Search Console) before any data can arrive.
Not availableThe source doesn't report this until it's set up (e.g. ecommerce events in Analytics).

Overview

  • What to do next — one ranked list of actions, built from Search Console, the crawl, your articles and your competitors. Each row says why it's there and is tagged Quick win, Refresh, Consolidate, New page, Not crawled, Crawled, not indexed or Fix page. Its button takes you to the place to act: Write it starts a new article with the keyword filled in. Data sources that are missing are listed underneath.
  • Search presence — domain rank, organic keywords, estimated monthly traffic, traffic value, referring domains, backlinks, broken backlinks and spam score. Use Take first snapshot or Refresh; it warns when the figures are stale.
  • Ranking profile and Top ranking keywords — where your keywords sit, and what improved, declined, appeared or was lost week on week.
  • Summary cards for Search Console, AI assistants and Technical health, each with a button into the full section.

Search Console — real search performance

What it is — a direct, read-only connection to your property in Google Search Console, so you see the numbers Google actually reports rather than third-party estimates. It also powers the assistant's striking-distance and top-movers tools, the What to do next list and each article's search card.

How to connect

  1. Open the website → Search Console → Connect Search Console.
  2. Grant read-only access at Google. You come back to the website's Search Console section.
  3. If it can't match a property to your domain, choose one under Pick a Search Console Property (Load my properties).
  4. Sync pulls on demand. The disconnect icon stops the connection and keeps your history.

Operator setup required. Search Console uses Google sign-in, so a platform operator must have configured the Google client credentials. If they haven't, Connect tells you so instead of failing silently.

What it shows

  • A Day / Week / Month / Quarter range, and four tiles: Clicks, Impressions, Avg CTR and Avg position.
  • A clicks trend, a Devices split, and a Breakdown table with Queries, Pages, Countries and Appearance tabs. Queries can be filtered by Page and Segment (all countries and devices, one device, or one country).
  • Drill down: click a query to see the pages Google showed for it, with a warning when several pages split the impressions, which is a cannibalisation signal. Click a page to see the queries that brought it impressions, then Filter Queries by this page.

Reporting lag. Search Console data lands 2–3 days behind, so the most recent days may look sparse. That's Google's lag, not a sync problem.

Cannibalisation — your pages competing with each other

Pages competing with each other lists the queries where Google shows more than one of your pages, with a severity, impressions, the number of pages and the best position. It flags when Google's main page isn't the one earning the clicks. Filter by severity or click split and sort as you like. Consolidate lets you pick the page to keep and copies the redirects for the others (Apache .htaccess format).

Rank Tracker — the keywords you chose, followed daily

What it is — the Rankings card follows a fixed set of keywords you pick and checks each one's Google position every day. Why it matters — it's the one section that answers "are the keywords I care about moving up or down?" Pro add-on

  • Add keywords — one per line or comma-separated. Choose the device (Desktop / Mobile) and the location (All of GR or one city), then Track these. Keywords are checked daily in Greek results. Check now runs a check immediately. Change device or location on a row moves an existing keyword.
  • Tiles — Visibility (share of tracked keywords in the top 10), Avg. position, Ranking (how many rank, how many are outside the top 100) and Could not check. A failed check is unknown, not unranked, and neither failures nor unranked keywords distort the averages.
  • Visibility over time, the distribution across bands (Top 3 · 4–10 · 11–30 · 31–100 · Not ranking), and the Tracked keywords table with position, change, trend and ranking page. You can filter it by country, device, location and source.
  • Found in Search Console — keywords your site already shows up for, with a verdict (Earning clicks, Recurring, Near page one, Below the bar). Switch on Add qualifying keywords automatically to have them tracked up to a ceiling you set. Stopping tracking a keyword also stops it being suggested back.

Drops are surfaced. When keywords fall out of the top 10, the platform raises a Flow event to owners and admins.

SERP Features

For your tracked keywords: which search features appear on the results page (AI Overview, featured snippet, People Also Ask, image pack…), whether each one cites you, and which keywords show the feature without you. A feature you're absent from is a finding, not a gap in the list. Counters show how many keywords were Checked, are Unknown (the latest check failed) or were Never checked. Refresh re-reads them.

Backlinks

From the backlink index: Backlinks, Referring domains, Referring main domains, Domain rank, Spam score and Broken backlinks, plus a Referring domains list (linking site, its rank, anchor, which of your pages it links to, first seen). Use Snapshot now or Refresh. If the list can't be fetched it says Unknown, not zero. (Ranking keywords used to sit here; they're now on Overview.)

Competitors

A one-year chart of you against your rivals, switchable between organic traffic, keywords, traffic value, referring domains and domain rank. Under Tracked domains, rivals are measured on the same weekly run and in the same market as you. Some are suggested; add your own with Add competitor. Their keywords are kept, and feed the New page ideas in What to do next.

Domain Audits

Weekly tracking of your own domain. Click Track this domain to start. It shows rank, organic traffic and the last audit, plus a history of each audit (rank, keywords, traffic, referring domains, backlinks, spam, cost).

Keyword Research

Your saved research runs: target keyword, topic, volume, difficulty, CPC, competition, opportunity, trend and more. Each row has a Track icon; New research starts one. Opening a run shows its Keyword clusters, which you can Save as plan into the Topic plan. If a page of yours already ranks for the keyword, it warns you before you write a competing article. The Asked of AI tile (Check AI volume) shows how often the term is asked of AI assistants.

Keyword ideas for one of your pages

  1. In Suggested from your pages, type part of a page title or URL and pick the page.
  2. You immediately see, for free, Queries Google already shows this page for (90 days) from your Search Console data.
  3. Get keyword ideas (paid) adds Keyword ideas around this page's title: volume, CPC and competition.

Every row has Research and Track buttons. Below, Page titles not yet tracked or researched lists pages you haven't worked on yet.

Site Crawl

Full site crawl audits your pages for technical problems. Click Crawl the site (later Crawl again). It shows pages read, an on-page score, issues (with the change since the last crawl) and pages affected. Issues are grouped: Cannot be indexed, Broken links, Redirect chains, Duplicate titles or descriptions, Duplicate content, Pages returning an error. Each group has a Fix: line and Show all. Pages that couldn't be fetched are flagged as unknown, not clean.

Page Health

The Site Health card runs a Lighthouse (Core Web Vitals) and on-page audit of your homepage. Use Run check or Re-check. It shows On-page SEO, Performance, Accessibility and Best practices gauges, and What we checked, group by group. An audit that returned no data shows as Audit failed with the cause, never as a clean score.

llms.txt

Builds an llms.txt file from your crawled pages, leaving out pages that return errors or have left the sitemap. Use Rebuild, Copy or Download, then host it yourself at /llms.txt. The platform never writes to your site.

Toolkit Runs and Reports

  • Toolkit Runs — a log of the research and audit runs the assistant made for this website.
  • Reports — New report builds a saved report from the sections you tick: Search presence, Rank tracker, Search Console, Analytics, Site health, Full site crawl, AI visibility, Competitors and Competing pages. Choose Every month, Every week or Only when I ask. Each build is frozen with its own period, so an old report keeps its own numbers. Build now builds one immediately.

What it affects elsewhere

  • Your default site is what new articles pull internal-link suggestions from.
  • Articles, keyword research and tool runs from the assistant are filed under the site and show up on its page.
  • Connected Search Console data feeds the assistant's first-party tools, What to do next, and each article's search card.
