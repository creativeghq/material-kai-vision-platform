Connected Websites & Search Console

/profile?tab=websites

Connecting a website is the foundation of the whole SEO feature. You point the platform at your site, it reads your sitemap, fetches each page and builds a semantic index of your content — without ever publishing or changing anything on your site. That index is what lets the SEO assistant suggest internal links back to your own pages when it writes articles, and it gives you a per-site dashboard that gathers all your SEO work in one place, including your real Google Search Console performance. This page covers adding a site, the preview-then-crawl safety step, the dashboard's tabs, and connecting Search Console.

Where: your Profile → the Websites tab (/profile?tab=websites), between Social Accounts and Keys. Unlike the SEO Toolkit, this tab is open to any signed-in user — you don't need the Pro add-on to connect a site (though crawling spends credits).

What the crawler does (and doesn't do)

What it is — a read-only crawler that reads your sitemap, fetches each listed page, and builds a semantic index of the content. Why it matters — that index is how the assistant knows what pages you already have, so it can suggest genuinely relevant internal links. It never publishes or modifies anything on your site — it only reads. The crawl re-runs on a schedule (roughly daily) to keep the index fresh, and you can force a fresh crawl any time with Recrawl.

Adding a website

Click Add Website and fill in:

FieldWhat to enter
Website URL *Your domain, e.g. https://yourdomain.com.
Sitemap URLOptional — leave blank and the platform auto-detects it (it tries /sitemap.xml, /sitemap_index.xml, then robots.txt).
Display NameOptional friendly name for the site.
Max pages to indexHow many pages to crawl (default 50, minimum 10, hard cap 1000). This bounds the cost of the crawl.
Use as defaultMake this the site that inter-linking suggestions pull from when you write a new article. One site is your default.

Preview before the full crawl

Because the crawler is charged per page (it fetches each one through Firecrawl), adding a site doesn't immediately crawl everything. Saving runs Add & Preview — a Sitemap Preview that reads your sitemap, reports the total URLs found and how many it will crawl, and fetches a sample of 5 pages so you can confirm titles and descriptions come through cleanly. If it looks right, click Proceed with Full Crawl. This preview-then-commit step means you never spend credits on a full crawl of a site whose sitemap was wrong.

You can re-run the 5-page Preview any time from the row's action icons, and Recrawl now (full) forces a complete fresh index. Remove deletes the site and all its indexed pages.

The per-site SEO dashboard

Click a site (or its Open SEO dashboard icon) to open its dashboard. The header shows the domain, page count and last-crawl time with a Recrawl button, then four headline counters — Articles, Keyword research, Toolkit runs and Tracked domains — over five tabs.

TabWhat it lists
ArticlesEvery SEO article written for this site — title, target keyword, SEO score, word count, status. Click a row to open the full article viewer. (Empty until you ask the assistant to write one.)
Keyword ResearchSaved keyword-research runs — target keyword, topic, number of keywords, volume.
Toolkit RunsA log of the assistant's research/audit tool runs against this site — subject, kind, country, result.
Domain AuditsTracked domains — domain, rank, organic traffic, last audit — from the domain-snapshot tools.
Search PerformanceYour real Google Search Console data (see below).

Google Search Console — real search performance

What it is — a direct connection to your property in Google Search Console, so the Search Performance tab shows the numbers Google actually reports for your site rather than third-party estimates. Why it matters — it's the ground truth for how you're doing in search, and it powers the assistant's striking-distance and top-movers tools.

How to connect Search Console

  1. Open the site's dashboard → Search Performance tab → Connect Search Console.
  2. You're sent to Google to grant read-only access (the webmasters.readonly scope). The sign-in and callback happen server-side — Google returns to the platform, not into the app — so the connection is handled securely and you land back on the Websites tab.
  3. If your Google account has more than one property, pick the Search Console property that matches this site. The platform also tries to auto-match it to your domain.
  4. It backfills the last 28 days immediately, then refreshes nightly. Use Sync to pull more history on demand, or Disconnect to stop (your historical rows are kept).

Operator setup required. Search Console uses Google OAuth, so a platform operator must have configured the Google client credentials (under Operations → Keys). If they haven't, the Connect action tells you so instead of failing silently.

What the Search Performance tab shows

Once connected, the tab presents your last-28-day performance:

  • Four headline tiles — Clicks, Impressions, Average CTR and Average position.
  • Clicks trend — a sparkline of daily clicks across the window.
  • Devices — clicks split by device (mobile / desktop / tablet), each with its share.
  • Breakdown — a table you can switch between Queries, Pages, Countries and Appearance, each row showing Clicks, Impressions, CTR and Position. Page rows link out to the live page.

Reporting lag. Google Search Console data lands a couple of days behind, so the most recent 2–3 days may look sparse — that's Google's lag, not a sync problem.

What it affects elsewhere

  • Your default connected site is what the SEO Toolkit uses for internal-link suggestions when writing articles.
  • Articles, keyword research and tool runs the assistant produces are filed under the site, so they show up on this dashboard.
  • Connected Search Console data feeds the assistant's first-party GSC tools (striking-distance, top-movers).
