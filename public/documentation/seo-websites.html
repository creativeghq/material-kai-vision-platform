Connected Websites & Search Console

/profile?tab=websites /websites/:id

Connecting a website is the foundation of the whole SEO feature. You point the platform at your site, it reads your sitemap, fetches each page and builds a semantic index of your content, without ever publishing or changing anything on your site. Each connected site then gets its own page: a dashboard that gathers your search performance (Google Search Console), your audience (Google Analytics), your rankings, your visibility in AI assistants, your articles and your technical health. Every section says plainly when a figure is unknown rather than showing a zero.

Where: your Profile → Websites (/profile?tab=websites) lists your sites. Click a site to open its own page at /websites/:id, with breadcrumbs back to My Profile › Websites. The list is open to any signed-in user; the paid research sections need the SEO Toolkit add-on, and crawling spends credits.

The website list

The Your Websites card lists each site with Status (Indexed, No pages found, Crawl failed with the error shown under the URL, or Not crawled), Pages and Last crawl. On each row:

  • Click the row to open the website's page. Older links that used ?website= redirect there.
  • The recrawl icon runs Recrawl now (full).
  • The More menu has Preview (sample 5 pages), Edit and Remove. Remove deletes the site and all its indexed pages.

Adding a website

Click Add website and fill in:

FieldWhat to enter
Website URL *Your domain, e.g. https://yourdomain.com.
Sitemap URL (optional)Leave blank and it's auto-detected from /sitemap.xml or robots.txt.
Display Name (optional)A friendly name for the site.
Max pages to indexHow many pages to crawl, which bounds the crawl's cost. The default is 50, enough for most blogs. Raise it for a larger site; the preview tells you if your sitemap holds more than the cap.
Use as defaultInter-linking suggestions for new articles pull from this site.

Preview before the full crawl

The crawler is charged per page read, so saving with Add & Preview doesn't crawl everything straight away. It opens a Sitemap Preview showing Total URLs, Will crawl up to, and Sample success for 5 sample pages, so you can check that titles and descriptions come through. A warning appears if the sitemap holds more pages than your cap. If it looks right, click Proceed with full crawl. You never spend credits crawling a site whose sitemap was wrong.

After that, the index is kept fresh by the Page crawl schedule and by Recrawl. Each run reads the pages search demand points to first, then uses spare capacity to work through sitemap pages it hasn't read yet. The article writer also reads unread pages on its topic before writing.

The website page

The header shows the domain, the page count and last crawl, with Visit and Recrawl. A failed crawl shows as a red banner. Under it sits the Search market, then four counters (Articles, Keyword Research, Toolkit Runs, Domain Audits) that each open their section. A rail on the left (a strip on a phone) holds ten sections; a section with several panes shows them as tabs across the top. The open pane is in the address as ?section=…&view=…, so any of them can be bookmarked or linked. Older links that named a section from before the rail was regrouped still land on the right pane.

Search market

What it is — the country and language Google results are read in for this site. Rank tracking, keyword research and articles all use it. Where it comes from — the line under the header says the market and its source: set on this website, from the tracked keywords, from Search Console, from the AI-visibility subject or from the business country. Owners and admins can click Set market to choose a Country and a Language, or Use the site data instead to go back. If nothing gives a market, the line says so, and trackers and research do not run until it is set.

Paid checks run only when you schedule them

What it is — Reports → Schedules lists the five collectors that call a paid data provider: Rank tracking, Domain tracking (your domain and your competitors), Homepage audit, Page crawl and AI Visibility. Each one is Off until you choose Daily, Weekly or Monthly. Why it matters — nothing paid runs on a schedule nobody chose. The table shows each collector's Per Run (Max) and Per Month (Max) credits before you switch it on, and its Last Run and next run. A schedule is charged to the person who switched it on. If a price can't be read right now, it says so instead of showing 0.

Every collector also has a button that runs it once: Check now, Re-check, Snapshot now and the like. Each shows up to N cr beside it, from the same estimate.

The same actions on every keyword

Wherever a keyword appears (Search Console queries, cannibalisation, the rank tracker, SERP features, keyword research and its suggestions, top ranking keywords, What to do next), its row carries the same four icons: Track (adds it to the rank tracker; shows as tracked everywhere at once), Research (charged; a repeat within 30 days is free), Write an article (charged, asks you to confirm first) and Copy.

A figure is a number, or the reason there isn't one

What it is — every tile on these pages either shows a number or says why it can't. Why it matters — "we couldn't fetch your backlinks" and "you have no backlinks" are very different, and a zero would hide which one you're looking at. Where a number would be, you'll see:

WordMeaning
NoneThe source answered and genuinely has nothing for this site yet.
Unknown (amber)The source failed, so the figure is unknown. It is not zero. A refused data-provider call reads this way too.
Not measuredNever collected for this site. Run it to fill it in.

Overview

  • Set up this website — until every step is done, a checklist leads the Overview: connect Search Console, set the market, add competitors, track keywords, run the first crawl and switch on a schedule. Each step opens the place where it's done, and the card disappears once all are ticked.
  • What to do next — one ranked list of actions, built from Search Console, the crawl, your articles and your competitors. Each row says why it's there and is tagged Quick Win, Refresh, Consolidate, New page, Not crawled, Crawled, not indexed or Fix Page. Its button takes you to the place to act: Write it starts a new article with the keyword filled in. Data sources that are missing are listed underneath.
  • Search presence — domain rank, organic keywords, estimated monthly traffic, traffic value, referring domains, backlinks, broken backlinks and spam score. Use Take first snapshot or Refresh; it warns when the figures are stale.
  • Ranking profile and Top ranking keywords — where your keywords sit, and what improved, declined, appeared or was lost week on week.
  • Summary cards for Search Console, AI assistants and Technical health, each with a button into the full section.

Search performance — real Google Search Console data

What it is — a direct connection to your property in Google Search Console, so you see the numbers Google actually reports rather than third-party estimates. It also powers the assistant's striking-distance and top-movers tools, the What to do next list and each article's search card.

How to connect

  1. Open the website → Search performance → Connect Search Console.
  2. Grant access at Google. You come back to the website's Search performance section.
  3. If it can't match a property to your domain, choose one under Pick a Search Console Property (Load my properties).
  4. Sync pulls on demand; a nightly pull runs on its own. Disconnect stops the connection and keeps your history.

The connection also lets the platform resubmit your sitemap to Google, remove submitted sitemaps that now answer 404 or 410, and ask Google whether your pages are indexed, newly published articles first. A site connected before this access was requested shows Update Google access: click it to re-consent in place. Your history and Analytics property are kept.

Operator setup required. Search Console uses Google sign-in, so a platform operator must have configured the Google client credentials. If they haven't, Connect tells you so instead of failing silently.

What it shows

  • A Day / Week / Month / Quarter range, and four tiles: Clicks, Impressions, Avg CTR and Avg position.
  • A clicks trend, a Devices split, and a Breakdown table with Queries, Pages, Countries and Appearance tabs. Queries can be filtered by Page and Segment (all countries and devices, one device, or one country).
  • Lens on the Queries table: All queries, Striking distance (8–20) (queries averaging position 8 to 20 with at least 20 impressions), Rising and Falling. Every row carries the keyword actions.
  • Drill down: click a query to see the pages Google showed for it, with a warning when several pages split the impressions, which is a cannibalisation signal. Click a page to see the queries that brought it impressions, then Filter Queries by this page.

Reporting lag. Search Console data lands 2–3 days behind, so the most recent days may look sparse. That's Google's lag, not a sync problem.

Cannibalisation — your pages competing with each other

Pages competing with each other lists the queries where Google shows more than one of your pages, with a severity, impressions, the number of pages and the best position. It flags when Google's main page isn't the one earning the clicks. Filter by severity or click split and sort as you like. Make this the main page records which page should win the query, and the row then shows it. If a competing page is one of your articles, you can have a refresh of that article drafted so it stops competing; it's charged at the real model cost and the changes come back as proposals you approve. Consolidate lets you pick the page to keep and copies the redirects for the others (Apache .htaccess format).

Keywords → Tracked — the keywords you chose

What it is — the rank tracker follows a fixed set of keywords you pick and checks each one's Google position on the schedule you set, or whenever you press Check now. Why it matters — it's the one section that answers "are the keywords I care about moving up or down?" Pro add-on

  • Add keywords — one per line or comma-separated. Choose the device (Desktop / Mobile) and the location (the whole country or one city), then Track these. Keywords are checked in the website's search market, and tracking is refused until a market is set. Check now runs a check immediately. Change device or location on a row moves an existing keyword.
  • Tiles — Visibility (share of tracked keywords in the top 10), Avg. position, Ranking (how many rank, how many are outside the top 100) and Could not check. A failed check is unknown, not unranked, and neither failures nor unranked keywords distort the averages.
  • Visibility over time, the distribution across bands (Top 3 · 4–10 · 11–30 · 31–100 · Not ranking), and the Tracked keywords table with position, change, trend and ranking page. You can filter it by country, device, location, source, tag, movement, position band and SERP block.
  • Found in Search Console — keywords your site already shows up for, with a verdict (Earning clicks, Recurring, Near page one, Below the bar). Switch on Add qualifying keywords automatically to have them tracked up to a ceiling you set. Stopping tracking a keyword also stops it being suggested back.

Drops are confirmed before they're announced. One live Google read is a sample, so a reading that leaves the top 10 is re-read straight away. Only a confirmed drop raises a Flow event to owners and admins, and the alert opens the SEO assistant with the keyword and the page that was ranking. When Google hides a result behind its own redirect link, the tracker still records which of your pages ranks.

Keywords → SERP features

Google decides which blocks appear on a results page; this view only reports them, from the rank checks. For your tracked keywords: which search features appear on the results page (AI Overview, featured snippet, People Also Ask, image pack…), whether each one cites you, and which keywords show the feature without you. A feature you're absent from is a finding, not a gap in the list. Counters show how many keywords were Checked, are Unknown (the latest check failed) or were Never checked. Refresh re-reads them.

Authority

Backlinks & Authority — from the backlink index: Backlinks, Referring domains, Referring main domains, Domain rank, Spam score and Broken backlinks, plus a Referring domains list (linking site, its rank, anchor, which of your pages it links to, first seen). Use Snapshot now or Refresh. If the list can't be fetched it says Unknown, not zero.

Audit history — one Domain History series for your own domain: rank, keywords, traffic, referring domains and backlinks per capture, earlier audits included. Snapshot takes one now; the Domain tracking schedule keeps it going. When the data provider changes how it estimates traffic, figures from either side of that change are not compared, and the tile says why.

Competitors

A one-year chart of you against your rivals, switchable between organic traffic, keywords, traffic value, referring domains and domain rank. Under Tracked Domains, rivals are measured on the same Domain tracking run and in the same market as you. Some are Suggested; add your own with Add competitor. Their keywords are kept, and feed the New page ideas in What to do next.

Keyword Gap

What your active competitors rank for that this site neither ranks for nor tracks: the keyword, which competitors rank, their best position and the volume, with the keyword actions on each row. With no competitors, or nothing collected yet, it says so instead of showing an empty table.

Keywords → Research

Your saved research runs: target keyword, topic, volume, difficulty, CPC, competition, opportunity, trend and more. Each row has the keyword actions; New research starts one. Opening a run shows its Keyword clusters, which you can Save as plan into the Topic plan. If a page of yours already ranks for the keyword, it warns you before you write a competing article. The Asked of AI tile (Check AI volume) shows how often the term is asked of AI assistants.

Keyword ideas for one of your pages

  1. In Suggested from your pages, type part of a page title or URL and pick the page.
  2. You immediately see, for free, Queries Google already shows this page for (90 days) from your Search Console data.
  3. Get keyword ideas (paid) adds Keyword ideas around this page's title: volume, CPC and competition.

Every row has Research and Track buttons. Below, Page titles not yet tracked or researched lists pages you haven't worked on yet.

Site Health → Site Crawl

Full site crawl audits your pages for technical problems. Click Crawl the site (later Crawl again). It shows pages read, an on-page score, issues (with the change since the last crawl) and pages affected. Issues are grouped: Cannot be indexed, Broken links, Redirect chains, Duplicate titles or descriptions, Duplicate content, Pages returning an error. Each group has a Fix: line and Show all. Pages that couldn't be fetched are flagged as unknown, not clean. Each issue row with a URL has Ask JARVIS to fix, which hands the page and the issue to the assistant to explain the problem and draft the exact change, and Open article when the page is one of your articles.

Site Health → Pages

Every sitemap page with what the crawl read, its Search Console clicks and impressions over 28 days, its on-page score and its open proposals. Tiles count Pages Known, Pages Read, Pages Reviewed, Open proposals and Applied. Click a page to open its drawer:

  • What the crawl read — title, description, canonical, language, word count and status.
  • What Google shows it for (90 days) — its Search Console queries, with striking-distance ones marked.
  • On-page audit — Audit runs the on-page checks for this one URL.
  • Review — a charged AI review of the page with concrete proposals, each with Copy and Dismiss. A proposal is marked applied on its own once the crawler finds it on the live page, and stale if the page changed to something else.

Site Health → Page Health

The Site Health card runs a Lighthouse (Core Web Vitals) and on-page audit of your homepage. Use Run check or Re-check. It shows On-page SEO, Performance, Accessibility and Best practices gauges, and What we checked, group by group. Failing checks can be handed to the assistant, like crawl issues. An audit that returned no data shows as Audit failed with the cause, never as a clean score.

Site Health → llms.txt

Builds an llms.txt file from your crawled pages, leaving out pages that return errors or have left the sitemap. Use Rebuild, Copy or Download, then host it yourself at /llms.txt. The platform never writes to your site.

Reports

  • Toolkit Runs — a log of the research and audit runs the assistant made for this website.
  • Reports — New report builds a saved report from the sections you tick: Search presence, Rank tracker, Search Console, Analytics, Site health, Full site crawl, AI visibility, Competitors and Competing pages. Choose Every Month, Every Week or Only when I ask. Each build is frozen with its own period, so an old report keeps its own numbers. Build now builds one immediately.
  • Schedules — which paid collectors run, and how often. See above.

What it affects elsewhere

  • Your default site is what new articles pull internal-link suggestions from.
  • Articles, keyword research and tool runs from the assistant are filed under the site and show up on its page.
  • Connected Search Console data feeds the assistant's first-party tools, What to do next, and each article's search card.
