Material Recognition

/recognition

Upload one or more photos of a real-world surface and let Hybrid AI identify the material, describe its visual properties, and match it against the catalogue — turning a single picture into structured, searchable knowledge.

How to open it: go to Material Recognition at /recognition.

Step by step: identify a material from a photo

  1. Open Material Recognition at /recognition.
  2. Click Choose Files / Select Images — or drag and drop — and add one or more photos of the surface you want to identify.
  3. Wait for the hybrid-AI analysis to run; a progress indicator shows the work in flight.
  4. Review the recognised attributes for each image — material type, category and sub-category, dominant colours, textures, finish and surface pattern.
  5. Browse the visually-matched catalogue products returned alongside each result.
  6. Act on a match — open it for full specifications, add it to a moodboard, or refine the search in the Agent Hub. You can also line up several candidates on the Compare page or search the Knowledge Base from the same understanding.

What this page is for

Material Recognition answers the question "what is this, and do you have something like it?" Point it at a tile, a fabric swatch, a stone sample, a wood finish or any finished surface, and it returns the most likely material type and sub-category, a description of how the surface looks (colours, textures, finish, surface pattern), and a set of visually-similar products from the catalogue. It's the fastest way to go from a photo on your phone to a shortlist of real, sourceable materials.

What "Hybrid AI" means

Rather than relying on a single model, recognition combines several AI models working together on every image:

  • A visual encoder — converts the picture into a visual "fingerprint" that can be compared against catalogue images to find genuine look-alikes.
  • A vision-language analysis — "reads" the image and writes a structured description: the material type, its category and sub-category, and its visual attributes.

Because two complementary techniques are combined — one for matching, one for describing — the result is richer and more reliable than any one model could produce alone. That's why every result carries the Hybrid AI badge.

Recognition works best on a clear, well-lit, close-up photo of the surface itself, with the material filling most of the frame. Avoid heavy glare, strong shadows or busy backgrounds for the most accurate read.

Uploading your images

There are three ways to add photos, and you can analyse several at once:

  • Choose Files / Select Images — click the button to browse and pick images from your device.
  • Drag and drop — drop one or more images straight onto the upload area.
  • Multiple at once — add a whole batch; each image is analysed independently and gets its own result.

Common image formats are supported (such as JPEG, PNG and WebP). Selected images appear as thumbnails before analysis, and you can remove any individual file or clear all and start again.

Step by step

  1. Add your photos with Choose Files / Select Images, or drag and drop them onto the upload area.
  2. Review the thumbnails of the selected images; remove any you didn't mean to include.
  3. Click Start Recognition to analyse them with Hybrid AI. A progress indicator shows the work in flight.
  4. Read the results — each image returns its identification, visual attributes and similar catalogue products.
  5. Act on a result — open a matched product, save it to a moodboard, or take it to the Agent Hub to refine.

What you get back

For each analysed image, recognition returns three kinds of information:

ResultWhat it tells you
Material identificationThe material's type and category, plus a more specific sub-category where it can be determined.
Visual attributesDescriptive properties of the surface — dominant colours, textures, finish and surface pattern.
Similar productsVisually-matched items from the catalogue, so you can see real materials that look like your photo.

Each identification is shown with an indication of how confident the analysis is, so you can judge how closely the read fits your image.

Acting on the results

  • Open a match — jump from a similar product straight to its full catalogue entry to see specifications, availability and more.
  • Add to a moodboard — collect the materials you like onto a moodboard to build out a scheme.
  • Compare options — line up several matched materials side by side on the Compare page.
  • Refine in the Agent Hub — take an image or a result into the Agent Hub and ask in plain language for variations, narrower matches or follow-up searches.

Where recognition fits

Recognition is the visual front door to the catalogue. The same understanding that powers it also drives search across the platform, so a photo here connects naturally to the rest of your workflow — searching the Knowledge Base, comparing candidates, and building boards and proposals.

Recognition is powered by AI analysis. Most identification is included in normal use, but some advanced AI actions may consume credits — where that applies, the platform makes the cost clear before you proceed.
