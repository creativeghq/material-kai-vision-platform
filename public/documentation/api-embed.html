Website Embed

/functions/v1/products-3d-api

Show your products — in 3D, in AR, with configurable finishes — on your own website. Two lines of HTML, or the JSON API underneath if you'd rather build your own.

Where: create a key at Profile → Keys → Website Embed. Keys are per workspace, so any admin can manage them and they outlive whoever created one.

See it running: a live demo page — a real product, a real 3D model, real prices, and a compatibility rule you can trigger.

Four tags, one script

One <script> registers all four. Which one you place is a decision about your page, not an installation step — you never load a second file, and you can put more than one on a site.

TagThe question it answersNeeds
<materialkai-builder>“Show me this product” — or, with no product-id, “what have you got like this?”A catalogue key
<materialkai-configurator>“Let me build something that isn't in your catalogue yet.”A catalogue key + a published configurator
<materialkai-assistant>“Work something out for me” — a sizer, a cost comparison, a search.A tools key
<materialkai-product>The 3D viewer on its own. The builder mounts this internally.A catalogue key

The two-line version

For your catalogue there is one tag. Give it a product-id for a page that is already about one product; leave it off and it asks the visitor what they're after instead.

<script src="https://app.materialshub.gr/embed/materialkai-product.js" defer></script>

<materialkai-builder
  api-key="mk_embed_…"
  product-id="YOUR-PRODUCT-ID">
</materialkai-builder>

That renders the product in 3D with orbit controls, an AR button on Android (Scene Viewer, straight from the .glb; iPhones get the 3D viewer without an AR button), its price, and — if the product has configurable options — swatches that repaint the model and update the price as they're picked.

AttributeWhat it does
api-keyRequired. Your embed key.
product-idRequired. The product to show.
show-add-to-cartAdds a cart button. It doesn't take payment — it hands your page the chosen configuration (see below).
autorotate="false"Stops the model turning on its own.
api-basePoint at a different environment. Rarely needed.

The other situation: they don't know what they want yet

Drop the product-id and the same tag handles the case where the visitor doesn't know which product they want. It asks what they're after, lets them pick materials and finishes, shows them the result — the real 3D model when one exists, an AI impression when it doesn't — and ends in a price if the catalogue matches, or a quote request if it doesn't.

<script src="https://app.materialshub.gr/embed/materialkai-product.js" defer></script>

<materialkai-builder api-key="mk_embed_…"></materialkai-builder>

With a product-id it skips straight to that product, which is the first example above. <materialkai-product> also still works on its own and is what the builder mounts internally — but there is no reason to reach for it: the attributes decide.

<materialkai-builder api-key="mk_embed_…" product-id="YOUR-PRODUCT-ID"></materialkai-builder>

The fallback is the point, not the error case. A visitor who asks for something you don't stock becomes a quote request with everything they specified — and the picture they were looking at when they asked. A configurator bound to one product can only ever sell what somebody already photographed, modelled and priced.

Letting a visitor plan a room

A product with a 3D model gets a Plan your room button. It opens our hosted planner in a new tab, already scoped to your key, with that product placed at its real measured size — the visitor sets their room dimensions and arranges pieces to see how they fit.

It is a hosted page rather than part of the widget on purpose: the planner is a full 3D application, and loading it into every product page would cost every visitor for a feature few open. Nothing is saved — the arrangement lives in the visitor's browser and comes to you attached to their enquiry.

Only products with a model get the button. Without one the planner has no measured size to work from and would place a 60 × 60 cm placeholder — inviting somebody to check whether it fits, at a size nobody measured.

Letting a visitor build something you don't stock

The builder matches a visitor's description against products you already have. <materialkai-configurator> is for the case where there is nothing to match: a fitted kitchen, a wardrobe run, anything you make rather than pick off a shelf. The visitor lays out zones, sets module widths and counts, chooses finishes and appliances, and watches a real price move as they do.

<script src="https://app.materialshub.gr/embed/materialkai-product.js" defer></script>

<materialkai-configurator
  api-key="mk_embed_…"
  blueprint="YOUR-BLUEPRINT-ID">
</materialkai-configurator>
AttributeWhat it does
api-keyRequired. A catalogue key.
blueprintRequired. Which configurator to open. Without it the widget says so rather than guessing.
api-basePoint at a different environment. Rarely needed.

The price is the same derivation that records the money when the configuration becomes a plan — not a second one written for the widget. What an anonymous visitor is shown on your page is what the quote will say.

Your costs stay on our side. Every line's cost basis is folded to a single price before it leaves the server, so material cost, labour rate and margin are never in the page — even though someone reading your source can see everything the widget received. The arithmetic in the browser runs on the folded figures and lands on the same total.

The hardware and service schedule — hinges, legs, sockets, ducting — is derived alongside the price and shown as counts, never money. The plan counts; the quote prices.

How it ends

There is no cart here, because nobody adds a fitted kitchen to a basket. The visitor finishes on Request a quote — name, email, a note, and a bot challenge — and it arrives as a quote request with the full configuration attached. Your page can also listen for it:

document.addEventListener('materialkai:quote-request', (event) => {
  const { blueprint_id, title, composition, estimated_total, currency } = event.detail;
});

The contact details aren't in that payload on purpose — they've already been delivered to your workspace, and putting an email address into an event any script on the page can read would be handing it to every tag you've installed.

As with the cart handoff, the same payload goes to the parent window via postMessage when the widget sits in an iframe.

Scope a key to specific configurators. When you create a key you can restrict it to configurators instead of products — useful when a partner's site should be able to open your kitchen planner and nothing else. A key scoped to products or categories sees no configurators at all: it's a key about products.

A configurator has to be published for embedding — separately from being active internally, and for the same reason products have an online-store switch. An unpublished one returns the same “not found” as an id that never existed, so nobody can discover what you're still working on by guessing.

The tools, as buttons

<materialkai-assistant> puts the calculators on your page: size a heat pump, compare heating costs, estimate a kitchen, price a spec, search materials. It needs a tools key (chosen when you create one) and serves none of your catalogue — but every enquiry it produces lands in your workspace.

<script src="https://app.materialshub.gr/embed/materialkai-product.js" defer></script>

<materialkai-assistant api-key="mk_embed_…"></materialkai-assistant>

It opens as buttons, not a chat box — and that is deliberate. Most visitors don't know what to type at a blinking cursor, and every keystroke at one would bill you for a model turn. Pressing a button runs a single calculation directly: no AI, no tokens, nothing to pay. A visitor's first useful answer costs you nothing.

ButtonWhat it works outCosts you
Size a heat pumpThe output a room or building needs.Nothing
Compare heating costsRunning costs across fuels.Nothing
Estimate a kitchenA ballpark from size and finish level.Nothing
What would this costPrices a written spec against your catalogue.Nothing
Request a quoteHands you the enquiry.Nothing
Show me what works with thisSearches your materials by description.A fraction of a cent — off by default

The form under each button is built from what that calculation actually accepts, so the options offered are always the options it takes. Nothing is typed in by hand on our side and then left to drift.

The one place AI is involved

After a result, a visitor can ask for it to be explained — two sentences, in plain language. That single turn is the only model call on this surface, and it cannot search, price, or write anything: it only reads the answer the button already produced. A public text box is safe here because there is nothing behind it to reach.

Explanations are capped in money, not in turns. Each key has a daily spend limit you set. A cap counted in questions would be meaningless — a real turn ranges from under half a cent to several euro, so “twenty a day” is a budget somewhere between four cents and ninety euro. When the day's limit is reached the buttons keep working; only the explanations pause.

Your key is meant to be public

The embed key ships in your page's source. That's what it's for — like a Stripe publishable key or a Google Maps key. It is not a secret, and it is not what protects your catalog. Three things do:

ControlWhat it stopsWhat it can't
Allowed websitesAnother site mounting your widget with your key.It's enforced by the browser, so it can't bind a script that isn't one. That's true of every publishable key.
Requests per minuteAnyone burning through your quota — browser or not.—
What the key may serveA key seeing more of your catalog than you chose.—

Add your domain before you go live. Include any staging or *.myshopify.com preview domain you test on. Miss it and the widget says “This website is not allowed to use this embed key” — which is the message doing its job, and a one-line fix in Profile → Keys.

Limiting what a key can show

When you create a key you choose its reach: everything published, only certain categories, only specific products, or only certain configurators. Because the key is public, this is the only control that genuinely constrains what an embed can pull — anything the request could ask for, anyone reading your page source could ask for too. Hand a partner a key scoped to one range and that's all it will ever return.

Only products you've published to your online store are served at all. Unpublish one and it disappears from every embed immediately.

The add-to-cart handoff

We never take payment. When a visitor clicks the cart button your page gets an event with the configuration, and your own cart does the rest.

document.addEventListener('materialkai:add-to-cart', (event) => {
  const { product_id, name, price, currency, quantity, options } = event.detail;
  // options → { "Fabric": "Velvet Emerald", "Legs": "Walnut" }
  myCart.add({ product_id, quantity, properties: options });
});

If the widget sits inside an iframe the same payload is also sent via postMessage to the parent window, so you can listen either way.

On Shopify this wiring is already written — see Putting this on Shopify below.

Putting this on Shopify

Nothing special is needed: the widget is a plain script tag and a custom element, so it goes onto a Shopify storefront the same way it goes onto any other site. You don't have to edit theme code, and you don't have to install anything.

  1. Theme → Customise, open the page you want it on, and Add section → Custom Liquid (on a product page, Add block → Custom Liquid).
  2. Paste the snippet from Profile → Keys → Website Embed into it.
  3. Add both your live domain and your your-store.myshopify.com address to the key's allowed websites — the theme editor previews on the myshopify.com one, so leaving it out means it works live and looks broken while you're setting it up.
  4. Save, and view the page as a shopper. The theme editor's preview frame is a fair test; the section list is not.

Sending a configured product to Shopify's cart

On a product page, add this below the widget in the same Custom Liquid block. It adds the variant the shopper currently has selected and records their chosen finishes on the line item, so whoever picks the order can read them:

<script>
  document.addEventListener('materialkai:add-to-cart', function (event) {
    var detail = event.detail || {};
    var variantId = {{ product.selected_or_first_available_variant.id | json }};
    if (!variantId) return;

    var properties = Object.assign({}, detail.options);
    properties['_materialkai_product_id'] = detail.product_id;

    fetch(window.Shopify.routes.root + 'cart/add.js', {
      method: 'POST',
      headers: { 'Content-Type': 'application/json' },
      body: JSON.stringify({
        items: [{ id: variantId, quantity: detail.quantity || 1, properties: properties }]
      })
    }).then(function () {
      document.dispatchEvent(new CustomEvent('cart:refresh', { bubbles: true }));
      document.dispatchEvent(new CustomEvent('cart:build', { bubbles: true }));
    });
  });
</script>

Properties starting with an underscore are hidden from the shopper but stay on the order, so the exact configuration can be reconstructed later. The ones without stay visible — those are what the cart, the order confirmation and the person packing it read.

Those last two dispatchEvent lines are what make most themes refresh their cart drawer. Without them the item is genuinely added and the shopper sees nothing happen.

Shopify charges Shopify's price. On a Shopify product page the money is theirs — price, stock and tax all come from the variant. Treat the widget's figure as a guide there, and keep your finish options priced as Shopify variants if a choice is meant to change what's charged. A configurator that tried to own price on someone else's checkout would be wrong about all three.

A one-click version exists. We've built a Shopify theme app extension — the widget as a drag-in block with its own settings, and product matching by metafield so every product carries its own id instead of you pasting one per page. It needs publishing to the Shopify app store before you can install it, so for now: ask us, and use the Custom Liquid route above in the meantime. It produces the same result.

The JSON API

If you'd rather render it yourself. Send the key as ?key= or in an x-embed-key header — the query string avoids a CORS preflight, so it's one request instead of two.

ActionWhat you get
?action=listYour published products. &only_3d=true for just the ones with a model; limit and offset to page.
?action=product&product_id=…One product: images, price, its glb/gltf/usdz models with real-world dimensions, and its configurable options.
?action=configure&product_id=…&option_value_ids=a,bWhat that combination costs — and whether it's allowed.
?action=blueprintsYour published configurators — enough to choose one, not enough to price anything.
?action=blueprint&blueprint_id=…One configurator: its zones, options and rates, with every cost basis already folded to a price.
?action=eventRecords a view, an AR launch or a cart handoff. The widget does this for you.
 
fetch("https://bgbavxtjlbvgplozizxu.supabase.co/functions/v1/products-3d-api"
      + "?action=list&only_3d=true&key=mk_embed_…")
  .then((r) => r.json())
  .then(({ products }) => console.log(products));

Prices come back VAT-inclusive, matching what your online store shows. Option price differences are included in the same figure — the total is always calculated on our side, so it can't drift from what a quote would say.

Unavailable combinations

If you've set up compatibility rules — “velvet isn't available on oak legs”, “walnut legs require velvet” — configure returns them as violations and the widget shows the reason and disables its cart button. The same check runs when a configuration becomes a quote, so a blocked combination can't reach a document a customer signs.

Seeing whether it's working

Profile → Keys → Website Embed shows the last 30 days: views, 3D loads, AR launches, cart handoffs — and which pages the widget is running on. If you've just installed it and the page list is empty, the script isn't loading or the domain isn't allowed.

What you need first

  • A product published to your online store — that's the same switch the storefront uses.
  • A 3D model (a .glb) uploaded to that product, if you want 3D rather than a photo. We read its real-world size straight from the file. The same file drives AR on Android (Scene Viewer) — no separate AR file is needed.
  • Options on the product, if you want configurable finishes.

Without a model the widget falls back to the product's photo, so it's still worth embedding before your 3D work is finished.

For the other two tags:

  • <materialkai-configurator> needs a blueprint published for embedding — no products, no 3D models. It prices what you'd build, not what you stock.
  • <materialkai-assistant> needs a key created as the free tools rather than my catalogue. Nothing else — it serves none of your catalogue, so there is nothing to publish first.
