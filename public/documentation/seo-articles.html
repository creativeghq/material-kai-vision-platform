SEO Article Writer Pro add-on

/agent-hub?capability=seo-article /websites/:id?section=articles /profile?tab=keys

The article writer turns a target keyword into a finished, publishable article about your business. It researches what ranks and what readers ask, reads your own pages on the topic so it works from your real products, prices and photos, writes the article, runs an editor review that checks every claim, and finishes with an analysis report. You then refine it in the article viewer, keep it current with Update article and Compare with what ranks now, and publish it straight to your own WordPress site.

Where: a website's Articles section (/websites/:id?section=articles) → New article, or the Generate full article quick-start in the SEO Article Pipeline toolkit of the AI studio. It needs the seo-toolkit add-on. Articles open in the article viewer from the Articles list.

Starting an article

What it is — there is one way to make an article: the Generate full article form (“Research, read our pages on the topic, plan, write, editor review, then an analysis report”). Why it matters — every article goes through the same path, so every article gets your pages, your facts and the editor review.

FieldWhat to enter

You can also start the form already filled in: Write on a Topic plan row, or Write it on a New page row of the website Overview's What to do next list.

When it refuses to start

Before anything is charged, the writer checks three things and tells you plainly if it won't go ahead:

  • Not enough credits — an article needs a reserve available while it's written; you're charged only what it actually uses (typically about 90 credits).
  • Same keyword already written — the website already has an article on that keyword. Pick a different keyword or angle.
  • One of your pages already ranks for the keyword — it names the page and its average position and suggests updating that page instead of writing a competing one.

The Keyword Research detail warns about the last case too: “You already rank for this with …” or “A page of yours already covers this closely … Consider updating it rather than writing a new one.”

What happens while it writes

The progress strip walks Research → Planning → Writing → Analyzing → Done, with a caption for each step (“Researching keywords with DataForSEO…”, “Writing article with Claude Opus…”, “Finalizing report…”). Behind those five steps:

  • Research reads the search results, the questions readers ask, and what the answer engines tell searchers about the topic. The article is later checked against those reader expectations.
  • Your own pages on the topic are read first, including ones the crawler had not read yet. The writer works from their facts, links only pages that exist, and places your real photos.
  • Writing follows the website's Brand Profile voice and your brief. Your brand name, slugs, material names and search terms are never translated.
  • Editor review runs section by section and fixes what it finds, including the search snippet. It never invents a citation or a supply claim. Greek articles also pass a dictionary and grammar gate.
  • Analysis scores the finished article and only reports. Nothing rewrites the whole article after the review.

The job keeps running if you leave. A failed run shows its error rather than an empty article.

The article viewer

Opening an article shows a wide viewer. The header carries the title, status, slug and schema type, and a stats bar shows Score: N/100, word count, reading time and the credits this article cost. Below are tabs. Each one appears only when it has something to show: Content, Optimize, Brief, Gaps/Gains, Research, Inter-Linking and Export.

Content — the article, with a sidebar

One action row sits across the top: Update article, Compare with what ranks now and History, with a Due for refresh badge (and its reason) when the article needs attention, and “Last reviewed …” beside it.

  • Search appearance — the Meta title (/60), Meta description (/155) and URL. Over-long fields say “will be truncated”; an empty one carries a Missing badge.
  • The article body — View Markdown / View Rendered, Copy Markdown, and Edit, which you finish with Save and re-score.

The sidebar on the right holds, top to bottom:

Optimize — scores and fixes

The SEO and GEO score gauges, and the Fixes you can apply here card. Each fix now has a Preview fix button: nothing changes yet, and the proposed rewrite appears under Article Suggestions on the Content tab for you to add or dismiss. Re-analyse re-scores the current text and Revert last puts the previous draft back. If the gap is about authorship or first-hand experience, a notice says it is “Not a writing problem” and points you to the Brand Profile, where you set it once for every article.

Brief, Gaps/Gains, Research, Inter-Linking

  • Brief — target length, headings, outline, key terms and the FAQ. You can add a question to the FAQ; leave the answer blank and it's written for you (about 2–3 credits).
  • Gaps/Gains — topics competitors cover that you don't, and the ones you already do.
  • Research — the search results behind the article: SERP features, key terms, competition, questions and statistics.
  • Inter-Linking — internal-link suggestions to pages in your connected site's index, with Insert and Copy MD link.

Keeping an article current

  • Update article — checks prices, sizes and dates against the pages the article links to. It is free when nothing is out of date. Anything stale comes back as proposed updates in Article Suggestions, never as a silent rewrite.
  • Compare with what ranks now — compares the article with the pages ranking today and drafts changes as suggestions. It can also propose a new title and meta description, with an Apply button. After applying, publish again to send them to your site.
  • History — Article History keeps the text before each change, newest first (the last 30). Each entry is labelled (Edited, Fix applied, Refreshed, Updated, Restored, Regenerated). View → Restore this version brings one back, and the text you replaced stays in the history.
  • Why an article is due — the Articles list, the article itself and the weekly nudge give a reason: it slipped from position X to Y, dropped out of the results, clicks fell, has no Search Console rows 28 days after publishing, or is simply past its refresh interval. Mark reviewed clears it.

The Topic plan

What it is — a card on the website's Articles section: one page per keyword cluster, under its pillar. How to fill it — open a keyword research run and press Save as plan on its Keyword clusters. Each page moves through Idea → Briefed → Writing → Written → Published. You set Mark briefed (or Back to idea) yourself; the later steps follow on their own when an article for the keyword exists, is finished, and goes live. Write starts the article with the keyword filled in.

Brand Profile — the website's voice

What it is — the website's Brand Profile section (/websites/:id?section=brand) holds the voice the writer uses. By default every website shares the workspace default voice. Click Give this website its own voice to give one site its own, or Use the workspace default instead to go back. A brief that names its own author always wins.

  • Voice — Tone, Personality, Preferred terminology, Words to avoid, Writing style, and Learned from your edits (lessons you approved, each removable).
  • Who is writing — Author name, title and page URL, Publisher, Reviewed by, AI disclosure (Not stated / Human written / AI assisted / AI generated) and Author bio.
  • First-hand experience — Proprietary data, Owned examples, Methodology, Credentials.

Changes apply to new articles from the next run. Only a workspace admin can change the brand profile.

Publishing to WordPress

Connect once: Profile → Keys → Publishing → the WordPress card. Create an application password in WordPress (Users → Profile → Application Passwords; your normal password is never used), enter Site address, Username and Application password, and click Verify and connect. The WordPress user must be an Author, Editor or Administrator. A switch lets a refresh's better title or meta description be applied and republished on its own for live articles; every other change still waits for you.

Publish: in the article viewer's Export tab, click WordPress draft or Publish.

Publish cannot unpublish. Once an article is live, WordPress draft is refused, because saving as a draft would take the page down. Use Publish to update it. An article with an empty picture slot is refused too. Connecting a site needs an owner or admin; publishing needs an owner, admin or marketing member.

Export

The Export tab's Publish anywhere card packages the article as one JSON document: title, meta, URL suggestion, the content as Markdown and HTML (with real tables, lists and callouts, built from the article as it reads now), outline, FAQ, images, links, JSON-LD schema and cost. Use Copy JSON or Download .json, or Copy Markdown on the Content tab.

What it costs

  • Each article stage bills what it actually used. The total is shown on the article's stats bar.
  • Suggestions, lessons, Compare with what ranks now, generating a picture and writing an FAQ answer each use credits, and the result says how many. Adding or dismissing a suggestion is free, and so is Update article when nothing is out of date.

Worked example: run keyword research, then Save as plan on its clusters → on Articles → Topic plan, click Write on a page → add a note and the pages you want linked → when it's done, fill any picture slots and review the suggestions → Export → Publish to your WordPress site → enter its URL under Watch this page so you can see how it does in search.
