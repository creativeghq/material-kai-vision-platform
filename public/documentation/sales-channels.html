Sales Channels — Shopify, WooCommerce, Skroutz & product feeds

/commerce Pro add-on · Shopify / WooCommerce / Skroutz Marketplace

Sales Channels connects the marketplaces and webshops you sell through to Finance. An order placed on Shopify, WooCommerce or Skroutz lands here as an ordinary sales order — with the cost of each line and an income category already on it — so it counts in every profit report like any other sale. The same page publishes product feeds (Google / Shopify / WooCommerce, Skroutz and BestPrice.gr dialects) so those marketplaces can list your catalogue, runs the Skroutz order queue, and records the payouts a channel sends you.

Where: enable a channel module — Shopify, WooCommerce or Skroutz Marketplace — from the App Launcher / Modules. Each one adds Sales Channels to your workspace navigation (/commerce); the channel's own entry opens the page filtered to it (/commerce?platform=shopify and so on). The shared engine behind them, Commerce Core, is not sold on its own — any channel module grants it.

What's on the screen

The header reads "Marketplaces and webshops that send orders into Finance." with a Connect a channel button. A tab strip filters everything below by platform: All · Skroutz · Shopify · WooCommerce · Generic. Top to bottom:

  • Channel comparison — the last 30 days per channel: orders, revenue, cost, fees, margin and how many orders have an issued document. Appears once a channel has delivered orders.
  • One card per connection — on/off switch, webhook endpoint, what happens when an order arrives, credentials and Settings.
  • Product feeds — the XML links marketplaces poll to list your products.
  • Skroutz orders — the accept/reject queue, shown for each enabled Skroutz connection.
  • Payouts — the lump-sum transfers a channel pays you, matched to the orders they cover.
  • Sync activity — every inbound order and what was done with it. "This is the first thing to read when orders stop."

Connect a channel

What it is — a connection stores the store's address, its API credentials and a webhook signing secret. Why it matters — the credentials let the platform read orders and write documents back; the secret proves that an order really came from your store. Credentials are stored server-side and never read back into the screen — afterwards you only see Credentials stored / No credentials.

  1. Click Connect a channel. Pick the Platform, give it a Name (e.g. "Our Shopify store") and the Store URL.
  2. Paste the credentials the platform asks for:
    • Shopify — an Admin API access token with read_orders and write_orders.
    • WooCommerce — a Consumer key and Consumer secret.
    • Skroutz — the Smart Cart API token (Skroutz Merchants → Services → Skroutz Marketplace; generating a new token expires the previous one).
  3. A Webhook secret is generated for you (Regenerate for another). Keep it — you paste it into the store.
  4. Click Save connection. The channel is saved switched off; turn it on with its switch when you're ready.
  5. Copy the connection's Webhook endpoint (Copy) into the store, following the hint under it:
    • Shopify → Settings → Notifications → Webhooks — subscribe orders/create, orders/updated, orders/paid and orders/cancelled.
    • WooCommerce → Settings → Advanced → Webhooks — topics order.created and order.updated, the endpoint as Delivery URL, the secret as Secret.
    • Skroutz Merchants → Smart Cart webhook settings.

Don't hand-edit the secret with & ' " < >. WooCommerce decodes those characters before signing, so a secret containing one fails every delivery's signature check with nothing to read. The generated secret avoids them, and the form refuses one that contains them.

Later, Replace credentials (or Add credentials) on the card swaps the token and, optionally, the webhook secret — leave the secret blank to keep the current one. The trash icon removes the connection. If the last call to the store failed, its error is shown at the top of the card.

What happens when an order arrives

Four switches per connection decide how far the platform goes on its own:

SwitchWhat it does
Create or match the customer in CRMFinds the buyer in CRM or creates them.
Issue the fiscal document automaticallyIssues the receipt or invoice the buyer asked for and transmits it to ΑΑΔΕ.
Send the document back to the channelWhen a document is issued automatically, writes a link to it (and its number) onto the order in the store — Shopify order metafields, WooCommerce order meta plus a customer-visible note. The link is stable and always opens a freshly signed copy; a draft is never sent. (For Skroutz, send the PDF from the Skroutz queue — see below.)
Reserve stockReserves the ordered quantities in the warehouse chosen in Settings.

Leave automatic issuing off at first. A fiscal document is numbered sequentially and transmitted to ΑΑΔΕ — issuing can't be undone, only credited. Let the first orders land, check that they read correctly, then switch it on.

How an order is read — every delivery's signature is verified before anything is processed, and a repeated delivery of the same order is recognised and skipped (skipped dupe), so a store's retries never create a second order. Prices are read as VAT-inclusive or VAT-exclusive from the order itself, shipping becomes its own taxed line, and the platform re-adds the lines and compares the total and the VAT against what the store says. If either is off by more than a cent, the order lands for review instead of being issued.

Matching lines to your products — each line is matched to a catalogue product by the store's product id, then by SKU or barcode — but only when exactly one product matches. A line that matches nothing (or several) is left unmatched: it has no cost, and the sync log says how much of the order is unmatched, because that is exactly how much its margin is overstated.

Which document — the platform issues what the buyer asked for. An order flagged at intake, or one where it can't tell whether the buyer wanted a receipt or an invoice, is held rather than defaulting to a receipt (a business buyer can't deduct one). An order that arrived already paid is issued, not left as a draft. Skroutz states the requested document on the order itself.

Connection settings

Click Settings on a connection card — "Where an order from this channel lands, and what the buyer asked for."

  • Warehouse orders draw from — the warehouse stock is reserved in.
  • Income category — defaults to eCommerce; change it only if you keep your own chart of accounts. Commission from payouts goes to eCommerce Fees.
  • Receipt code / Invoice code — the document types issued for this channel.
  • Where this shop keeps the ΑΦΜ (Shopify / WooCommerce only) — neither platform has a VAT-number field, so it lives under whatever key the shop's invoicing plugin uses. Scan recent orders lists the custom fields that actually appear on recent orders, with how often; type the right one into VAT-number field and Invoice-requested field. Until these are set, every order from the shop lands for review.
  • Pull the last 30 days — fetches recent orders straight from the store and ingests any the webhook never delivered. The result names how many were new, already held, need review or failed.

Product feeds

What it is — a feed is an XML link a marketplace polls to list your catalogue. Why it matters — it's how your products appear on Skroutz, BestPrice.gr, Google Merchant Center or another shop without retyping them. Anyone holding the link can read your published products, so Rotate it to revoke access — the old link stops working immediately.

DialectUse it for
Google / Shopify / WooCommerceRSS 2.0 with the g: namespace — what Google Merchant Center and both webshop importers read.
SkroutzSkroutz's own <mywebstore> format. Skroutz silently drops a product missing any mandatory attribute.
BestPrice.grA <store> document with BestPrice's own tag names. BestPrice sends the buyer to your shop rather than taking the order, so it needs the feed and nothing else — there's no channel to connect.
GenericThe same RSS document, for any other importer that accepts it.
  1. Click New feed, name it (e.g. "Skroutz listing"), pick the Dialect and Create.
  2. Copy the link into the marketplace's merchant settings and switch the feed live.
  3. Click Choose what it lists: Everything published, Chosen categories or Chosen products, plus two switches — Published prices only and List products that are out of stock.
  4. Watch the line under the link: Last read … · N reads · N products, or Never read — if the importer is configured and this stays empty, it isn't reaching you.

Prices are sent VAT-inclusive, using each product's own VAT category. Stock comes from the warehouse: a counted zero is withheld unless you list out-of-stock products, and a product with no warehouse record has unknown stock, so it's listed as on order rather than claimed as in stock.

Three reasons a feed comes out short or empty — each one is named on the page:

  • Your storefront is closed. Every product link in every feed points at your public store, so with it closed the feed refuses to build. Open it at Finance → Settings → Online Store.
  • Nothing is published. "N priced products, none published" — publish them in Online Store, or turn off Published prices only for the feed.
  • Products the marketplace will drop. After each build the feed says e.g. "42 of 310 will be dropped on arrival for a missing attribute — mpn (38), ean (22)". The feed still returns normally, so without this the listing would simply be short. Skroutz and BestPrice require different attributes (BestPrice needs neither EAN nor description).

From a supplier import to a marketplace listing

Products loaded from a supplier XML now arrive with everything a feed needs: a price row (a re-import updates it in place rather than duplicating it), their category, their image, and mappable MPN, barcode and weight fields. They still need publishing before a feed with Published prices only lists them — the page tells you when products are priced but none are published.

Skroutz orders

What it is — unlike a webshop, Skroutz starts a clock: each order has to be accepted or rejected, and since 30/03/2026 dispatch needs an explicit Set as ready. Why it matters — an order left open expires, and an expired order is a lost sale nobody tells you about. So Skroutz is a queue with a count of what's waiting on you, not just a log.

  1. Click Fetch orders to pull what's open (until a Skroutz webhook is registered, this is how you see them).
  2. For an open order, click Accept or Reject.
  3. For an accepted order, click Set as ready when it's packed.
  4. Once its document is issued, Send <number> uploads the rendered PDF to the order on Skroutz (7 MB limit).

The table shows each order's State (open, accepted, dispatched, delivered, expired, returned…), what the buyer Asked for (receipt or invoice), and when it was seen; the order number links to the sales order in Finance.

Payouts

What it is — a channel doesn't pay per order: it pays in one lump, net of its commission, covering many orders. Why it matters — matched naively, the orders never show as paid and the commission never appears, so margin is overstated by exactly the commission. Recording the payout here settles the covered orders in full and books the commission as a cost (eCommerce Fees), not as a discount off revenue.

  1. Click Record a payout: pick the Channel, enter Their reference, Paid on, the Period from / to, Gross collected, Commission and fees and Net received (defaults to gross − fees).
  2. Save and match attaches the channel's orders from that period automatically — skipping any already on another payout.
  3. Click the payout to see the covered orders (each with its document number, or not issued). Remove any that don't belong, or Match orders again after widening the period.
  4. Click Settle. The orders are marked paid and the commission is booked. If gross − fees doesn't equal the net, the payout is marked discrepancy with a note instead of absorbing the difference.

A reconciled payout can't have orders removed from it — the payments already exist.

Channel comparison

Margin per channel is revenue less the cost on the lines and the commission on the payouts — so it's only a fact when the lines carry a cost. A channel whose lines have no cost shows margin as unknown (with the reason), never the revenue figure dressed as margin; a channel where only some lines matched shows partial and names how much is unmatched.

Sync activity

Every inbound order with its Outcome: created, updated, skipped dupe (a repeated delivery), needs review or error, plus the detail of why. The order number links to the sales order. With more than a few entries you can search and filter by channel and outcome.

What it affects elsewhere

  • Orders — channel orders are ordinary sales orders. On a channel order with an issued document, the invoice row offers Send to channel, so a document you issued by hand can still be pushed back to a Shopify or WooCommerce store. The order's Shipping tab records the carrier and tracking number (see Dispatch).
  • Document numbering — under Finance → Settings → Documents, a series can be set to Applies to one channel (Skroutz, Shopify + WooCommerce, Shopify only, WooCommerce only, Own online store, Register (POS)), so a marketplace can be numbered apart from your shop. A Shopify or WooCommerce order uses an exact-channel range first, then a Shopify + WooCommerce range, then All sales; Skroutz is never grouped with the webshops.
  • Profit & reports — because each line carries its cost and the eCommerce category, channel sales flow into the P&L and every profit report automatically.
  • Your Online Store — feeds link to it, so it must be open.

Worked example — first week on Skroutz. Enable Skroutz Marketplace → Connect a channel with your Smart Cart token, leave Issue the fiscal document automatically off → open your store in Online Store and publish your products → New feed in the Skroutz dialect, fix what the "will be dropped" line names, and give Skroutz the link → when orders come in, Fetch orders, Accept, issue the document from the order, Send it, Set as ready → when Skroutz pays, Record a payout and Settle. Once the first orders read correctly, switch automatic issuing on.

Not the same as… the surplus/last-stock Marketplace on Discover (selling to other trade members on this platform), or the Greek Marketplaces add-on of Price Monitoring (reading Skroutz/BestPrice prices to watch the market).
