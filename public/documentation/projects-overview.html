Projects · Overview, costs, visits & team

/projects/:id

A project's Overview is the page you land on when you open it. It answers the questions you would otherwise go and look up one by one: what is this job, how far along is it, what has it cost against what we agreed, when are we next meeting the client, and who is working on it.

Where: open any project from Projects — the Overview stage is the first one and opens by default.

The layout

  • The hero. The project's cover picture runs across the top with its status and category, the name, the description (long ones fold, with Show more) and the key facts — Client, Deadline, Budget and Tasks — laid over the image. Change cover picks a different picture.
  • The main column holds the working panels: Cost & margin, Rooms (with budget per room when rooms carry one), and Visits & meetings.
  • The sidebar holds Details (client, category and linked property — change the category or attach the project to a property here), Tasks (a progress bar, Todo / Active / Blocked / Done counts, a Milestones strip showing “N/M reached” and the next one, and what is Next due) and the Team.

The page header carries All projects, Invite client (managers) and a More (⋯) menu with Save as template, Archive (managers) and Delete (the owner only).

Cost & margin

What it is — what the job has actually cost so far, set against what you agreed to be paid for it. “Supplier bills, labour, expenses and open purchase orders tagged to this project, net of VAT.” Why it matters — you find out a job is losing money while there is still time to do something, not when you close it. It reads the same figures as the project's Finance section, so the two never disagree; Job cost detail takes you there.

  • Four figures — Contract value (accepted quotes), Spent so far (with the share of the contract value, or of the budget when there is no contract), On order (purchase orders not billed yet) and Forecast margin (shown red when negative; “Needs an accepted quote” when there is no contract yet).
  • A spend bar split into Materials & suppliers, Labour and Expenses, with what is on order, not billed yet hatched on the end — and a line saying how much is left of the contract or budget, or how far over it you are.
  • Materials by category. The materials figure is broken down by the product category of what was bought (tiles, sanitary, lighting…). A supplier bill is spread over the lines of its purchase order; what no product line explains shows as “Bills not linked to a purchase order line” or “Lines with no product category”, so the rows always add up to the materials total.
  • Material deliveries — open purchase orders with supplier, amount and expected arrival date, tagged Late once the date has passed or No ETA when the supplier gave none.
  • Crew on the job, last 7 days — who logged time on the project each day. Hours come from Log time on a task.

Unknown is never shown as zero. If the figures cannot be loaded the panel says “They are unknown, not zero.” A budget in a different currency from the costs is not compared, and the panel says why; expense claims still awaiting approval are listed separately and not counted yet.

Who sees it: the owner and project managers who also have finance access — the same people who see the Finance section.

Visits & meetings

What it is — the showroom visits, site surveys, design presentations and calls for this project, in one list with Upcoming and Past. Why it matters — a client asking “when are we choosing tiles?” has an answer on the project itself, and the client can ask for a slot without phoning.

A visit has a purpose: Showroom visit, Material selection, Design presentation, Site survey & measurement, Delivery / installation or Call.

The team books a visit

  1. On the Overview, press Book a visit.
  2. Choose What for, the Date and Time — “You're free at” suggests times from your published hours (Profile → Schedule); any time can still be booked.
  3. Add Where (showroom, site address or a call link) and Notes, and tick Remind me by email an hour before if you want it.
  4. Save. A calendar invite goes to the client.

The client requests a visit

A client you invited sees Request a visit on the project instead. They pick what it is for and a day, choose one of the free times — the project owner's published hours, minus anything already booked — and can add a message. Times are shown in the client's own time zone, with the team's time beneath. The request appears as Requested in your list, with Confirm and Decline — the client is told “They confirm it and you get a notification.”

Set your time zone. Free slots are worked out from the owner's availability in their time zone. Availability settings now refuse to save hours without a time zone, so a client in another country never books against the wrong clock.

Booked visits also appear on the project's task Calendar.

Project team

What it is — the people from your workspace who work on this project. You choose them one by one — it is not the whole workspace — and give each a role. Why it matters — before, only the owner could open a project; a colleague given work on it could not even read it. Clients are separate: they are invited with Invite client and see a read-only slice (see the stages).

RoleWhat they can do
OwnerEverything. Only the owner can hand the project over or delete it.
Manager“Works on everything, sees the money, edits the project and manages its team” — including Billing, Finance (with finance access), archiving, inviting clients and adding or removing teammates.
Member“Works on tasks, rooms, documents, site and requests.” The working sections of the project, without the money.

How to add someone

  1. On the Overview's Team panel, choose a person in Add a teammate… (anyone in your workspace team; new people are invited from Profile → Team first — see Team).
  2. Pick their Role — Manager or Member — and press Add.
  3. They get a notification and the project appears in their Projects list, with a Project manager or Team member badge in the header.
  • Each person shows how many open tasks they hold on the project — a quick read of who is loaded.
  • Assigned work, not on the team lists anyone holding open tasks on the project who is not on its team (an HR employee, or a colleague given one task), so nothing is quietly sitting with someone outside it.
  • A teammate can Leave this project themselves. Someone suspended or removed from the workspace loses access straight away and shows as No longer in the workspace.

The “added to a project” and “someone commented on a task” notifications are platform defaults. A workspace owner can switch either off, or keep only the bell, in Automations → Platform defaults.
