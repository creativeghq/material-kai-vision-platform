What the embed looks like on someone else's website

Everything in the card on the left is the widget: two lines of HTML on a page that knows nothing about the platform. Pick a specification, see it, and price it — and if the catalogue cannot satisfy it, you get a quote instead of a price. The model, the imagery, the prices and the compatibility rules all come from the live API.

The stages, in one widget

  1. Pick anything that matters. Values marked ● are ones a published product actually carries; the rest is the shared taxonomy. Offering only stocked values would make this a product picker with extra steps.
  2. See it. The frame at the top is the same frame throughout. A match puts the real product in it — glTF model, finishes, AR. No match draws your specification instead, labelled as an AI impression, because there is no photograph of a thing that does not exist yet.
  3. Price it. A match gives you its price and the product itself. Nothing matching gives you a quote request — and that is the normal ending, not the error case: a configurator bound to one product can only ever sell what someone already modelled and priced.
  4. Near matches carry no price, deliberately. A near match is a suggestion, not an offer.
  5. The quote request creates a CRM contact and a quote_requests row carrying your exact spec, and lands in Admin → Quote Requests. No total: nobody has priced it, and a number would anchor the negotiation on a figure that came from nowhere.
