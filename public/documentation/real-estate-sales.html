Real Estate · Viewings, offers & sales

/properties Estate Agent

This is the deal-making end of the agency workflow: you show a property, you collect the offers made on it, and you complete the sale. Each step has its own home in the Real Estate workspace — a viewing scheduler that puts appointments on your calendar, a per-property offer ledger that keeps every bid in one place, and a sale completion that turns your agency commission into a real invoice in Finance. Alongside them sits the instant valuation tool, which gives a seller an automated price estimate on the spot and captures them as a lead while you're at it.

Where: open Real Estate at /properties and use the Viewings and Sales tabs; offers are taken on each property record. Everything here is gated to the Estate Agent role. The pieces reach into Finance (commission invoice), Contracts (e-sign) and Leads (seller leads).

Viewings — schedule a showing, and see it on your calendar

A viewing is an appointment to show a property to a prospective buyer. From the workbench you schedule one against a property for a specific buyer, and you set — and later update — its status, so the appointment always reflects where things actually stand (arranged, done, cancelled, and so on). The status control is yours to drive; the viewing record follows it.

The key connection is that scheduled viewings sync into the platform calendar. You don't keep a separate diary for showings — the moment a viewing is booked it lands on your agenda alongside every other appointment on the platform, so a property showing sits next to your meetings and reminders in one timeline. See the calendar reference under Profile → Appointments for how the shared agenda works.

  • Per-buyer, per-property. A viewing ties a buyer to the property they're coming to see, so the appointment carries the context of who and what.
  • Status you control. The scheduler exposes a status you move through the viewing's life, keeping the record honest as plans firm up or fall through.
  • On your agenda automatically. Booking a viewing writes it to the platform calendar — no double entry.

Offers — a ledger on every property

Every property carries an offer ledger: the running list of bids received on it. Each entry is a PropertyOffer — you record the amount, from whom it came, and its status. Because the ledger lives on the property, you can see the full negotiation history for that home in one place rather than chasing offers across emails and notes.

When you accept an offer, the module runs an accept-cascade: accepting one offer updates the others and the property's state accordingly, so the ledger settles into a consistent picture — the winning bid is marked accepted and the competing offers are reconciled around it, rather than left dangling as if all were still live.

Why the ledger matters: multiple buyers can bid on the same property at once. Recording each as its own PropertyOffer, with a clear amount, source and status, means the negotiation is auditable — and the accept-cascade means the single action of accepting a bid does the tidy-up on all the rest, so you never have two "accepted" offers on one property.

Sales — completion, and the commission invoice

When a deal goes through, a PropertySale records the completed transaction on the property. Completion isn't just a status flip: completing a sale raises the agency commission as an invoice in Finance. That means your commission stops being a number in your head and becomes a proper Finance invoice — issued, tracked and reconciled like any other income document, so agency earnings flow straight into your books.

The Overview dashboard rolls the sales picture up into three headline totals:

Overview totalWhat it shows
Gross salesThe total transaction value of completed sales — the headline volume you've moved.
InvoicedWhat's been raised as invoices off the back of those sales — the billed side of the picture.
CommissionsThe agency commission earned, i.e. the income the completion cascade posts into Finance.

Signing the paperwork — Contracts

The legal documents that go with a transaction are handled by the Contracts module, not re-invented here. Transaction documents are sent for public e-signature with a full audit trail, so an offer acceptance or a sale agreement is signed and evidenced through the same signing pipeline the rest of the platform uses.

Instant valuation (AVM) — a price on the spot

A seller doesn't have to wait for a manual appraisal to get a number. The instant automated valuation (an AVM — automated valuation model) gives an estimate immediately from a handful of property details. It's driven by the ValuationWidget, into which you enter the property's address, property type, town, and area/size; it returns a ValuationResult.

ValuationResult fieldWhat it returns
EstimateThe headline valuation figure for the property.
Low–high rangeThe confidence band around the estimate — the plausible spread rather than a single false-precision number.
ComparablesThe number of comparable properties the estimate was built from — your sense of how well-supported it is.
Median price per m²The median per-square-metre price underpinning the estimate, useful for sanity-checking against local expectations.

The valuation does double duty. Requesting one (with the seller's GDPR consent) also captures a seller lead — the person asking "what's my property worth?" is exactly the person you want in your pipeline, so the request feeds straight into Leads as a seller opportunity.

Credits: the instant valuation is a credit-metered AI/analytics step — running an AVM draws on your workspace credits, the same way listing-copy generation and photo tagging do. See Credits for how metering works.

End to end: a seller runs an instant valuation (and becomes a lead) → you list the property → you schedule viewings that show up on your calendar → buyers make offers into the property's ledger → you accept one (accept-cascade tidies the rest) → documents are e-signed via Contracts → you complete the sale, which raises the commission invoice in Finance and updates the Overview's Gross sales / Invoiced / Commissions totals.
