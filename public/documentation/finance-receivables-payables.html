Receivables & Payables

/finance finance.manage

This is the "who owes what" side of Finance. Five tabs — Receivables, Payables, Follow-ups, Payments and Cheques — let you track money owed to you, money you owe, chase the overdue ones, and record every settlement as it happens. This guide walks each tab: what it is, why it matters, exactly how to work it day to day, and what it changes elsewhere on the platform.

How to open it: top navigation → Finance → the Receivables, Payables, Follow-ups, Payments or Cheques tab.

How the five tabs fit together: Receivables and Payables are the two ledgers (in vs. out). Follow-ups is your chase queue for quotes that haven't converted yet. Payments is where you record money actually moving — and recording one here is what reduces a Receivable's or Payable's outstanding balance. Cheques tracks post-dated/received cheques so settlement-by-cheque shows up in your balances too. Everything here rolls up into the Dashboard totals.

Receivables

What it is — the list of everything customers owe you: open invoices plus un-invoiced receivables (deposits, advances, fees). The tab label carries a live count so you can see at a glance how many items are outstanding. Each row tells you the customer, the originating document, the amount, and how overdue it is. Why it matters — this is your accounts-receivable ledger; it's the single place to see who hasn't paid and decide who to chase first.

The toolbar gives you Add receivable, New invoice, an All categories filter, and a search box ("Search customer / number…"). Above the list sit five aging buckets, each showing a € total and an invoice count: Current, 0–30 days, 31–60 days, 61–90 days and 90+ days — the further right, the more overdue.

How to chase an overdue invoice using the aging buckets

  1. Look at the buckets from the right: start with 90+ days, then 61–90, then 31–60 — oldest debt is the most at-risk, so it gets called first.
  2. Click a bucket to focus the list on just those rows.
  3. Use the search box to jump to a specific customer or document number, and the All categories filter to narrow to one income category if you only want to chase, say, a particular project's fees.
  4. Open a row to view the source document and act on it — record a payment if it's been settled, or note a follow-up.
  5. When the customer pays, record it under Payments so the row's Due drops and it falls out of the overdue bucket.

How to add a receivable

  1. For amounts that aren't on a formal invoice yet (a deposit, an advance, a fee), click Add receivable, fill in the customer, amount, category and due date.
  2. To raise a brand-new invoice from here, use New invoice on the toolbar.

To invoice an accepted quote, use Issue invoice on the quote page — that's what creates the invoice that then appears here. The Receivables tab is where it lands, not where you raise it from a quote.

How to read each row — the columns are your working data for every outstanding item:

What it affects elsewhere: the receivables total on the Dashboard is the sum of the Due column here; recording a payment against a row updates both its balance and that total.

Payables

What it is — the mirror image of Receivables for money you owe: open supplier bills plus un-invoiced payables (rent, utilities, advances). Why it matters — seeing your outgoings by age lets you plan cash, pay on time, and avoid late-payment problems with suppliers.

The toolbar gives you Add payable, Supplier credit note, New supplier bill, an All categories filter, and a search box ("Search supplier / bill #…"). The same five aging buckets appear (each with a € total and a bill count): Current, 0–30 days, 31–60 days, 61–90 days, 90+ days.

How to add a supplier bill

  1. Click New supplier bill for a formal supplier invoice, or Add payable for an un-invoiced cost like rent or utilities.
  2. Pick the supplier, the expense category, enter the amount and the due date.
  3. If a supplier has issued you a credit, use Supplier credit note instead so it offsets what you owe.
  4. When you pay it, record the outgoing under Payments — the row's Due reduces and it moves toward Current/cleared.

Suppliers must be marked is_supplier in CRM before they appear here — see CRM. If a supplier isn't showing in the picker, that flag is usually why.

What it affects elsewhere: the payables total on the Dashboard is the sum of the Due column here; supplier credit notes and recorded payments both reduce it.

Payments

What it is — the running record of money received and money paid. Why it matters — a payment recorded against a receivable or payable is what updates that document's outstanding balance and feeds the Dashboard totals — it's the link between "we agreed they owe X" and "X has actually arrived." Each payment ties back to the customer/supplier and the related document.

The toolbar has Record payment and an All categories filter, plus a search box ("Search reference / counterparty…").

How to record a payment

  1. Click Record payment.
  2. Set the Direction — in when a customer pays you, out when you pay a supplier.
  3. Choose the Method (how the money moved), the Category, and enter a Reference and the Amount.
  4. Allocate the amount to the document(s) it settles — the Allocated figure shows how much of the payment has been tied to documents, so a single payment can clear part or all of a receivable/payable.
  5. Save. The matched Receivable/Payable's Paid goes up and its Due goes down automatically.

How to read each row — the list is your settlement history; search by reference or counterparty to find a specific transaction:

ColumnDescription
DateDate the payment was made/received.
Directionin / out.
MethodPayment method.
CategoryPayment category.
ReferencePayment reference.
AmountPayment amount.
AllocatedAmount allocated to documents.

What it affects elsewhere: every recorded payment shifts the Paid/Due figures on the linked Receivable or Payable and updates the cash totals on the Dashboard.

Cheques

What it is — a tracker for post-dated and received cheques as they move through their lifecycle. Why it matters — cheques settle later than they're handed over, so tracking them here means cheque-based settlements are reflected in your balances alongside other payments instead of being forgotten until they clear.

How to log a cheque

  1. Click New on the toolbar.
  2. Set the Direction (in for a cheque you received, out for one you issued), the Cheque no., the issuing Bank, the Due date and the Amount.
  3. Track its Status through the lifecycle as it progresses (e.g. held, then deposited/cleared).

How to read each row:

ColumnDescription

Cheque no.Cheque number.
BankIssuing bank.
DueCheque due date.
AmountCheque amount.
StatusLifecycle status.

What it affects elsewhere: cheque settlements join your other payments in the balances, so post-dated money isn't invisible until the cheque date arrives.

Follow-ups

What it is — your action queue of quotes that need following up — the quotes that haven't yet converted into accepted business. Why it matters — a quote sitting idle is potential revenue going cold; this tab is how you keep cash flow healthy before anything becomes a receivable. The tab shows a count so you always know how much is outstanding.

How to work the queue

  1. Use the Open filter to show the quotes that still need attention.
  2. Sort your attention by Days idle — the longer since the last activity, the more urgent the nudge.
  3. Check Next scheduled to see when the follow-up is due, and act on the quote (call, re-send, revise).

How to read each row:

ColumnDescription
QuoteThe quote needing follow-up.
StatusCurrent quote status.
ValueQuote value.
Days idleDays since the last activity.
Next scheduledNext scheduled follow-up.

What it affects elsewhere: once a quote is accepted and you Issue invoice on the quote page, it leaves this queue and becomes a row in Receivables above.

Tip — a weekly rhythm: use the age-bucket filter on Receivables to build your collections call list (oldest first), work the Follow-ups queue by Days idle to keep new quotes warm, then record each settlement under Payments so the Dashboard and the aging buckets stay accurate.

These tabs read from the same data as the Dashboard — the receivables/payables totals you see on the overview are the sum of these lists.
