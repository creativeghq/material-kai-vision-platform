CRM · Deals & Pipeline Pro add-on

/crm?tab=pipeline /crm/deals/:id crm.view

The Pipeline is where you track deals — the opportunities you're working to close — as they move through the stages of a sales process. Each deal is linked to a contact or company, carries a value and a probability, and rolls up into a weighted forecast. Crucially, stages belong to a deal type — a construction deal and a property sale run different playbooks — so a deal can only ever sit in a stage its own process defines. This guide covers the board, deal types & stages, the deal record, the team, the forecast, and the automation that fires as a deal advances.

Where: CRM → the Pipeline tab (/crm?tab=pipeline). It's a Pro add-on — an owner enables Deals & Pipeline from the tab (a member can request it). The same board also appears in Real Estate, pinned to the property deal type.

The board

The board lays a deal type's stages out as columns, each column headed with its deal count and total value. A deal is a card showing its name, the linked contact/company and value, its expected close date, and a small checklist.

  • Move a deal by picking a stage — each card has a stage dropdown (it's deliberately not drag-and-drop). Choosing a later stage advances the deal; the move is recorded on its timeline.
  • Mark won / lost — the card's ⋮ menu moves a deal to the type's winning stage, or opens a short reason dialog for a loss. Lost deals collapse into their own section with a Reopen action.
  • Checklist — a per-deal to-do list (done / total) you tick off from the card.
  • Header stats — the open-deal count, a toggle to reveal lost deals, and the weighted forecast per currency.
  • Deal type switcher — flip the board between your pipelines (hidden in Real Estate, which is locked to the property type).

Deal types & stages

What it is — a deal type is a pipeline with its own stages. Why it matters — different sales run differently, and the platform enforces that: a deal belongs to one type, and can only sit in a stage that type defines — a Construction deal physically cannot land in a property "Conveyancing" stage. Which stage counts as won or lost is a property of the stage (a flag you set), never a magic status word.

Platform defaults come ready to use — Real Estate, Project, Construction and General — and are read-only. A workspace admin can add their own from the board's Types button:

  • Create a type — give it a name and a subject (a property, a project, or none); it's created with a starter set of stages in one step.
  • Manage stages — add, reorder, rename, delete (blocked while a stage still holds deals), and mark which stages are winning or losing.
  • Per-stage email — the ✉ on a stage sets up an automatic email to the contact when a deal reaches it (see Automation below).

The deal record

Clicking a deal opens its record page at /crm/deals/:id:

  • Stage card — move the deal (the same dropdown), see its position (“3 of 5”), and reopen it if closed.
  • Activity timeline — the deal's history and where you log notes and activity (the same activity feed as a contact/company record).
  • Details — contact, company, property, project (each a link), value, probability and expected close.
  • Deal team and Notes cards (below).

Deal team

Assign several workspace members to a deal, each with a functional role — Account Executive, BDR, Account Manager, Solutions, Exec Sponsor or Contributor. This is “who to chase,” not an access grant: adding someone to a deal team doesn't change what they can see or do — that's still governed by their workspace role. Add from a member search, change a role inline, or remove.

The forecast

The board header shows a weighted forecast: each open deal's value multiplied by its probability (or, where no probability is set, by how far along its stage is), summed up. It's grouped by currency — EUR and GBP are never added into one made-up number — and it's derived in the database, so what you see is a single consistent figure across the board and the assistant. The line reads, per currency, “€X weighted of €Y · avg Z%.”

The timeline — pipeline history

Every stage and status change is written to the deal's timeline automatically — “Moved to Tender from Estimate,” “Deal won at Handover,” “Deal lost — price.” Because it's recorded at the database level, the history is the same whoever made the move: you on the board, you on the record page, or the assistant from chat.

Automation — when a deal changes stage

A stage move raises a Flow event — Deal Stage Changed, Deal Won or Deal Lost — carrying the deal, its stage, value, and the contact/company. You route these through Flows like any other trigger (notify the owner, post to a channel, start an onboarding). Each comes with a seeded default you can retarget or pause without a deploy.

For the common case, there's a shortcut: the ✉ “email the contact when a deal reaches this stage” on a stage in the Types manager. You write the subject and body (with {{contact_name}}, {{deal_title}}, {{stage}}, {{value}} variables) and it's saved as an ordinary workspace flow — so it shows up under Flows with run history and can be edited or paused there. A contact with no email is simply skipped, not a failed send.

From the assistant

The assistant can run your pipeline from chat with the Deal pipeline tool (Agent tools): list your open deals, give the weighted forecast, create a deal for a contact, move one to a stage by name, or mark one lost with a reason. It works under your own permissions, so it sees exactly the deals you would. (Property and project deals are created on the board, since they need a listing or project attached.)

Gating & access

  • Pro add-on. Deals & Pipeline is a paid add-on that depends only on CRM (you don't need Real Estate). Enabling it from either CRM or Real Estate lights it up in both.
  • Roles. Everyone with CRM access can see the board; sales roles and above can create and manage deals; a client role is read-only; managing types and stages is owner/admin only.
  • No per-deal credits. Deals don't cost credits; only the stage-email automation draws on the flow run cost like any automation.
