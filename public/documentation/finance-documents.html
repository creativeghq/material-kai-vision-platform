Financial Documents

/finance /finance/invoices/:id finance.manage

Financial Documents is where you create everything the money side of your business runs on — sales invoices, receipts, credit notes, expenses and delivery notes — and keep it all organised with income and expense categories. This guide explains each document type: what it is, when you'd reach for it, how to create it step by step, and what it changes elsewhere in the platform.

How to open it: top navigation → Finance → choose the Invoices (or Receipts / Credit notes / Expenses / Delivery notes) tab. Each tab is one document type; the tabs share the same look and the same toolbar concepts, so once you know one you know them all.

Before you issue your first document, set up Finance Settings — your business identity, a numbering series per document type, your categories, and (if you transmit to the tax authority) e-invoicing. Those settings supply the issuer details, the legal sequential number, and the category pickers you'll use here.

The document types at a glance

What it is — the Finance page splits into one tab per kind of financial document. Why it matters — each type has a different legal and bookkeeping role, so knowing which tab to use is the first decision every time. Use this table as the map; the rest of the page details each tab.

Two toolbar concepts that recur everywhere

Before the per-tab detail, learn these two buttons — they appear on several tabs and behave the same wherever you see them, so the per-tab sections below won't re-explain them.

  • Sync from myDATA — pulls the documents the tax authority already has on file for your business, so issued invoices and incoming expenses can be reconciled without re-keying them. This is how documents others issued to you (and your own already-filed ones) appear automatically. It only works once you've entered your myDATA inbox credentials in Finance Settings → Documents.
  • Income category / Expense category — opens the manager for the category list used across the document lists, filters and reports. These same categories are also editable inline on a document header, so you can keep your books consistent without leaving the document you're working on. See Finance settings.

Invoices tab

What it is — the list of every sales invoice you've raised. When you'd use it — to bill a customer, to check what's still owed, or to find and re-open a past invoice. How — click New to start one (the editor is detailed further down), or click any row to open its detail page. The toolbar lets you Sync from myDATA, manage the Income category list, filter by category, and search by number or customer when the history gets long.

Toolbar: New · Sync from myDATA · Income category (manage list) · All categories filter · search by number / customer.

Receipts tab

What it is — proof-of-payment documents. When you'd use it — when money has actually changed hands, including every sale rung up at the till. How — click New to create one in the editor, or Open POS to ring up a sale that produces a receipt directly. The rest of the toolbar mirrors Invoices (Income category, category filter, search).

Toolbar: New · Open POS · Income category · All categories filter · search by number / customer.

ColumnWhat it shows

DateThe issue date.
CategoryThe income category assigned.
TotalThe gross document total.
DueThe amount still outstanding.
StatusThe document status.
mDThe myDATA transmission status (ΑΑΔΕ).

Receipts and invoices both count as income, so both carry an Income category. The difference is intent: an invoice is a demand for payment (it can sit unpaid in Receivables), a receipt records that payment was made.

Credit notes tab

What it is — a document that credits or refunds an amount against an invoice you already issued. When you'd use it — a return, a price correction, or a cancelled order where the original invoice is a legal record you can't simply delete. How — click New, then choose the Type and Reason and link the original invoice in the Invoice field so the credit offsets the right document.

Toolbar: New.

ColumnWhat it shows
NumberThe credit note number.
DateThe issue date.
TypeThe credit note type.
ReasonThe reason for the credit.
AmountThe credited amount.
mDThe myDATA transmission status (ΑΑΔΕ).
InvoiceThe invoice this credit note offsets.

A credit note doesn't erase the original invoice — it counter-balances it. Both documents stay on file, which is exactly what the tax authority expects.

Expenses tab

What it is — your business costs, captured and categorised. When you'd use it — every time you pay (or owe) a supplier — materials, rent, subscriptions, services. How — use Sync from myDATA to pull in expense documents suppliers already filed against your VAT number, or record them manually; assign an Expense category so the cost lands in the right report bucket. Search by supplier or VAT number to find a specific cost.

Toolbar: Sync from myDATA · Expense category (manage list) · All categories filter · search by supplier / VAT.

What it affects elsewhere: expenses are your business costs, categorised; they feed Payables (what you owe) and the finance Reports. Categorising them well is what makes those reports meaningful.

Delivery notes tab

What it is — a record of goods dispatched. When you'd use it — when stock leaves your premises, especially when the goods move before (or separately from) the invoice. How — click New, pick the delivery note Type, and record the dispatch. Delivery notes are often tied into the dispatch/warehouse flow.

Toolbar: New.

ColumnWhat it shows

The invoice detail page

What it is — the full view of a single invoice, reached by clicking any row in the Invoices tab. Why it matters — the list shows you the summary; the detail page is where you act on the document. Creating & editing documents

The big picture — creating any document follows the same four-beat rhythm regardless of type. Here it is in brief, then the full editor field-by-field below.

  1. Open the relevant tab (e.g. Invoices) and start a new document.
  2. Pick the customer/supplier and add line items (products/services with quantity, price and VAT).
  3. Assign an income/expense category — categories are editable and can be assigned inline on the document header.
  4. Save; issue/transmit when ready. Issued documents flow into the relevant balances and reports.

Document & delivery options

What these do — these are the toggles that decide what happens the moment you create the document: whether it transmits, whether it moves stock, whether it's emailed, and — most importantly — whether it's issued as a legal record or saved as a draft.

  • Submit to myDATA on issue — transmit electronically when issued.
  • Print online code / QR.
  • Include in MYF report.
  • Move stock on issue — decrement warehouse.
  • Print terms & comments.
  • Send by email on create / Send by SMS on create.
  • Info box (printed, ≤250 chars) — extra printed note (warranty/delivery terms).
  • Issue now — assigns the legal number + date (otherwise saved as draft).

When everything's right, click Preview to check the PDF, then Create invoice.

Draft vs. issued: "Issue now" assigns the official myDATA number — drafts can be edited freely; issued documents are legal records. Leave it off while you're still working on the document, and turn it on only when the document is final.

See also: e-invoicing and Finance settings.

Categories

What it is — the income and expense labels you tag documents with. Why it matters — categories are how your Reports break revenue and cost down; without them every figure is one undifferentiated lump. How — they can be added and managed directly from a document's header, and used as filters across the document lists and reports — so you keep your books consistent without leaving the document you're working on. (You can also pre-seed and manage the full list in Finance Settings → Categories.)

Finding documents

Why it matters — finance histories grow fast. Every document list supports text search (by supplier/customer, document number or reference) and category filtering, so a large history stays manageable. Reach for search to re-open a specific document; reach for the category filter to review everything in one bucket.

What documents affect elsewhere: invoices and receipts you issue here feed Receivables and the Dashboard automatically; expenses feed Payables and reporting. Nothing here is a dead end — every issued document moves a balance or a report.

Step by step: create an invoice

The full happy path, end to end, from opening the tab to handing the customer a way to pay:

  1. Open Finance from the top navigation, then select the Invoices tab.
  2. Start a new invoice.
  3. Pick the customer. The VAT number is validated; for Greek customers the details can be enriched automatically from ΑΑΔΕ — this also helps avoid a rejected submission later.
  4. Add line items — choose catalogue products or add custom lines — each with a quantity, price and VAT rate.
  5. Assign an income category from the document header (categories are editable and can be managed inline) so the invoice lands in the right report bucket.
  6. Review the totals (net, VAT and gross) to confirm the figures.
  7. Issue the invoice. On issue it is transmitted to ΑΑΔΕ / myDATA and appears in Receivables.
  8. Generate the PDF and share a payment link with the customer.

For the transmission details and statuses (what the mD column means and how to fix a failed send), see e-invoicing.

Step by step: record an expense

Expenses are quicker than invoices — there's no PDF to send, just a cost to capture and categorise:

  1. Open Finance from the top navigation, then select the Expenses tab.
  2. Start a new expense.
  3. Pick the supplier.
  4. Enter the amount and VAT.
  5. Assign an expense category from the document header so it reports correctly.
  6. Save. The expense feeds Payables and the finance Reports.

Instead of typing expenses one by one, try Sync from myDATA on the Expenses tab to pull in documents your suppliers already filed against your VAT number — then you only need to categorise them.
