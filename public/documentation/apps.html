Apps & Modules

/apps

MaterialsHub is assembled from modules — self-contained capabilities you switch on per workspace. Rather than crowd the top navigation with everything, the optional ones live behind the App Launcher: each workspace turns on exactly the apps it needs (HR, Email Marketing, Docs, Flows, and more), and only those appear. This page explains the framework: where apps live, how you enable one, how paid add-ons and BYOK apps work, and how a member can request an app the owner controls.

Where: the App Launcher is in the top navigation (after Discover); the full /apps hub lists everything. You manage activation from either the launcher or Profile → Modules. Apps are enabled per workspace.

Active vs available apps

The launcher and the /apps hub split apps into two groups:

  • Everything active in this workspace — the apps already switched on; click one to open it. (If none: “No apps active in this workspace yet.”)
  • Available to add — apps you can enable. Each shows its name, an icon and a short description, plus an enable action.

An app only appears for a workspace when it's globally enabled on the platform and entitled to that workspace — that's what keeps each tenant's launcher to just its own apps.

Price tiers

Every app declares a tier, which governs how you get it:

TierWhat it means
FreeIncluded — switch it on directly.
Pro / EnterpriseA paid add-on. Each is bound to a Stripe product, so enabling it goes through checkout/billing before it unlocks for the workspace. (Examples: HR, Email Marketing.)

How to enable an app

  1. Open the App Launcher (or /apps) and find the app under Available to add.
  2. Click enable. A free app switches on immediately; a paid one takes you through checkout (it's tied to a Stripe product) and switches on once billing is set up.
  3. The app moves to active and its screens, tabs and tools appear for that workspace. You can also manage activation from Profile → Modules.

Sub-modules & providers

Some apps have sub-modules that extend a parent — and in the modules grid they're grouped under that parent so the list stays tidy. Each sub-module is enabled (and priced) on its own, but only makes sense with its parent on. Examples:

  • Real Estate → Property Management (lettings) and Investments — extra tabs inside Real Estate.
  • Payments → provider sub-modules Stripe and Viva — priced add-ons that appear under Finance. A provider handles online checkout for subscriptions, credits and invoice/quote pay-links, plus webhook reconciliation and the customer billing portal. Turn on the provider(s) you use; more processors slot in alongside.

Requesting an app (members)

Enabling a paid app is an owner action (it commits spend). If you're a member, you can request an app instead: the owner is notified (a module-access-requested event flows through Flows) and can approve it, after which you get access. This routes the “I need this capability” to the person who authorises it, cleanly.

BYOK apps — bring your own key

Some apps run on your credentials, not the platform's — “bring your own key” (BYOK). Enabling the app is only step one; the app won't actually do its outbound work until you add your own key/account. Two examples in this guide:

  • Email Marketing — BYOK-only: campaigns send exclusively from your own verified Resend account. No shared platform sender.
  • HR → Ergani — files to the Greek labour authority (ΠΣ Εργάνη) using your Ergani Web API credentials.

BYOK credentials are added per workspace under Profile → Keys & Connections (or in the app's own setup card). Each BYOK app's page below documents exactly what to add and where.
