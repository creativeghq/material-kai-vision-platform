MoodBoards · Presentation sheets

/moodboard/:id

Presentation sheets are polished, client-ready A3 pages produced from a board — a material grid, a colour scheme, a lighting plan, a full deck. They are how a board stops being an internal collection and becomes a deliverable a client understands. This guide explains what each sheet type is, whether it's interactive or passive, what it costs and the inputs it expects, how sheets are generated through the Agent Hub (with an in-chat canvas for the interactive ones), and how the finished PDFs are stored on the board and bundled into a Client View.

Where it is

Open a board at /moodboard/:id. Sheets are started from the board's Tools tab via New Tool, or simply by asking the Agent Hub for the sheet you want. The finished PDF is attached to the board so you can re-open and re-render it later.

What's on the screen

What it is — sheets are generated through the Agent Hub: you describe the sheet you want, the assistant assembles it, and the finished PDF is attached to the board. Why it matters — each sheet is stored with the moodboard and can later be bundled into a project-level Client View. How — for passive sheets the assistant assembles the content and renders to PDF immediately; for interactive sheets it first opens an in-chat canvas where you place fixture symbols, draw dimensions, or position callouts directly on the image, and only then is the PDF rendered.

Interactive vs passive: three sheet types — Lighting plan, Annotated render and Elevation / render pair — open an in-chat canvas first. You choose interactive types when the value is in where things sit on an image. The passive sheet types render immediately because their content is just a list or layout.

The sheet types

Reach for the passive sheets (Material board, Color palette, Concept board, FF&E schedule, Area breakdown, Full deck) when the content is a list, a layout or a roll-up the assistant can assemble outright — they cost less or nothing and need no manual placement. Reach for the interactive sheets (Lighting plan, Annotated render, Elevation / render pair) when the whole point of the page is precise positioning on an image.

When to use each sheet

  • Material board — when the client just needs to see the chosen products together, with a word or two of context per item. The everyday "here's the palette of materials" page.
  • Color palette — when the colour scheme is the point: a clean strip of named swatches you can sample straight from images already on the board.
  • Concept board — early in a project, when you're selling a mood or story rather than specific products: a free-form image collage with captions.
  • Lighting plan (interactive) — when fixture positions matter: drop light symbols onto a backdrop and add a legend so an electrician or client reads the intent.
  • Annotated render (interactive) — when you want a render that explicitly points at the products in it, so the viewer knows exactly what they're looking at.
  • Elevation / render pair (interactive) — for the technical side-by-side: an elevation next to a render with dimensions and tile callouts you draw on.
  • FF&E schedule — when you need the formal furniture/fixtures/equipment table, typically pulled straight from a quote so pricing and the schedule agree.
  • Area breakdown — when one page must say everything about a room: hero render, plan, elevation, materials & finishes, fittings and a palette strip together.
  • Full deck — at hand-off, to combine several finished sheets behind a cover page into one PDF you can send as the proposal.

Step by step: produce a presentation sheet

  1. From the board, choose a sheet type — for example a material board, colour palette, lighting plan, FF&E schedule or full deck — via the Tools tab's New Tool, or simply ask the assistant for it.
  2. The Agent Hub drives the sheet: describe what you want and the assistant assembles it.
  3. For an interactive sheet, complete the in-chat canvas — place fixture symbols, draw dimensions, or position callouts directly on the image.
  4. The finished sheet renders as an A3 PDF attached to the board, ready to re-open and re-render later.

Worked example — building a full deck for hand-off

Ask the Agent Hub for a Material board (0 cr) of your chosen products with a chip description each, and a Color palette (0 cr) sampling swatches from images already on the board — both render to PDF immediately and attach to the board. Then ask for a Lighting plan (interactive, 3 cr): the in-chat canvas opens, you drop fixture symbols onto the backdrop and add a legend before it renders. Finally ask for a Full deck (3 cr), select those three sheets in the order you want plus a cover page, and the assistant combines them into one A3 PDF you can send as the proposal — or bundle into a Projects → Client View.

What it affects

On interactive sheets every symbol, callout and dimension is placed relative to the backdrop image, so the canvas and the rendered PDF stay in sync whatever the image size. Every sheet outputs an A3 PDF stored with the moodboard, so you can re-open and re-render it later — and the same sheets can be bundled into a project-level deliverable via Projects → Client Views. The complete catalogue of what the assistant can build is in Tools & Capabilities.
