Real Estate · Listings & workbench

/real-estate Estate Agent

A listing is one property you're marketing — a flat for sale, a house to rent — and everything a buyer or portal needs to know about it. You build and maintain each one in the Property Workbench: a guided, multi-step editor that adapts its fields to the type of property you're describing, keeps portal-ready field sets alongside your own, and layers in the platform's AI to write the copy, tag the photos and pick a cover. When you're happy, you publish — and the same record becomes a public property page, a card in Discovery, and a line on your agency profile, all at once.

Where it is

Top navigation → Real Estate (/real-estate) → the Listings tab. The tab shows your existing properties; a new one opens the Property Workbench. Real Estate is a Pro add-on — if you don't see it, enable it from the App Launcher first. Working with listings needs the Estate Agent role.

What a listing record holds

Every property in the Listings tab is summarised by a small set of headline fields — the same ones buyers filter on in Discovery — and each carries a public slug (the address of its own public page). At a glance a listing row shows:

  • Property type — apartment, house, plot, commercial, and so on. This is the field that drives the workbench: choose it and the editor shows the right fields for that kind of property.
  • Transaction type — sale or rent. It changes how the listing reads and how buyers find it.
  • Town / area — the location, used both on the public page and as a Discovery filter.
  • Price — the asking price (or rent).
  • Bedrooms — another headline filter for buyers.
  • Photos — the property's image set, with one chosen as the cover.
  • Status — where the listing is in its lifecycle (e.g. draft vs. published).
  • Slug — the stable public identifier for the listing's own property page.

The Property Workbench

Rather than one long form, the workbench walks you through a property in steps presented as tabs, so you can fill what you know now and come back to the rest. Two things make it more than a generic form:

  • Category-specific field editors. The fields aren't fixed — they change with the property type you picked. Describing a plot of land surfaces different inputs than describing an apartment, so you only ever see fields that make sense for what you're listing. This keeps records clean and comparable within a type.
  • Portal-ready field sets. The workbench understands the Spitogatos and OpenImmo portal field vocabularies, so as you fill in a property you're also filling in the structured attributes those portals expect. That's what makes a listing portal-ready — when you later syndicate it (see Portal syndication), the data the portals need is already there rather than being re-keyed.

Why the type matters first. Set the property type early in the workbench. Because it decides which category-specific editor you get, changing it later means a different field set — so pick the right one up front and the rest of the steps line up.

Adding a property — step by step

  1. Go to Real Estate → Listings and start a new property. This opens the Property Workbench.
  2. Choose the property type and transaction type (sale or rent). The workbench now shows the category-specific field editor for that type.
  3. Work through the steps, filling the details — town/area, price, bedrooms and the type-specific attributes. As you go, you're also populating the Spitogatos / OpenImmo field sets that keep the listing portal-ready.
  4. Upload the photos. Add the property's images; let AI tagging and the auto cover pick do their work (below).
  5. Generate the listing copy with AI, or write your own description.
  6. Optionally generate a brochure PDF to share or print.
  7. Set the visibility controls, then publish when the listing is ready to go live.

The AI helpers

Three assistants live in the workbench. All of them draw on your workspace credits — they're AI features, so each run has a small credit cost.

  • AI listing copy. Generate the property's description automatically instead of writing it from scratch. It's a credit-metered generation step, so you get a polished draft you can edit rather than a blank box. (Internally this is the "P2 AI — credit-metered listing-copy generation".)
  • AI photo tagging. When you upload property photos, the platform auto-labels them — so images are described and searchable without manual captioning.
  • Auto cover pick. Alongside tagging, the system chooses the best image as the listing's cover, so the strongest photo leads on the public page and in Discovery without you hand-picking it (you stay in control of the final choice).

Cost note: listing-copy generation, photo tagging and the auto cover pick consume workspace credits like the platform's other AI features. See Credits for how consumption is metered.

Brochure PDF

From a listing you can generate a property brochure PDF — a shareable, printable document for the property. The generator returns a pdf_url to the finished file and its page_count, so you can hand a professional brochure to a viewer or attach it to an email without building one by hand.

Publishing & visibility

Publishing a listing is what makes it public. Once published, the one property record shows up in three places at once:

  • Its own public property page. A standalone page (reached via the listing's slug) with the details, photos and an inquiry form. The form requires GDPR consent before a prospect can send their details — those inquiries become your leads.
  • Discovery. The listing appears in Discovery, where buyers browse and search by property type, transaction type, town, price range and bedrooms — the same headline fields you set in the workbench.
  • Your agency profile. It's listed under your agency profile's Listings, so your public storefront stays in sync with what you're actively marketing.

You decide what goes public with the per-listing visibility controls: an "Open for all" toggle plus a public-listings switch. Together they let you keep a property internal while you work on it, then flip it out to the public page, Discovery and your agency profile when it's ready — or pull it back to internal at any time.

Public = discoverable. A published, public listing is browsable by anyone in Discovery and reachable at its public URL, and its inquiry form feeds real leads into your pipeline. If a property isn't meant to be seen yet, keep the "Open for all" / public switch off until it is.

Driving it from the assistant

You don't have to open the workbench to work with listings. The dedicated Estate agent can create and update properties for you through the manage_real_estate toolkit — ask it in chat and it edits the same records. See AI Agents.

End to end: add a property in the workbench → let the AI draft the copy, tag the photos and pick a cover → optionally generate a brochure PDF → set visibility and publish → watch inquiries land as leads, and push the portal-ready listing out via syndication.
