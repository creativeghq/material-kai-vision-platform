Quotes · Requests

/quotes?tab=customer-requests /quotes?tab=requests

Requests are the front door for inbound demand. Instead of starting from a blank quote, you start from what someone actually asked you to price. There are two inboxes: Customer Requests, from your own customers and your website, and Network Requests, from other members of a supplier network you belong to.

Where it is: top navigation → Quotes → the Customer Requests tab (workspace admins) or the Network Requests tab (members who manage a supplier network). Each opens inside the Quotes page.

Customer Requests

The Customer Requests tab has a count strip — Total, Pending, Quoted, Accepted, Rejected and Draft — and two lists:

  • Incoming Requests — specifications sent from your website that nobody has priced yet, with From, What they asked for, Source, Status and Received. A picture generated from the request is labelled as an AI impression, not a catalogue photo.
  • All Quote Requests — every quote a customer has submitted to you, with Quote Name, Status, Status Tag, Items, Created and Expires, a filter, and an action to open each one. Create Quote for User starts a quote on a customer's behalf.

Network Requests

In a supplier network, a member's quote can be submitted up to you (More → Submit to parent on their quote). Network Requests has two lists. Incoming holds the requests your network members submitted to you: expand one to see its lines, Decline a line you cannot supply, open the quote, or escalate the request to your own parent. Sent upward holds the requests you submitted; once your parent has priced them, Apply to quote copies the prices onto your quote, and Save to catalog keeps them for next time. Lines still waiting on a price read Awaiting.

How to turn a request into a quote — step by step

  1. Open Quotes and switch to Customer Requests.
  2. Review the request — what was asked for, by whom and from where.
  3. Open the quote (or create one for the customer) and continue as usual: add line items, set prices on the Pricing tab, add FF&E specifications, preview the PDF, then send with Email. See Quotes · Build & send.

What it affects

A request becomes a normal quote that follows the build-and-send flow: it produces a PDF and a public, token-protected page at /q/:token the customer can accept without logging in, and acceptance creates the sales order and a draft invoice in Finance.
